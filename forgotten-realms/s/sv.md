## Svardborg

Svardborg was a frost giant fortress built atop an iceberg in the Sea of Moving Ice. After Ordning of the giants was destroyed, it came under the control of Jarl Storvald as he sought to increase the standing of his kin by bringing about the Age of Everlasting Ice on Toril.

Geography
It was a completely isolated settlement situated on top of an ice formation that closely resembled the hundreds that surrounded it.

Features
The fortress comprised a number of old frost giant hunting lodges, arranged in a circle around a central peak of ice. The core of the iceberg was hollow and flooded with water from the surrounding sea. This created a well-guarded dock that housed the frost giants' vessels.

One of the recovered lodges served as a home for the Jarl, another as a barracks for the frost giants,  while the temple of Thrym housed a white dragon.

Defenses
While the fortress was protected by over a dozen frost giants there were no structural defenses. The giants believed its remote location precluded its discovery.

History
During the age of Ostoria, Svardborg was an isolated frost giant hunting village. When the giant empire fell, the berg on which the settlement was built floated north into the Sea of Moving Ice. The settlement became covered in ice due to the freezing temperatures of the far north.

Some time later, the mated pair of white dragons, Cryovain and Isendraug formed their lair and accompanying nest within Svardborg's temple.

After the destruction of the Ordning by Annam All-Father, Jarl Storvald sailed to Svardborg aboard the Krigvind and reclaimed the home of his ancestors. He planned to use the settlement as a base from which he could recover the Ice that Never Melts.

Just after the giants reclaimed the village, they found a clutch of eggs left by the pair. The giants blackmailed the dragons into servitude, and locked up Isendraug in Svardborg and bound Cryovain to the Krigvind.

Notable Locations
 Temple of Thrym: The giants' place of worship featured a massive ice-sculpture of the titan Thrym.

Inhabitants
In addition to its frost giants residents, Svardborg housed their giant owl and winter wolf pets, along with the northlanders, yetis and the white dragon they kept as slaves.

Notable Inhabitants
 The frost giant Jarl Storvald
 The mated white dragons Isendraug
 The Zhentarim mage Nilraun Dhaerlost

## Svartalfheim

Svartalfheim was a divine realm in Nidavellir, the third layer of Ysgard. Home to secretive but benevolent drow, it was the realm of Eilistraee under the Great Wheel cosmology and believed by some to be also ruled by Erevan Ilesere.

Description
Like all realms in Nidavellir, Svartalfheim was a system of underground caverns. Unlike the rest of the layer, however, the realm's stone walls emitted a silvery glow, while natural springs and geysers warmed up the rivers and kept the caves at a comfortable warm temperature. The entire realm emanated a feeling of peace and love that was atypical for Ysgard.

The realm had wild regions covered in dark forests that thrived on the earth's warmth and required no sunlight. Some of the caverns displayed abundant deposits of quartz, mica, and pyrite, of which the drow were overly fond and employed them abundantly as decoration and jewellery.

The materials that made up the cavern walls had excellent acoustic properties. Throughout the realm, sounds were richer and sweeter, so that even the echoing sound of distant miners could be mistaken for song. This trait greatly improved the magical abilities of bards and amplified the singing abilities of even the most untalented visitors. It was common for people to continue to sing even after leaving Svartalfheim, unaware that this enchantment faded as soon as they left.

Cosmography
The realm was at constant war with the dwarves and gnomes from the neighboring realm of Nidavellir. Skirmishes and raids were common, but casualties were rare on either side of the conflict, since the main goal of the drow was to outsmart the dwarves through pranks, theft, and insults.

Notable Locations
 Dokkar, the main merchant city of the realm. It was known for its narrow and crowded streets, as well as for its merchants who never haggled.
 Yggwyrd, a large necropolis that contained shrines to all the elven deities. All drow of Svartalfheim planned to die in Yggwyrd, and all elven visitors had the ability to speak with the dead while within the city. The few living inhabitants worked as guards to the different family crypts. Non-elves were forbidden entry, and the city was guarded by a number of undead protectors, including banshees and ghosts.
 Elkhound's Gate, a gateway to the Lower planes that was constantly guarded against invading creatures. Travelers leaving Svartalfheim through the gate normally passed unchallenged, but the guards were known to assist inexperienced adventurers.

Inhabitants
The drow inhabitants of Svartalfheim were mostly interested in living their lives and being left alone. They typically hunted on upper tunnels and spent time carving ironwood sculptures and crafting exquisite gemstones, some of them magically enhanced. The realm's inhabitants were also known for making an exquisite mushroom wine.

Bards living in Svartalfheim were known to sing about the joys of the moment, dance, and true love. They could be hired to sing praises about people, talking of lovers, appearances, or past achievements.

The realm's inhabitants were also famous for their skill in weaving and textile work. Their cloth products were known as the best in all of Ysgard, good enough to be enchanted into magic garments. The drow frequently engaged in commerce with gnomes and other respectable visitors.

Rumors & Legends
The Nidavellir dwarves believed that Svartalfheim was connected to the Demonweb Pits and to drow realms in the Underdark of the Prime Material plane. It was also believed that parts of the realm shifted between Ysgard and Pandemonium, and sometimes moved even into the Abyss.

## Svayyah

Svayyah was a water naga who lived in the Nagaflow in the mid–14 century DR. She acted on behalf of her people and made an agreement with the Cormyrean engineer Ivar Devorast when he planned to build a canal between the Lake of Steam and the Nagaflow.

Description
Svayyah was a water naga with a head that resembled a human's and a serpentine body.

Personality
She was more comfortable in water than on land. Like many of her kind, she tended to look down upon humans and other races as inferiors.

Abilities
Like most of her kind, she was adept at casting spells and could swim.

Relationships
She had some admiration for Ivar Devorast, even though he was a human. Devorast trusted her to keep her side of the agreement that they made with the nagas and this trust was well placed, as she took her commitment to her own word very seriously. She found him very puzzling and intriguing, as she normally saw humans as being motivated by gold, but Devorast seemed to want to build his canal without any purpose other than the fact that it hadn't been done before.

History
Svayyah met Ivar Devorast in the  while he was walking along the banks of the Nagaflow near the Nagaflow Keep, a fortress he had previously built for the city-state of Innarlith. When Devorast saw her head come out of the water, he immediately thought she was a human woman and asked if she needed any help or if she needed him to move his camp away from where she was bathing. Svayyah referred to him as a 'dista'sarra'. He asked what it meant and when she explained the translation, he immediately realized she was a naga. Svayyah learned from Devorast that he was the one who had built the keep, and she said that she found it beautiful and wanted to learn more about him. He agreed and decided to meet with her by the water to converse with her.

She learned from Devorast about his idea of building a canal from the Lake of Steam to the Nagaflow, which would thus connect the Sea of Fallen Stars to the oceans of Toril. The Nagaflow and the Nagawater were both bodies of water controlled by nagas and any attempt to create a shipping lane through these waters was going to need their agreement if it was going to occur peacefully. She went back to discuss the idea with her tribemates in the Nagaflow and they came to a consensus that they liked the idea, but they expected to have some profit from the enterprise themselves if the plan came to fruition. She questioned Devorast and tried to make it clear to him how difficult this enterprise was going to be. She greatly surprised Devorast with the amount of knowledge she had of the rest of Faerûn and she anticipated that a number of factions might oppose him, including Thay, the sahuagin and aboleths of the Inner Sea, the druids of Turmish, and the Zhentarim.

Later, once Devorast received a commission from the Ransar of Innarlith to go and build the canal, she and most of her tribemates kept their side of the agreement faithfully. Devorast reported that he had conversed with the nagas to the authorities in Innarlith as well, who didn't entirely trust the nagas. One of Svayyah's tribemates went behind the backs of the others to attack the canal site because he disagreed with the decision of the rest of the tribe. He appeared to show detest to Svayyah when Devorast mentioned her name to him. Devorast and others fought off the naga and then went to summon Svayyah to understand what happened. When Svayyah learned from Devorast what her tribemate had done, she summoned a council underwater among the water nagas with Ivar Devorast suspended in a magical air bubble to pass judgment. Svayyah was enraged by what her tribemate had done by violating their agreement and with the consent of the other nagas, she killed her tribemate and sent Devorast back to his canal project.

As Svayyah predicted, various factions did try to disrupt the project to build the canal. Some of these factions attempted to frame the water nagas of the Nagaflow for their own attacks on the project. Nyla, a Zhentarim agent in Innarlith, hired a banelar naga to attack the canal site. Devorast summoned Svayyah again, who personally teleported herself to the canal site to see for herself and she proceeded to defend Devorast from the banelar and killed him herself. Svayyah explained to Devorast that it was a banelar and not from their tribe and urged him to try to take more care in defending himself against those who might attack.

In another incident, Insithryllax, a black dragon who worked as an ally of the Red Wizard Marek Rymüt killed one of the water nagas by diving from a high altitude onto the creature as they were swimming in the Nagaflow. The dragon, along with some of his black firedrake children, then went to ambush the ambassador of Arrabar, which was a city that would have been on the northern end of the canal route in the Vilhon Reach, as he was traveling on the road to Innarlith, killing the ambassador and all witnesses, but purposely without using his acid breath weapon. He then dumped the body of the dead naga at the site in an attempt to give the impression that Svayyah's tribe was responsible for the attack.

Marek Rymüt later attempted to use a Maurezhi summoned from the abyss to assassinate Ransar Pristoleph after the latter had reinstated Devorast as chief builder for the canal. Marek got the Maurezhi to disguise himself as Svayyah while making the assassination attempt. Pristoleph's bodyguards fought off the Maurezhi and wounded it during the failed assassination attempt by cutting off its polymorphed ear. Pristoleph then went to the canal site to demand answers from Devorast about what happened to his agreement with the nagas. Svayyah then appeared and told Pristoleph that she had nothing to do with the attempt on his life and revealed that both of her ears were still intact, which led Pristoleph to doubt that it was really her behind the attack.

## Svian's club

Svian's club was an enchanted club that belonged to Svian of the Reghedmen Tribe of the Elk.

Description
Svian's club was a massive one-handed bludgeon with a metallic band of spikes attached to the weapon's head.

Powers
This club possessed a powerful +5 enchantment.

History
Svian's club belonged to a grim, stoic, and intimidating hero of the Tribe of the Elk, Svian, long before 1281 DR. The barbarian was known never to show emption, get excited, nor panic in combat. His battle tactics were simple: stand your ground and pulverize every opponent who approaches with Svian's club. Later in the hero's career, he was mortally wounded in battle. Without showing pain nor suffering, he simply sat down at a nearby rock and passed away. Several hours later, his tribesmen four the body. They never realized he was wounded and did not notice his passing until it was too late.

The barbarian was interred in one of the barrows of the Burial Isle. The club remained there as of 1281 DR, guarded by the spirits of Reghedmen ancestors and later that year, by disturbed undead.

## Svartalfheim cloak

Svartalfheim cloaks were magical cloaks from the realm of Svartalfheim.

Description
These cloaks were black and blue in coloration, made of a light material, and had a fur collar.

Powers
These cloaks granted their wearers increased powers of stealth, on par with that of a cloak of elvenkind, and granted the power to cast wraithform once per day.

History
In the, a group of adventurers from the city of Ravens Bluff on the world of Toril traveled to Ysgard in search of the legendary Well of Mimir. While on their journey they came into the possession of one of these cloaks. According to some, the Ravenians earned one from the frost giant Brimir for competing against his fellow giants. Other sources told of the Ravenians having broken into Brimir's hall and stolen the cloak from his bedroom or having won a Svartalfheim cloak from Queen Titania for having competed against fey of the Seelie Court in a series of challenges.

## Svartlebee's Woundseeker

Svartlebee's Woundseeker was a unique enchanted greatsword that once was famously wielded by Svartlebee the Custodian, sometime before the late 15 century DR.

Powers
The Woundseeker possessed a minor +1 enchantment that guided the blade towards the target and increased its deadliness. Additionally, the weapon was especially effective against opponents who had already suffered damage—each subsequent slash with the blade resulted in it deepening the wounds, in turn causing heavier damage and leaving bleeding lacerations behind.

History
The Woundseeker belonged to Svartlebee the Custodian, a mercenary who used the weapon to remove nuisances from people's lives and cleaned up the crime scenes afterwards. Subsequently, the blade changed owners, eventually falling into the hands of the Flaming Fist Gauntlet Yeva, who accompanied Duke Ulder Ravengard on a mission to Elturel in the, and survived an attack from the Cult of the Absolute in the Waukeen's Rest roadside inn.

## Svian

Svian was a somewhat famous Reghedmen warrior of the Tribe of the Elk who was an unmoving wall of stone in battle sometime long before the late 13 century DR.

Description
Svian, a man of imposing stature, stood tall and wiry, his grim countenance a reflection of his inner strength. He was renowned in the north for his stoicism, serious demeanor, and unwavering cool in battle.

History
Svian won many battles in his life, never getting scared, never getting excited, by simply standing firm and devastating his enemies with powerful blows. It was believed that when he was gravely injured in battle, sometime before the late 13 century DR, Svian simply walked to a rock, sat down, with no indication of pain, and died. It would take his tribesmen several hours to realize Svian had passed. He left behind a powerful enchanted club that would later be known as Svian's club. The weapon followed its master to the Reghedmen Burial Isle and stayed there at least until the, when it was recovered from the recently disturbed dead by the hermit wizard Eddion Carradoc.

## Svenyird Olafsson

King Svenyird Olafsson was ruler of the Northlander kingdom of Gnarhelm in the Moonshae Isles during the early-to-mid 14 century DR.

Description
He had a ruddy complexion. His build was not too large by Northalnder standards.

Personality
He was wise and patient, and slow to disregard his allies. He valued courage and the willingness to go to war even as he counseled in favor of peace. He loved a gripping tale.

Relationships
He was the son of King Rolf Olafsson of Gnarhelm and cousin to King Dagus of Olafstaad. His son and heir was Brandon Olafsson.

History
Circa, when Svenyird was still young, his father hosted Elminster at Olafstaad. In the face of the boy's desire to see the world, the sage agreed to take him back to Waterdeep to study with his apprentices and to become literate.

Svenyird received his first scars in a battle with the Ffolk sometime before the signing of the Treaty of Oman in the . He would go on to gain fame for slaying a sea dragon, whose head he would later mount in his throne room.

When his father died in the, Svenyird assumed his throne.

In the, he apppointed his son as an ambassador to the court of High King Tristan Kendrick in order to ascertain why the Ffolk had apparently violated the Treaty of Oman.

He died of illness in the, and was succeeded by Brandon.
