## Izac Uthor

Izac Uthor was a goblin commander who rose to power during the turmoil that wracked goblinkind near the lands bordering western Anauroch before the Battle of Bones occurred.

Personality
Izac was brutish and ambitious. He recognized the potential benefit of uniting a horde of goblins against a common "enemy."

Activities
In the winter of 1089 DR, Izac gathered his army near the abandoned village of Thandril near the northwestern portion of the Goblin Marches.  His horde included 200,000 goblins and tens of thousands of orcs and other goblin kin.  This army called itself the Thugs of Uthor.

## Izhkin

Personality
He seemed incomprehensible and was content with great acts of violence to keep ruling his realm.

Relationships
He had an assistant by the name of Golgamann who only spoke to him.

## Izimmur

The Izimmur family was one of the twenty influential families that made up the Onsruur (the hereditary council of lords) in Khôltar, south Faerûn, circa the Year of Wild Magic, 1372 DR.

Organization
The Izimmurs tended to be short and thin, with black hair and eyes so dark they could also be called black. They never forgot a slight, but did not usually resort to physical violence against those that wronged them, choosing to poison them instead. This family kept as much of their activities as secret as possible, and spied on others to learn their secrets.

Activities
This family researched various crafters, and those that they invested in were chosen with care. All businesses in which they had a stake were spied upon to monitor progress and constantly exhorted to increase profits. They were quite proud of their stable of crafters and businesses, thinking of them as their prized possessions or pets.

## Izzaxu

Izzaxu was a dreadful creature of flames forged out of fire elementals by the drow who was bound to the ancient Kiln behind the vault door in the Forlorn Cliffs of the  Middledark.

Description
Izzaxu appeared as a floating helmed skeletal construct-like creature that lacked its lower half. Engulfed in roaring flames, it wielded two sabers.

Abilities
Izzaxu had access to a variety of fire-based spells and abilities, such as burning hands, flame whirl (Izzaxu whirled around in a twister of flames and  blades damaging everyone around), overheat (the very air around the creature heated up attempting to incinerate everyone around). Izzaxu could channel the energies of the Elemental Plane of Fire to cause an explosive wave of flames centered on itself. It was also immune to all mundane and magical fire.

History
Izzaxu resided inside the secure magical vault that came to be known as Izzaxu's Kiln, the ancient abandoned site of countless drow summoning rituals. The masters of the site dabbled in conjuration and created the creature using fire elementals. Eventually the drow left Izzaxu's Kiln, bounding the flaming creature to the summoning chamber.

Eventually, the vault was discovered by Torga Burduum and her group of dwarves searching for the means of creating powerful weaponry. Reaching the ancient Kiln, she uncovered magic texts describing a creature called Izzaxu. Foolishly, Toga and her group broke the seals that were holding the elemental monster, thinking they would be able to control the creature. Izzaxu was too powerful for the dwarves and incinerated the dwarves as it exited the portal. Those who avoided the infernal flames fled through the caves, triggering the traps left behind by the drow, and met their doom after being trapped inside Izzaxu's Kiln.

Circa 1490 DR, Izzaxu's Kiln was unsealed by the Champion of the Burning Dawn. The Kiln was teeming with creatures summoned by the drow traps triggered by the fleeing dwarfs. The Champion and the companions fought through hezrous, quasits, dretches, and blazing skeletons before reaching the ghostly dwarves of Torga Burduum's group. Agreeing to free her from the cursed undead existence, the champion challenged Izzaxu, destroying the creature. Torga channeled Izzaxu's flames and essence to create her greatest work—Torga's Pyre. She gifted the enchanted sword to the Champion and departed for the afterlife.

## Izco I

Izco the Elder, or Izco the First was the Revered Counselors of the great Nexalan empire whose rule started almost exactly 100 years before the arrival of the Golden Legion in 1361 DR.

History
Izco was elevated to the Nexalan throne after the death of Revered Counselor Zomoc in the . He was selected by the nobility of Nexal due to being a descendant of the great Revered Counsellor Ipana and a nephew of general Coyo who was sacrificed to the Bloody Zaltec by Zomoc in the .

Tenth Revered Counselor of Nexal, Izco the Elder's rule was similar to that of his son – filled with cultural growth and artistic growth. Under his rule, the trade relationship with Pezelac was made consistent and reliable while Huacli and other subjugated settlements continued to pay tribute to Nexal. This period of time was known for the highest number of bloody sacrifices, men and women, to the god of war Zaltec, using the tribute slaves from the conquered states. Seldomly subjugated rates attempted to rebel against Nexal. Those who dated were faced with Nexal's armies and a higher number of sacrifices, while states that attempted to negotiate night number of sacrifices were dealt with more leniently.

A rare occurrence in Nexala history – the rulership was passed from Izco the Elder to his son, Izco II, while the Revered Counselor was still alive. To celebrate the passing of the throne, Izco the First added a layer to the Great Pyramid of Zaltec, making it even higher and cementing its status as the highest man-made structure in Maztica.

It was celebrated by carving out a thousand hearts in the glory of the Bloody God, a tame number by all accords. The humble sacrifice angered the cruel deity, and the volcano of Mount Zatal awoke. The mountain retched lava onto Nexal and into the waters of the great Lake Tezca. The lake's waves battered the island where Nexal stood as well as the Valley of Nexal's lowlands. The destruction was minimal as most stone buildings affected remained standing and the havoc avoided most grown crops. The death toll of the disaster was several hundred Nexala. Without wasting time, zealous priests of Zaltec started another Feathered War against the cities of the Valley of Nexal. All city-states took ten thousand men and women from their homes, and all were immediately butchered in honor of Zaltec. When the Feathered War was over, and the Nexala atoned, Izco the Second took the throne of the Revered Counselor.

Izco the Elder spent the rest of his life as a poet and songster as well as learned to weave pluma. And his son's reign ended less than forty years after Izco the Elder ascended on the throne.

History
Izco's passing of power to his son, Izco II, was celebrated with a humble sacrifice of a thousand men and women, a humble number that angered the deity of Nexala. Zaltec the Bloody awoken Mount Zatal causing a disaster, and later, a large-scale Feathered War was organized to atone in the eyes of their god. After the cities of the Valley of Nexal carved ten thousand hearts, the bloodthirsty was was sated, and Izco II took the throne from his still-living predecessor.

The rule of Izco II was similar to his fathers, characterized by cultural and artistic growth of Nexal and continuous flow of tribute of goods and slaves from the subjugated territories.

With the death of Izco II, some less than 100 years before the arrival of Amnian colonists, Nexal picked its next ruler from among the military – venerable general Pakli whose rule ended in the .

## Izu's hammer of fabrication

Izu's hammer of fabrication was an enchanted hammer wielded by a loyal Harper ally named Izu, sometime before 1373 DR.

Powers
The hammer of fabrication possessed a powerful +6 enchantment, making it a devastating weapon, especially for a light hammer. Additionally, Izu's hammer dramatically improved the wielder's mastery over crafting armor and weapons as well as magically imbued them with knowledge of lore. However, the weapon's magic made sure the hammer could only be wielded by a Harper.

History
Izu was a blind master smith who died in the Silver Marches sometime before the late 14 century DR. As the blind smith spent years of his life making enchanted armor and weapons for the Harpers, his heirs gifted Izu's hammer of fabrication to a young Harper scout the smith was especially fond of. Sometime later, that Harper went missing, along with the hammer, while mapping the Upperdark.

By the, Izu's hammer of fabrication found its way to the city of Melvaunt on the Moonsea. The hammer was kept by the Gondar priests of The Purple Portals.

## Izwae

Izwae was a drow mercenary who accompanied Dhourn Ba'Tol during his search for the legendary Adamantine Forge.

History
The drow wizard Dhourn Ba'Tol vowed to share his fortune with a group of drow mercenaries in exchange for protection if they find the Adamantine Forge. The group consisted of Arcagh, Artimezt, Izwae, Moy and Tecothy.

However, Dhourn's rival Xargrim sought to find the Forge first and once met Dhourn. The rivals were both planning an ambush against each other, but began to talk of a pact, ultimately arguing until their words turned to screams. Xargrim summoned an unusual spectator adept at petrification and fled. Dhourn and his companions were all petrified for so long that Dhourn's memory shard faded.

## Izzinid Claddafin

Izzinid Claddafin was a scholastic Eilistraean drow of Eilistraee's Wanderers in the late 14 century DR.

Description
Izzinid had thick white hair kept tied back, and wore a green outfit.

History

In the, the well-educated Izzinid followed Kelgoth Darksong joined the Elves of Cormanthor at Myth Drannor, following Kelgoth's aim of restoring the mythal. They stayed at the fortified camp nearby, but the surfacer elves were both distrustful and paternalistically protective of the drow, and refused to help them go into the ruins. Frustrated by this, Kelgoth, Zelliira, and Uhlsdrin Nirveth went into the ruins on their own to find the three Icons that would unlock the Key to the Mythal, only to quickly run into trouble. With the prompting of Aelorin Ungive, the elves and their allies set about helping the drow and finishing their quest, eventually finding all three of the Icons. For the final stage of the plan, Izzinid organized an expedition into the ruins of Ildrannath's Tower, where the Key to the Mythal was supposed to be kept. Although it was indeed there, it was ultimately stolen by the forces of the Red Wizard Droic Orgul―sent from Thay to raid and loot the ruins of Myth Drannor—prompting a short struggle between the elves and Red Wizards over the fate of the city.
