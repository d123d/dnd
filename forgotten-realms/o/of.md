## Of the Fall of Netheril

Of the Fall of Netheril was a book written by Rhaugilath the Ageless, also known as the Shackled Scribe of Larloch, a Netherese archlich dwelling at Warlock's Crypt.

The book detailed Netheril's complete history. However, Rhaugilath considered the Netherese Diaspora after Netheril's fall to be an integral part of the history, as well as the expected restoration of Netheril, thought to be only beginning in 1374 DR. Thus, Of the Fall of Netheril was never likely to be finished, though it had reached several volumes by that time.

Larloch reviewed each chapter as it was completed, and they were delivered to Candlekeep to be archived in the library there.

In one passage, Rhaugilath discussed the phaerimm, the formation of the Anauroch, and history's perception of Netheril. The book also provided important information about the mysterious Terraseer and mentioned the castle of Spellgard.

## Offices of the Middle Magistrate

The offices of the Middle Magistrate were the base of operations of the Middle Magistrate of Bral.

Location
The complex was located on Trader's Way in Middle City, on the leading starboard side of Bral's Great Market, about a half block away and around the corner from the curios shop of Gamalon Idogyr, the famous Tethyrian mage.

Structure
The complex consisted of several connected buildings: a courtroom, a jail with six cells, a barracks for the Magistrate's Watch, and offices. The architecture here was far less fancy than that of the High Magistrate's.

Activities
The Watch of the Middle City were far more active than those of the High City and included about 20 guardsmen and several mages. Guardsmen were well-equipped and trained fighters, wearing chainmail and carrying longswords and crossbows. They patrolled in groups of four to a dozen, though only occasionally, unless they were summoned to apprehend a particularly dangerous criminal.

## Offalmound

Offalmound, sometimes named interchangeably with the name of its Abyssal layer–Rarandreth, was the 223 layer of the Abyss, and the domain of an ancient Netherese deity of rot and decay called Moander.

History
Many scholars argued that after the perceived death of its ancient master Moander in the, the layer ceased to exist. Another school of thought on the matter was that along with the deity's body, their realm was ejected beyond the Astral Plane, where it floated adrift. These theories seem to have been false or Moander's demise was not as complete as many thought. As of the late-14 century DR, Offalmound remained a layer of the Abyss, with its lordship contested. The layer remained contested following the Spellplague, but as Moander was fully restored as a power of Lower Planes, the fate of his putrid Offalmound remained unclear.

## Of Healthful Enchantments for the Body

Of Healthful Enchantments for the Body was a tome that collected crafting recipes for various enchanted belts and boots.

Contents
The book collected ingredients, recipes, and arcane formulae required for crafting and enchanting several magic articles of clothing. Some of the recipes listed included belts of agility that required use of elemental essence of air, star sapphire or rogue stone, and spells of cat's grace. The recipe for belt or frost giant's strength required blue diamonds or jacinths and a bull's strength spell. Another item listed in the tome was a pair of boots of striding.

History
As of the, several copies of the tome could be found across the Sword Coast. One copy found its way into the hands of Shadow Priests who worshiped the King of Shadows in the Crossroad Keep, before the fort was re-taken by the Graycloaks of Neverwinter and the Kalach-Cha.

## Of Blades and Blooded Honor

Of Blades and Blooded Honor was an ancient book that collected lore and history of the elves of Cormanthyr, preserved and secured behind Candlekeep's fortress walls as of the 14 century DR.

History
On 11 of Flamerule, the, Athol of Candlekeep responded to Danilo Thann's of Waterdeep numerous very persistent requests regarding the Of Blades and Blooded Honor. Athol turned down the Harper as the tome he had his eye on was ancient and one of the five oldest books stored in the library fortress of Candlekeep. Instead, Athol sent Danilo a sampling pages copied from the book, and if the Waterdhavian was satisfied with the contents, Athol offered to hire a scribe to make a complete copy foe a humble price of 5,000 gp. A generous offer, considering the scribe's youth and lack of experience.

## Of Style and Grace (magic shop)

Of Style and Grace was a magic shop in the city of Ravens Bluff in the 14 century DR that specialized in spell components. The shop's name was a play on words of the owner's name, Grace Stiles.

Location
This shop was located roughly within the Gowntown neighborhood of the city's Temple District, standing along Fire Lane.

Structure
This shop stood within a fairly good-sized stone building with six pillars that stood outside. It was only a single story, but the building was quite tall. A wooden sign hung above its entrance that read "Of Style and Grace" in jagged Common lettering and beneath that had a pentagram burnt into it. The sign was considered to be unsettling by most Ravenians. Upon opening the entry door, a small bell would ring.

The interior of the building had a vaulted ceiling and was divided into two sections, with the front serving as the shop and the rear serving as both the living quarters of the owner and the store's storage space. A narrow hallway led into the back half of the building, separated by a counter. The counter was modest and not very functional, serving only to deter patrons from wandering into the backroom.

The shop had a large pentagram carved into its wooden flooring, with the outline filled with silver. A large red velvet chair stood at its center, along with a small stand with a silver bowl of peaches. The owner could often be found sitting there, reading some magical tome.

Services
Of Style and Grace specialized in selling spell components, though it did not keep them on open display. Rather Grace would fetch the desired components from storage and do transactions with a purse she wore around her waist at all times.

Reputation
Among some, the shop carried a reputation of dealing in evil magic, which was a misconception born from the owner's predilection for collecting cursed items. However, as she found mild amusement in these rumors, she did nothing to quell them, considering them to be little more than harmless fun.

History
Sometime in the late 14 century DR, a group of adventurers visited Of Style and Grace under the direction of John Porter in hopes of finding a means to cure Marian Wicksal of poison she had ingested. Hesitant to aid someone she considered her long-time rival, Grace directed the adventurers to seek out a herbalist in Crow's End that went by the moniker of The Root Lady.

Later, another group of adventurers visited Of Style and Grace after being told by Aaron Fleeting that it was where the illusionist con artist Mortimer Mittlemer typically shopped for spell components, but this was a clever lie meant to give Fleeting time to make distance between him and the adventurers. When questioned, Grace denied knowing anything about Aaron, his sister Erin Fleetwing, or Mortimer, though expressed resentment over being associated with the con artist.

## Of Thine Will

Of Thine Will  was a shadar-kai who led a group of servants of the Raven Queen into the shadow-cursed lands of Faerûn during the late 15 century DR.

History
In the, Of Thine Will led He Who Was and Lover's Whisper into the lands rich with darkness and tragedy. The malignant curse that twisted the shadow-cursed lands into the mockery of darkness and evil was too strong even for the Shadowfell's shadar-kai. They witnessed the land's darkness, making Lover's Whisper strangle her own raven and the Raven Queen ordered their retreat. Despite Of Thine Will's protestations, He Who Was stayed behind to punish those souls who deserved a post-mortem judgement.

## Officially Unofficial History of the Circus of the Last Days!

Officially Unofficial History of the Circus of the Last Days!, or Officially Unofficial Circus History for short, was a book dedicated to the history of the planar Circus of the Last Days from the Feywild, penned sometime before the late 15 century DR.

Content
The book collected the history of the circus and its last entry was the last time the Circus of the Last Days made a stop in Baldur's Gate before the . During that visit, the performances were headlined by someone called Dumpy Dumpling, who had been performing for a decade, combining firework farts and chicken-snapping, as well as his famous pie-in-the-ear trick. Despite the crowd cheering on, Dumpy lost his spark and passion for performances. One day, during a show, he called on a volunteer. A sly man with reddish-hair stepped onto the stage. The volunteer seemed to have charisma enough to inspire pure joy with his command of the stage. He caused riotous laughter with his one-liners and more with his witty comebacks. Not just the crowd was mesmerized by the redheaded volunteer, so was the book's author. When the show was over, the writer forgot that he was watching Dumpy Dumpling's show and not the red-headed man's.

Lucretious, the proprietress of the Circus of the Last Days quickly realized the volunteer's star quality and recruited him into her troupe. She offered him a job right there on stage to the crowd's thunderous applause. This was the origin of Dribbles the Clown, Norris Greenie.

History
In 1492 DR, a copy of Officially Unofficial History of the Circus of the Last Days! was sold by Nansi Gretta in her book store, The Bibliophile, in the Lower City of Baldur's Gate.
