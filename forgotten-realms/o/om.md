## Omlarandin Mountains

The Omlarandin Mountains were a mountain range in Tethyr and Erlkazar, known for its richness in omlar gems, which had a variety of magical uses.

Etymology
The name "omlarandin" is a corruption of Dwarvish olaramorndin, which means "magical peaks".

Geography
The Omlarandin Mountains were a cluster of lightly forested mountains located in the highlands in the east of Tethyr. They were the smallest grouping of mountains that were part of the greater Iltkazar Range and—with only a few exceptions—were little higher than the foothills around them.

The Omlarandins were most famous for being the only known source of omlar gems in Faerûn, though only a few were ever discovered in a given century.

One notable rock formation in the mountains was called Orators' Crag, due to its strange acoustics. Anyone whispering from the small outcropping in the cliff could be heard within the tiny valley below. The crag overlooked the upper waters of the Idolflow river.

The Levast River also had its headwaters in the northern reaches of the mountains.

Inhabitants
The Omlarandins were the most populated mountains in Tethyr after the fall of Iltkazar. Mountain giants and alaghi can be found there.

Bleucorundum, the "Sapphire Sentinel", a very old male sapphire dragon, guarded over the Sleeping Legion of the Knights of the Crescent Moon, in a secret location in the heart of the mountains.

Monsters
As late as 1372 DR, the mountains were inhabited by wyverns and displacer beasts. The latter were dubbed "Omlarcats", and in 1370 DR it was said that there were more displacer beasts in the Omlarandins than anywhere else in Faerûn. Though they caused problems with the shepherds of Saradush by stealing from their herds, the Omlarcats were only dangerous during their mating season, which fell in autumn each year, and the following spring when their young were born.

Trails & Byways
The trail from Saradush to Tibold passed through the mountains and was known as Borthun's Walk, after the famous dwarven explorer Borthun the Walker. The trail was generally considered safe; however, in the 1370s, wyverns nesting north of the trail began causing problems.

By 1370 DR, the Twisted Rune had placed a magical gate in the area to allow for instantaneous movement to other locations.

Notable Locations
 Chapel of the Blooded Moon  This buried ancient chapel was where the Sleeping Legion of the Knights of the Crescent Moon waited to be called upon to defend Tethyr against beholders again. The tunnels beneath the Chapel included an entrance into Deep Shanatar.
 Earthsky Pass  This was a steeply sloped valley in the center of the mountains.

History
Around, the armies of the Tavihr Dynasty of Calimshan established garrisons in the region of these mountains south of the River Ith.

## Oman

Oman was a Northlander kingdom that comprised the whole of Oman's Isle as well as the northern coast of Gwynneth in the Moonshae Isles. It was sometimes known as the kingdom of Iron Keep in honor of the great fortress that was its seat of power. This realm collapsed when Oman's Isle was overrun by fomorians and cyclopes in the late 14 century DR.

Description
Oman was the largest, most populous, most prosperous, and most powerful among the Northmen realms of the Moonshaes as of the mid–14 century DR. It was known to be peaceful within its own borders.

Geography
By the mid–14 century DR, the kingdom controlled all of Oman's Isle as well as the north coast of Gwynneth across the Strait of Oman, extending from the Strait of Alaron in the east to the mouth of the Strait of the Leviathan in the west and encompassing Codsbay Mire, parts of Winterglen, and Taeya Bog. Their claim extend as far south as the mountains of Highhome in the east and the Black Mountains in the west.

Its capital was Iron Keep on the north shore of Oman's Isle, located on the sheltered Iron Bay.

Government
Oman was ruled from Iron Keep by a king, who in turn presided over many subject kings across Oman's Isle. They projected their control over the northern shore of Gwynneth, where Northlander and Ffolk settlements alike fell under their jurisdiction. Their territory on Gwynneth included parts of the forest of Winterglen, and although it nominally extended all the way to the norther border of Myrloch Vale, the kings were more interested in the coast and the sea than anything further inland.

Defenses
Oman boasted the largest fleet of longships in the Moonshae Isles. This fleet was made more dangerous by the high level of coordination they could achieve in battle. In addition, Oman's armies were said to be the fiercest of the Moonshaes, with each minor king able to field as many as ten longship crews of 40 warriors or berserkers each.

Aside from raiding, the main concern of Oman's armies were the firbolgs of the Cambro Mountains, who threatened the realm's settlements on Gwynneth.

Trade
The people of Oman fished, farmed, raised livestock, and bred horses. Their main exports were goods and slaves seized in their raids against their Ffolk neighbors, timbers, and gold, much of which they traded for goods from the other Northlander isles. They were also known to trade with places like Ruathym and Waterdeep for the luxuries produced by more advanced societies. The port at Iron Keep was a major stop on the maritime trade routes with the Northlander isles to the north and lands as far as Waterdeep.

While there was little notable agriculture for export, the people of Oman were more equipped than most Northlander realms at feeding themselves, and did so by cultivating barley, potatos, and wheat and by breeding large herds of cattle, goats, sheep, and swine. They were also prolific hunters and fishers, although only the waters east of Oman's Isle boasted rich fishing grounds.

The shipwrights of Oman, particularly in Iron Keep, were said to be among the very best in the Northmen realms. They specialized in building longships, and their process involved the contributions of alchemists to make sealant, blacksmiths to make iron sockets and nails, ropemakers to twist ropes, and sewists to make the sails.

History
Oman shared much of the ancient history of the rest of the Moonshaes. In the 3 century DR, within a couple of generations of the coronation of Cymrych Hugh among the Ffolk of the Moonshaes, Illuskan raiders from Tuern and Gundarlun began attacking the Isles, and by the 10 century, they had launched an invasion and had come to settle in five separate realms: Gnarhelm, the Korinn Archipelago, Norheim, Norland, and Oman. Oman's Isle itself had once been occupied by the Ffolk before the Northmen conquest, and the Northmen would erect their Iron Keep atop an existing Ffolk castle. By the 14 century DR, Oman had long since expanded beyond Oman's Isle and gained control over the northern coast of Gwynneth.

By the time that Elminster first visited the Moonsahes in the early-to-mid-14 century DR, the Kingdom of Oman was the mightiest of the Northlander relams, and its ruler, Iron King Thelgaar Ironhand, had become one of the Northmen's greatest leaders. Thelgaar had assumed the throne around the turn of the 14 century DR, and much of his realm's strength had been attributed to her personal accomplishments in uniting the lords in his domain into a united nation. He famously waged successful wars against the giants of the Grampalt Highlands, united the other kings of Oman's Isle under his leadership, and stamped out all organized resistance to his rule on Oman's Isle. King Thelgaar was killed and usurped by Kazgoroth on Mirtul 15 of the, leading to the Darkwalker War.

Eventually, High King Tristan Kendrick brokered a peace between his Ffolk and the Northlanders under the Treaty of Oman in the . Then in the, High Queen Alicia Kendrick of the Ffolk managed to unite her people and the Northlanders into the United Moonshae Isles, making good progress in merging the cultures of both peoples. Oman became the center of this new Northlander culture as the people gave up their lives of raiding others and instead became self-sufficient by farming, hunting and fishing—selling their longboats instead of using them to attack their neighbors. They also welcomed a great influx of settlers, including Ffolk.

In the, it was said that Northlander raiders flying the colors of Iron Keep had begun to raid Ffolk settlements and merchants. It was unclear whether these were imposters or a return to tradition for the famously warlike Northlanders of Oman, and it was feared that mishandling of the situation by High Queen Alicia could push Oman into open revolt.

In the  a group of fomorians from the Feywild, under the command of the evil Queen Connomae of Vor Thomil, invaded Oman through a gate in the Grampalt Highlands. Together with malevolent native giants (namely firbolgs), they conquered the island, overrunning all Northlander and Ffolk villages, slaughtering the people, and ultimately toppling Iron Keep by Midsummer of that year. The Northlander settlers on Gwynneth would ultimately be driven from the island by the fey of Sarifal in the early 15 century DR.

Seeking to reclaim what was lost, a clan of Northlanders on Norland calling themselves Oman giantkillers developed fomorian-slaying tactics and recruited allies to help them retake Iron Keep and Oman's Isle.

Inhabitants
The Northlanders of Oman were among the most feared and warlike in the Moonshaes. Once peace had been established with the Ffolk, their aggressions turned toward Northlander raiders from outside the Moonshae Isles, most notably out of Ruathym.

Oman's towns were larger than most other Northlander communities, often numbering over a thousand inhabitants, and also tended to be wealthier and better fed.

A number of magic-users served Oman's king as of the mid–14 century DR, who were known to enchant weapons for their liege. Given the widespread hatred of the practitioners of arcane magic among the Northlanders, these spellcasters were kept secret from the public.

## Omlar gem

Omlar gems were exceptionally rare gems mined from the Omlarandin Mountains in Tethyr.

Description
Omlars were rock crystals with about the same hardness and color as aquamarines and could also be mistaken for emeralds of a lighter shade.

Powers
Omlar gems had the ability to easily absorb enchantments, which made them a coveted component of many a wizard's powerful magical items.

Source
These gems were only found deep in the hills and mountains of the Omlarandins and were so rare even there that only a few were found every hundred years.

Reputation
Because of their rarity and powers, omlar gems fetched sums of money worthy of princes and were sought out by wizards in Duhlnarim, Llorbauth, Saradush, and Zazesspur.

Notable uses
Omlar gems were used in the construction of the minarets lining the Trade Way in the Calim Desert from Calimport to Memnon during the Shoon Imperium. These minarets were erected in pairs, one on each side of the road. On the rooftop parapets of each of these 40-ft-high (12 m) towers, a fist-sized omlar gem was placed above a brass brazier. When the braziers were lit, the omlar gems glowed a brilliant blue and together created a magical field that protected the Trade Way out to 1,500 yd (1,370 m). This magical field defended against wind, sand, and glare and also against the effects of Calim's breath and Memnon's crackle and against purple worms.

After the fall of the Shoon Empire, the Trade Way and its minarets fell into disuse and decay. More than a third of the omlar crystals were pried out of their settings and stolen, to be sold for exorbitant prices in Calimport and Memnon.

In 1370 DR, Syl-pasha Ralan el Pesarkhal was actively seeking for wizards to help him restore the minarets and thus the financially beneficial traffic on the Trade Way. By three-quarters of the way through the year, only the first pair of minarets had been restored.

## Omu

Omu, also known as the Forbidden City, was a ruined city in Chult. The city was infamous for the subterranean dungeon-complex known as the Tomb of the Nine Gods. This was the location chosen by Acererak to place the soulmonger and trap the souls of previously-deceased Torilians, in the 15–century phenomenon known as the death curse.

Geography
Omu was located in a basin surrounded by cliffs in the heart of the southern Chultan jungle. It was extremely difficult to find.

History

The city was built atop rich veins of minerals and gems. In its early days, Omu prospered through mining and commerce, and Omuan jewelry was valued far and wide. Its riches and power led to greed, as the city demanded offerings from its neighbors in the form of slaves to work the mines. The city's fearsome armies soon marched across Chult as its people descended into depravity. This angered their patron god Ubtao.

Led by their generals, such as Thiru-taya, the Omuans extended their influence at least as far as the River Olung, and during the reign of Queen Zalkoré, they constructed a royal retreat along that river known as Ka-Nanji. Both Zalkoré and Thiru-taya fell out of favor after the queen struck a deal with an erinyes to preserve her beauty forever.

The city was forsaken by Ubtao sometime in the 13 century DR, and the clerics that lived within lost their divine power. The slaves revolted against their oppressors and the city fell to ruin. Signs of the once-great society, its currency and maps showing its location, were purposefully destroyed. It truly became the Forgotten City.

The city's lack of an overseeing deity drew the attention of primal spirits from the jungle. They offered power to scattered Omuans that remained, in exchange for their veneration. The Omuans promptly destroyed their temples to Ubtao and worshiped the brutal and discordant spirits, who came known as the nine trickster gods.

The trickster gods' power was limited. Since they could not grant spells to all their clerics, they demanded sacrifices and blood trials to test their resolve and entertain the decadent population. Their reign over Omu lasted for the next 90 years.

Sometime in the late 14 century DR, the trickster gods' behavior piqued the curiosity of the cosmos-traveling lich Acererak. Inspired by their bloody trials, Acererak invaded Omu, killed the trickster gods and forced the Omuans into servitude. The lich forced them to construct his own murderous dungeon underneath the city, one that would contain the malevolent spirits, known as the Tomb of the Nine Gods. Acererak then slaughtered the remaining Omuans and sealed their fates alongside those of their gods.

In the mid‒15 century DR, a group of yuan-ti from Hisari settled in Omu, followed by Ras Nsi, who was fleeing from Mezro. When a natural disaster shook the city and killed the yuan-ti abomination leader, Nsi undertook a ritual to transform into a malison and became the leader of the group.

A number of outlander groups led expeditions throughout the city over the following years, including adventurers, tabaxi and even Red Wizards from Thay.

## Oman's Isle

Oman's Isle, also known as simply Oman, was an island in the Sea of Moonshae within the middle of the Moonshae Isles. It was the heart of the kingdom of Oman prior to the 15 century DR, after which it was ruled by the giants of Vor Thomil.

Description
The Isle itself was peaceful and lovely, but made extremely dangerous by the giants who claimed it as their own and would hurl rocks at passing ships. Its shores were rocky.

Geography
Oman was a vaguely triangular island, with three major geographical features: the Old Stone Wood that lay along the east side of the island, the Grampalt Highlands that made up the southern part, and the Iron Bay that was located in the north. The seas around the island were rough, making the shielded waters of Iron Bay an ideal anchorage.

Oman's Isle was largely bordered by the Sea of Moonshae to the north and east, while the Strait of Oman separated it from Gwynneth and the southern portion of Oman in the south.

Flora & Fauna
The island supported large herds of cattle, goats, horses, sheep, and swine and was suited to the growing of barley, potatos, and wheat.

History
The island was originally settled by the Ffolk after their arrival in the Moonshaes during the 2 century DR. When the Northlanders discovered the Ffolk and began to raid them in the mid–3 century DR, the settlers of Oman struggled to resist. Eventually, the Northlanders would decide to conquer rather than raid, and would establish one of their kingdoms on the island. They captured the Ffolk castle on Iron Bay and expanded into their own fortress of Iron Keep. By the 14 century DR, the Northlander realm of Oman was the mightiest of the Northlander kingdoms.

In the, Grond Peaksmasher was freed from his glacial prison on Oman's Isle (where he had been imprisoned since circa  after a war with the dwarves of the Moonshaes) and brought firbolgs back to Oman's Isle, long absent since the invasion of the Northlanders, having retreated to the mountains of Gwynneth. Moonshae firbolgs had degenerated into corrupted evil beasts, but Grond hoped that under his guidance, they began to both culturally and physically resemble their more peaceful mainland cousins.

Following the creation of the United Moonshae Isles in the, the Northlanders of Oman's Isle welcomed Ffolk settlers.

In the  a group of fomorians from the Feywild, under the command of the evil Queen Connomae of Vor Thomil, invaded Oman through a gate in the Grampalt Highlands. Together with malevolent native giants, they conquered the island, slaughtered its inhabitants, and toppled Iron Keep by Midsummer of that year. They subsequently thwarted any attempts to retake the island, and in the decades that followed, Oman's Isle was known as a giant-ruled wilderness and the most dangerous of all the Moonshae Isles.

Driven to reclaim the island, a clan of Northlanders calling themselves Oman giantkillers developed and shared tactics for killing fomorians and their ilk. They hoped to recruit allies to help them retake Iron Keep.

By the late 15 century DR, both King Derid of the Ffolk and Jarl Rault of Norland desired to reclaim Oman for their respective peoples, but the giants attacked every boat that approached within a stone's throw of the island. It was thought that either of the rulers might bestow handsome rewards on any adventurers who could deliver them the Iron Keep. Rich seams of gold and iron, as well as the pristine woodlands of Oman were a primary motivator for the re-taking of the island.

Inhabitants
Prior to the fall of their kingdom on the isle, Northlanders ruled and populated the island. As of the late 15 century DR, the island was populated by firbolgs, fomorians, and other giants. The giant demigod Grond Peaksmasher also resided on the island for a time.

Notable Locations
There were several major settlements on the island, such as the Iron Keep, Lillemaren, Skien, Trondhelm, and Ramshorn. Other notable locations included the Icepeak and the Oman Moonwell.

The Cairn of Thelgaar Ironhand was said to hold riches and powerful magic and that it had remained undisturbed by the giants occupying Oman, but that it was also well-protected by the bound spirits of animals as well as more mundane traps.

## Omen

Omen was a common spell used by shugenja and wu jen in Kara-Tur. The spell was very rare outside Kara-Tur.

Effects
A caster used this spell to predict the outcome of an adventure or an event such as a battle. During casting, a wu jen communicated with spirits and a shugenja communicated with their deity. When a shugenja cast this spell, they remained motionless and waited for a sign from their deity. Spirits and deities were sporadic with the omens and they were challenging to interpret correctly. The spell took a substantial physical and mental toll on the caster, and even the most powerful shugenja or wu jen was only able to cast this spell once a week. A caster was unable to cast this spell in the phlogiston.

Components
For a shugenja, only a verbal component was required. For a wu jen, both a verbal and a material component were required. During casting, the wu jen needed to burn small pieces of paper written with the four directions.

## Omen (Faerûnian spell)

Omen was a common divination spell that was available to numerous Faerûnian priests of various faiths.

Effects
This spell was used by a divine spellcaster to beseech their deity for advice regarding a binary choice that had been presented to them. When the spell was cast, there was a significant chance the priest would receive a symbolic, but definitive response either in the affirmative or negative. It was also possible they received no response whatsoever.

It should be noted that any divine "signs", or misleading coincidental occurrences, were up for interpretation by the priest.

Components
In addition to verbal and somatic components, omen required a single gemstone worth at least 100 gp, which was consumed during casting.

## Omberdawn Villa

Omberdawn villa was the familial estate of the Omberdawn family, as well as a magnificent public park located in the Cormanthor district of Myth Drannor. Following the destruction of the city, the grounds became a swamp that was known as Sullymarsh.

Description
The structure of Omberdawn villa comprised two multi-level structures, one built upon the ground and another  above at tree-level. The ground level building itself was three stories tall, with two subterranean levels.

Geography
Sullymarsh was connected to the lower level of the Dwarven Dungeons in the north-west area of the park. The pathway of winding walkways to the north let to the once-serene Glim-Gardens. Speculum Grounds laid to Sullymarsh's south-west.

King Pujik's ormyrr tribe resided by one of the spawn pools, an extension of the pool of radiance.

A massive patch of healing blueglow moss could be found in the north-east of the marsh.

Sullymarsh was one of the locations of Myth Drannor that had beautiful spires of the city's Orrery constructed. The Spinning Spire of Sullymarsh laid destroyed in the Weeping War and was inactive circa 1369 DR.

History
Architects of Myth Drannor used their engineering and magic skills to turn this marsh into a magnificent public park. The park surrounded a beautiful lake that was maintained via a complex system of waterways constructed by the dwarves of Myth Drannor and protected by water spirits, naiads. Intricately-carved bridges were constructed to allow the citizens of the city to cross the waterways.

By 1369 DR the nature and the failure of the city's waterways reverted Sullymarsh back to its natural state, a marsh.

## Omeluum

Omeluum was an illithid born with arcane powers that managed to escape the Grand Design of its designated elder brain. It managed to establish a life of independence, and became a member of the Society of Brilliance during its travels throughout the Underdark.

Personality
Despite its disagreeable experiences within the illithid colony it had once been a part of, Omeluum had an appreciation for the lore and history of its people. Omeluum had a curious mind along with a drive to make the world a better place for others beyond itself.

Abilities
Fitting for a resident of the Underdark, Omeluum was a skilled alchemist. Using a tongue of madness fungus and timmask spores, Omeluum could brew a moderately-poisonous potion that could bypass the effects of magical stasis.

Possessions
Omeluum owned a ring of psionic protection, an item that helped it evade the psionic influence of elder brains within the Underdark.

Relationships
Omeluum was an associate of Lenore De Hurst, a Mystran cleric and scholar that dwelled within the Arcane Tower. It was friends with the orog (or possibly hobgoblin) Blurg, who expressed concern for Omeluum's safety when it disappeared during its research into the altered tadpoles.

History
Unlike nearly all other illithids, Omeluum was born with sorcerous powers, granting it natural defenses against the psionic control the elder brain exerted over its colony. Omeluum resisted the elder brain's control and fled the colony before its disappearance could be found out.

For some time Omeluum traveled with a lich, bringing the undead spellcaster souls in exchange for brains to feed upon. Eventually their paths in life parted, and it became a founding member of the Society of Brilliance alongside Blurg, Grazilaxx, Sloopidoop, Y, and Skriss.

As of the, Omeluum was studying alongside Blurg at the Ebonlake Grotto. It sought an alternative to brains as a food source for its people, though its research was progressing slowly. They spoke with several adventurers who had been afflicted by tadpoles and bore a strange artifact. Omeluum was shocked to find that their tadpoles had been magically altered. It offered a number of different types of aid to the adventurers, though to no avail. In a comment to Blurg, Omeluum was overheard expressing astonishment and pondering the idea that its species could possibly be evolving.

Omeluum and Blurg later returned to Baldur's Gate. Intrigued by their encounter with the adventurers, Omeluum began deeper research into the altered tadpoles and eventually caught the attention of the Cult of the Absolute. Detained by Enver Gortash, Omeluum was brought as a prisoner to the Iron Throne headquarters, an underwater prison. Omeluum was one of several prisoners that might have been freed by the same adventurers bearing the altered tadpoles it had met in the Underdark, though Omeluum encouraged saving others before itself. After this, if Omeluum survived, it thanked the adventurers and returned to the Society. Alternatively, Omeluum may have been murdered by Ptaris, a githyanki hatched from an egg acquired by Lady Esther, whose development was magically accelerated as part of an experiment run by the Society.

## Oman giantkiller

Oman giantkillers were fighters and rangers from the Moonshae Isles who specialized in fighting giants. The faction arose during the 15 century DR following the fall of Iron Keep and Oman's Isle to fomorians.

Activities
As giant-killers, they were trained to go toe-to-toe with heavy-hitting enemies, and relished standing at the very front of the frontlines against such foes. This did not mean they were reckless, rather that they were willing to take calculated risks against large creatures. They kept their oversized enemies' attention confounded and distracted until the chance to strike a killing blow presented itself.

Culture
Most Oman giantkillers were young Moonshavian adventurers. They were trained in specialized tactics for slaying giants and giant-kin by a Northlander clan on the island of Norland. This clan devised and taught the craft of giantslaying in hopes of one day liberating Oman's Isle from the fomorians of Vor Thomil, who had overrun the island's once proud Northlander kingdom in the . They mostly trained Northlanders who shared their dream of reclaiming the island, although they also shared their techniques with outsiders who promised to aid them in their goal. Even so, some of their students identified that their new skills were useful against many large monsters rather than just giants, and set out to pursue their own ends.

In true Northlander tradition, skill at sailing and knowledge about the sea were expected of an Oman giantkiller.

Prerequisites
In general, giantkillers were expected to be natives of the Moonshae Isles. Prospects were required to have at least some experience as a fighter and had to have mastered the hunter's quarry ability utilized by rangers.

Abilities
The tactics of the Oman giantkillers were useful for slaying any monster that towered over them. They were most dangerous in the thick of the action, and were tenacious and persistent in pushing through injuries and pursuing their quarry. More experienced giantkillers learned to refocus their battle fury to allow them to assault their foes with surprising vigor.

A favored maneuver was the "toss and close", in which the giantkiller hurled a weapon at their target, rapidly closed the distance, and retrieved their weapon for a follow-up melee strike. Another maneuver was to use two weapons to open a gaping wound in their enemy at a moment when any allies could strike at this same injury for a devastating coordinated attack.
