## Ayala Windspear

Ayala Windspear was an elf Harper of the late 14 century DR who saved Drogan Droganson's life after the kobold attack on Hilltop.

Description
Ayala was a slim elf, with long dirty-blonde hair, often put into a ponytail.

Personality
A deeply caring person, Ayala was very loyal to the Harpers. She knew a great deal about herbs, toxins, and poisons and used her knowledge to help and heal others.  She was a keen healer and herbalist and worshiped the deity Mystra.

Relationships
Ayala was a fellow Harper agent of Drogan and healed him back to health after he was gravely poisoned.

History
In the, Ayala was passing through the Silver Marches to note any strange occurrences for the Harpers when she tracked some kobolds. She knew something was wrong when the kobolds arrived in Hilltop, especially as these kobolds had traveled so far from their original home in the Nether Mountains.

With the knowledge that magical artifacts were being held in Hilltop, Ayala traveled quickly to Drogan but was too late to stop the attack. Though originally looked upon with suspicion by two of Drogan's apprentices, Xanos Messarmos and Dorna Trapspringer, Ayala earned their trust and worked alongside them and the one known as Drogan Droganson's pupil in efforts to protect Hilltop. Mischa Waymeet was originally trusting of Ayala and stayed behind at Drogan's school to help her with healing Drogan and protecting the school from any further attacks.

Ayala attempted to identify the poison and heal Drogan but it resisted and was later identified as a magically altered form of snake venom. Further healing required tressym tongue, charcoal, and helmthorn berries, which were collected further in town by Drogan's oldest apprentice. Ayala eventually managed to heal Drogan back to full health. She also provided detailed information about the stolen artifacts to Drogan's apprentices, which led to their recovery.

## Ayrorch

The Ayrorch or Council of Twelve, was the ruling council of the island-nation of Lantan, based in Sambar.

Activities
The council consisted of twelve members serving for life, chosen by the council itself. The council elected an Ayrar to lead and speak for the council, and a Lantar to represent the nation abroad. The rest of the members were known as the Santar (ancient Lantanese for "others").

The Ayrorch was served by the elite agent organization known as the Hands of Gond.

## Ayoi

Ayoi was one of the many isolated villages of the Fochu Peninsula in Wa. Its people made their living primarily on fishing.

Description
Most of the structures within the village were simple peasant shacks known as minka, built from wood with latticed walls and straw-thatched roofs. Apart from these poor residences were small barns, warehouses, and shops.

The people of Ayoi were much more welcoming of strangers than other settlements of the peninsula,  perhaps because its educated shoya was so keen on talking with anyone with more intelligence than the simple peasants living there.

The village had a population of about 410 individuals after .

Geography
Ayoi was located on the southwestern tip of the island of Tsukishima, on the south coast near the Momoben Forest.

Government
Villages in Wa were run by an administrator known as a shoya, typically a minor samurai, and Ayoi was no different in this respect. The shoya could call upon a local militia if needed. The shoya of Ayoi after  was Tamura Tenryu.

Trade
Like the other tiny settlements of the region, Ayoi had little contact with the other villages, much less with the rest of Kara-Tur or regions beyond. The people were fishers, but there is no evidence that they traded in fish.

The cost to spend the night in one of the small minkas was 3 fen. The village did not produce anything more expensive than 1 yuan to sell.

Defenses
About 41 citizens of Ayoi served in its militia. These persons were only armed with simple clubs or spears.

Religion
Like most of the villages of the Peninsula, the people of Ayoi were followers of the Path of Enlightenment. Unlike the other settlements, however, they did not demand a test of faith from strangers. According to Shigeruchan, the religious leader of nearby Gugedo, the people of Ayoi had slighted the Path and were in need of spiritual cleansing.

History
Sometime after Wa Year 1770 (1352 DR), deposed coiled dragon Za-Jikku began murdering citizens of the many villages of the Fochu Peninsula, transforming them into magical butterflies, the breath of which would create yun ch'i, the vapor of death, which he would use to live forever.

## Ayakar

Ayakar, nicknamed the Carnelian Star Among Opals, was coastal trading town in Turmish that was famous for its widely-popular bazaar. It received ships from far and wide across the diverse nation.

Description
The town seemed precariously built as its famous bazaar jutted of the edge of a cliff face, but was in fact quite structurally sound. It comprised nine large domed structures built over spacious and beauteous atriums. The residential area of town was situated inland from the coast line.

The scent of seawater permeated the air around Ayakar all year round, and a thing layer of salt.

Culture
The Ayakaran people primarily venerated the goddess Selûne, as she held influence over the tides. They tended to speak in rather long prose, preferring poetic language and quirky idioms.

Many locals carried on their person, long ornamental blades.

A favored meal often given as a gesture of welcome was a loaf of sourdough bread served with a small bowl of salt, for which the town was famous.

Geography
The town was situated in northern Turmish on a cliff overlooking the Sea of Fallen Stars.

Trade
Ayakar's bazaar attracted shoppers from far and wide. Within its nine domes were several public services buildings, along with numerous businesses, including: its renowned bakery, a bustling tea house, an angler's shop for all types of fishers, a sailmaker's establishment, the spice market, and a shop that specialized in traveler's gear.

The town housed several fermentation vats, which collected naturally-formed yeast from the sea, to be used in producing famous Ayakaran sourdough bread.

Fishing was also a robust industry in Ayakar, as the nearby bay was teeming way many unique species of fish.

Notable Locations
In addition to its notable businesses and trading space, Ayakar housed a public meeting space, a hospital run by clerics of Selûne, and public bathing houses.

Notable Inhabitants
 Khynree, the local wizard that was well-acquainted with the underwater storm giant fortress of Maelstrom.
 Mezhar, a djinn that took the appearance of a Turmisian man and worked as an agent for the Deryan family of Alaghôn.
 Thessalia, the half-sea elf that championed for Turmish against the forces of Thay.

## Aylin

Dame Aylin was an aasimar and the daughter of the goddess Selûne, who was imprisoned within the Shadowfell by General Ketheric Thorm in the 14 and 15 centuries DR.

Description

She had pale skin that was uniquely blemished, appearing as cracked marble, and light blonde hair she kept sheared on one side and long, brushed back along the other. She had white, feathered wings that granted her flight.

Personality
Aylin was a strong-willed, resilient and vengeful person. If she were ever wronged, Aylin would vow the full revenge with the full wrath of the heavens upon her foes. She was also quite direct, particularly about her feelings, even openly discussing her sexual attraction to Isobel in front of new friends. It was possible Aylin's vengeful, brash personality was the result of the trauma from a century of suffering as Ketheric Thorm's source of immortality, being repeatedly murdered by Sharrans in their initiation rite and the horrific experiments and torture Balthazar subjected her to.

Relationships

Aylin served her divine mother Selûne as a paladin.

Some time during the late 14 century DR, Aylin fell in love with Isobel Thorm, a devoted follower of Selûne. Isobel's father, Ketheric, assumed the love wasn't genuine but was wrong; even after over one hundred years being tortured and repeatedly killed and resurrected in the Shadowfell, Isobel's death was still a painful memory for Aylin. She also referred to Isobel as "her consort" and "my mate, most high" while praying to Selûne, giving the relationship a formal affirmation.

Her relationship with Ketheric himself was more complicated. He didn't approve of Aylin being intimate with his daughter, likely due to the difference in lifespan and rank.  Yet she was the daughter of Ketheric's patron deity, so the two kept a respectful distance from each other. When Isobel died, they both shared the pain of her loss, but did not bond over the experience. Any good will Aylin may have had towards Ketheric ended when he tricked her into the soul cage in Shadowfell. His betrayal of Selûne, especially given he abandoned the deity's service in favor of Shar, deceiving her and the pain and abuses she suffered there gave Aylin a deadly, burning hatred of Ketheric.

History

Sometime during the late 14 century DR, Aylin met Isobel Thorm, a devoted follower of Selûne.  The two fell in love at first sight, though it was a while after when Isobel learned that Aylin returned her feelings.  Isobel's father, Ketheric, assumed the love wasn't genuine, but didn't interfere in the relationship.  When Isobel was murdered by unknown assailants, Aylin grieved deeply.
During the, Ketheric Thorm and his advisor Balthazar tricked and abducted Dame Aylin with a tale that someone needed her help.  After being captured, Aylin was imprisoned within a soul cage in the Shadowfell for his then-goddess Shar. In return, Ketheric would be spared the loss and pain that accompanied the death of his daughter Isobel, who happened to be Aylin's lover.  After Aylin was imprisoned behind a gate underneath the Gauntlet of Shar, the immortal strength of Aylin's soul was transferred to Ketheric by unholy necromantic magic, granting the general eternal life.

Aylin was subjected to a century of torture and brutal experiments by Balthazar, including having her tongue removed at least once. She was also repeatedly slain by the goddess' would-be Dark Justiciars time and time again, only to return trapped within her cage. During this time, Dame Aylin came to be known as the Nightsong. Isobel never forgave her father for the horrors he subjected Aylin to.

In the, the wizard Lorroakan of Baldur's Gate attempted to secure the Nightsong for himself. He posted a bounty offering much riches for her retrieval. At least one adventuring party, led by Aradin, took the mage up on his offer.
