## Runehammer

A runehammer was a rare, magical +1 warhammer.

Description
Runehammers were typically constructed of darksteel. A large rune was marked on their head, indicating the spell it carried and giving the item its name.

Powers
Runehammers had the ability to cast a single spell once per day, but the spell was the same each time, stored in the hammer. There were three common types:
 Alhalbrin, which carried the spell heat metal
 Faerindyl, which carried the spell burning hands
 Thundaril, which carried the spell polymorph other, often into the form of either a snail or toad.

Some sources claimed that these spells could be cast at least six times per day. Each time a runehammer's power was used there was a very small chance of Moradin willing it to vanish to another plane, potentially taking along whoever was currently wielding it.

Due to being made of darksteel, all were known to impart a little electricity upon impact.

Creation
Forging a runehammer required the spell that was stored in the weapon.

Cost
Depending on the type of spell stored, the hammer could be worth between 11,000 and 18,000 gold pieces.

## Rushing Hills

The Rushing Hills was a series of rocky outcroppings that lay southeast of the city of Lheshayl in the Shining Plains in the Vilhon Reach.

Geography
The Rushing Hills were a rough and rocky range, consisting of natural outcroppings of rock. These hindered planting in the area.

Inhabitants
The Krakk't tribe of thri-kreen made their home here, as well as in the plains to the southwest. The tribe only numbered about fifty or so members and was nomadic. The tribe was further split into three distinct packs, for which reason outsiders called them "tri-kreen". They hunted rabbits, Plainsteeds, and other herd animals that entered their territory. They considered horse a delicacy. They rarely attacked travelers in the area, but the chances of an attack rose if there were elves in the group (as elf-meat was also a delicacy for thri-kreen).

The fire drakes of the Deepwing Mountains hunted for food in the Rushing Hills, and occasionally attacked the thri-kreen there.

History
Around 1360 DR, the folk of Lheshayl decided to try to drive the thri-kreen from the hills. They made sure no animals ever wandered into the tribes' hunting area. However, this tactic only drove the thri-kreen farther north and they attacked travelers on the road. This incident established the semi-official policy of driving some animals into the hills to keep the thri-kreen fed, so they did not prey on merchants journeying to Lheshayl.

Rumors
Liches and other undead were said to live in the hills, and some in Lheshayl said the undead controlled the thri-kreen, but these tales were unconfirmed.

## Ruldarr

Ruldarr was a shopkeeper and the owner of Ruldarr's Pipes, Locks, Tobacco, and Fine Furniture in the settlement of Bargewright Inn in the Savage Frontier in the mid-to-late 14 century DR.

Description
Ruldarr had a well-kept appearance with a small pointed beard and an oiled mustache.

Personality
He was a man of grace and refined manners, with a subtle humor. He was also a keen gardener and had a taste for exotic wines, which he often shared with guests. Among them was the bitter and salted black wine of Phelzol, a distinctly acquired taste that he had clearly acquired.

Possessions
His house stood at the southeastern edge of the upper part of the settlement, at the end of the street called the Black and standing beside Haeleth's house and opposite The Bargewright Inn itself.

His plants took up much of the room in his house and shop, many of them hanging plants in large, levitating pots. They rose and moved at Ruldarr's direction, or simply drifted about on their own, with leaves and roots dangling in the air. These plants seemed to enjoy Ruldarr's wines as well, and if a guest poured their unwanted wine into one, they would find several thirsty potplants hovering around wanting some too.

## Ruk

Ruk was the progenitor of the hill giant race.

Description
As chief of the hill giant kingdom of Ostoria, Ruk wore a golden crown and bejeweled rings. His weapon was an ebony club.

Relationships
Ruk was one of Annam All-Father's many terrestrial sons through his wife Othea. His brothers were Lanaxis, Vilmos, Nicias, Masud, Ottar, Obadai, Arno and Julian, and Hartkiller, who was the last born. He also had four terrestrial half-brothers through his mother, including Dunmore, the father of the voadkyn. He had many divine half-siblings through his father, including Stronmaus, Hiatea, Grolantor, Iallanis, Memnor, Skoraeus, and most likely Surtr and Thrym as well.

Ruk had at least one son, who was named Morg.

History
Like his other non-divine brothers, Ruk was born sometime around . When all of the siblings were old enough, Annam granted each of them an inheritance in the great giant empire of Ostoria. Chief Ruk laid claim to the rolling hills of Faerûn, and his seed became the hill giants of later millennia.

Thousands of years later, Annam discovered that Othea, Ruk's mother, had not been faithful, having had an affair with Ulutiu. After Ulutiu's amulet caused the Great Glacier and the Endless Ice Sea to form, Ruk and his brothers soon discovered a way to halt the amulet's magic; however, they were forbidden from doing so by their mother. Lanaxis, Ruk's eldest brother and the leader of all of them, summoned them together for a meeting in Voninheim. There, Lanaxis planned to murder his mother by poisoning the waters of the Well of Health, but he accidentally poisoned not only Othea but also Ruk and all of his other brothers except Arno and Julian and Dunmore.

Ruk was buried in a drumlin outside of Voninheim. There his body lay with those of his murdered brothers for about 3000 years until 1369 DR, when the firbolg Tavis Burdun unveiled the Twilight Vale and challenged Lanaxis. Lanaxis used powerful magic to reanimate Ruk's corpse as a zombie, and he did the same with all of the other brothers. The undead giant kings attacked Tavis with the full fury of their magics, but Tavis and his companions were protected by Annam's axe Sky Cleaver. Tavis then used the magic of Sky Cleaver to reveal to them, even in their undeath, that Lanaxis, their older brother, had betrayed them. Ruk and the other dead giants turned and charged at Lanaxis instead. Lanaxis created a magic shield to protect the portico from their onslaught. Nicias, the cloud giant, was able to shatter the shield with his morning star, but then Lanaxis killed each of them a second time with his sword. Ruk was the second to be slain. Lanaxis cut his body completely in half and sent it crashing through one of the walls of Voninheim.

## Runecaster

A runecaster was a divine spellcaster who knew of the particular advantages of rune magic. Runecasters used magic and a special alphabet to create runes, which were symbols that possess magical effects that were both powerful and capable of being used multiple times. Although rune magic was originally created by the dwarves and giants millennia ago, its practice eventually became available to other races. The Thunder Blessing in particular spread this form of magic quite effectively as young dwarves brought their particular skills out into the wider world.

Runecasters used their runes generally for defensive purposes such as preventing unwanted intruders from entering a particular corridor, protecting a relic, or otherwise deter others from undesirable actions. Applications included protecting temples, trapping poacher trails, and enhancing the capabilities of an otherwise understrength defensive emplacement.

The actual method to activate a rune depended on the will of the runecaster. Methods of activation included permanent activation, activation upon being touched, and activation upon being read. The last activation method was particularly noteworthy due to the fact that few people considered the act of reading to be of any negative consequence. This activation method could be further restricted if the runecaster wished. A particular race, physical appearance, or alignment could all be determining factors in a rune's activation. A password or phrase could also be included to prevent accidental activation of a rune.

Culture
Due to their cultural customs giants rarely shared the secrets of their rune magic with outsiders, though it did occur on occasion. All giants had some inherent affinity for rune magic, with the exception of Voadkyn and hill giants. The former were ignorant of the practice due to having branched away from giant society so quickly, while the latter were simply not intelligent enough to properly wield it.

Abilities
Runecasting A runecaster could create a rune, which possesses a predetermined magical effect. As the runecaster became more experienced, he or she could create a rune that could be triggered multiple times.
Maximize Rune A runecaster could create a rune that had the Maximize Spell effect on it.
Runechant A runecaster could sketch a rune symbol in the air to enhance a divine spell he was casting.

History
Following the Thousand Year War, the god Annam All-Father quested for some way to bolster the might of the giants. Around a thousand years later he won the secrets of rune magic in Mechanus by beating a high-ranking modron in a game of lots. Following this, the first runecasters in the world were the giant progenitors Nicias and Vilmos, who over the course of hundreds of years spread the secret of this magic to apprentices.

## Ruins of Adventure

Ruins of Adventure is an adventure for AD&D 1 edition that was set in the Forgotten Realms.

Description
Ruins of Adventure contains four short Forgotten Realms adventure scenarios which are connected and adapted from the Pool of Radiance computer game, and take place in the devastated town of Phlan. There are numerous pre-generated characters in this book. Monsters each have their own stats prepared and there are quite a few non-player characters.

Ruins of Adventure was written by James Ward, David "Zeb" Cook, Steve Winter, and Mike Breault, with a cover by Clyde Caldwell, and was published by TSR in 1988 as a 96-page book.

Summary
The adventurers are hired to remove evil forces from Phlan, presumably by killing them. They hear rumor of a Boss controlling them and seek him out. This Boss proves to be a worthy adversary, but in the end the adventurers defeat him.

## Ruby Rod of Asmodeus

The Ruby Rod of Asmodeus was an artifact.

Description
As the name implied, the Ruby Rods owned by the avatars and aspects looked like a big solid ruby that was formed into the shape of a rod. Those owned by avatars or powerful aspects were worth more than a million gp in material value alone, while weak aspects' were worth less, meaning upwards from 50,000 gp. However, the original owned by the Asmodeus' main body was made from a shard of evil and merely tipped with a ruby.

These  rods had an opaline tip looking like a clawed fist clutching a skull and a jagged bottom that looked like the rod was actually part of something bigger and snapped off from that bigger something. They were covered with good deities' and celestials' names, all spelled in reverse and sullied with black blood.

These rods gave off a soft red light. Upon shooting energy blasts, the rods gave off beams of light in every direction.

Powers
One of the easiest ways to use a Ruby Rod of Asmodeus in combat was to use it as a bludgeon. Used like that, a Ruby Rod worked like a +6 unholy greatclub; however, versions that worked like a +2 unholy greatclub or +1 morningstar owned by weak aspects were also known. Whatever the strength of the Ruby Rod when used as a bludgeon, they did not damage the victim in physical ways but also in magical ways, as though affecting the body with a inflict critical wounds, or inflict light wounds in the +1 morningstar version. Having Asmodeus' permission to wield a Ruby Rod allowed a person to wield it without getting hurt.

As mentioned above, Ruby Rods could shoot energy beams. To be more precise, there were three versions—a 150-foot (45-meter) beam of electricity, a beam of acid that was the same length, or a 75-foot (23-meters) cone of intense cold. The abilities could be used as often as he wanted by Asmodeus but only once per day by other people.

The Ruby Rod had three protective abilities. One forced people to bow down before the wielder, thereby making it impossible for them to attack the wielder. The second one was about creating a wall of force around the wielder as well as a 50-foot (15-meter) radius antimagic field that magically repaired the wielder, first by making away with detrimental enchantment effects, then with mundane detriments like lost body parts that grow back, diseases, and poison-induced effects, and at last with a full recovery of the wielder's physical health and magical energy. The last ability could be activated intentionally but did so automatically whenever the wielder fell victim to an enchantment effect. The abilities could be used as often as he wanted by Asmodeus, but only once per day by other people. Third, it could protect the wielder from spells by absorbing them into the rod. However, this use of the rod was not unlimited.

History
The original Ruby Rod of Asmodeus was created by Asmodeus from a sliver of the shard of evil – said to be the artifact of unfathomable evil which created the Abyss – which he used to kill He Who Was, his superior at the time. This was during the Dawn War. The crafting of the rod required a piece of the shard of evil and a huge ruby to be soaked in the blood of a thousand sacrificed mortals, quenched in Tiamat's acidic saliva, and polished with 777 angel tears. Given that it required Tiamat's drool, the time frame during which the rod was crafted was between Tiamat's birth, or Io's death at the hands of Erek-Hus, and He Who Was' death at the hand of Asmodeus who used the Ruby Rod for the deed.

According to the play The Trial of Asmodeus, which was allegedly based on facts, Asmodeus was obliged to always carry the Ruby Rod of Asmodeus by Primus. The rod would act both as a symbol for him and his devils' right to forge pacts with mortals and as a punishment device for all devils that do not respect the strictures of their bargains.

Reputation
Every avatar of Asmodeus carried a Ruby Rod of Asmodeus as a symbol of the office Asmodeus held, which was the one as overlord of Baator and as the one to whom all devils had to obey.

Notable Owners
All avatars of Asmodeus owned a Ruby Rod of Asmodeus, as did aspects of his—at least they owned some downgraded version of it.

Sometimes, Asmodeus gave a Ruby Rod to somebody else. However, it was never known if what he gave was a replica or the genuine thing.

## Ruby

Ruby was a variety of corundum that was a popular and plentiful jewel and had many magical uses in addition to its appeal as a sparkling gemstone. It was cherished by worshipers of many gods, including Callarduran Smoothhands, Erevan Ilesere, Gorm Gulthyn, Haela Brightaxe, and Labelas Enoreth.

Description
Rubies came in a variety of colors, from colorless through shades of red to deep crimson. The darker the shade and the fewer the inclusions and impurities, the more valuable was the gem. A typical specimen had a base value of 5,000 gp. About one in every hundred rubies was a star ruby.

Rubies were believed to represent love and revenge.

Ancient gem magic associated rubies with the healing powers of earth, which was the source of ruby golems' ability to regenerate and their affinity for travelling underground.

Powers

Superstitions around the Sea of Fallen Stars held that rubies bestowed good luck on their owners, but magical uses were much more reliable. Crushed to a powder and used in the enchantment, rubies greatly enhanced personal magic items that increased skill, ability, resistance, or luck. Ruby powder was also used to create the magic ink for scribing divine spells of the elemental and sun spheres, and arcane spells of the abjuration, alteration, or evocation schools. The best time of day to use powdered ruby was "the time of the summer sunset."According to Elminster, this was around 5 o'clock in the evening.

Facet cut and polished rubies were required in the crafting of a helm of brilliance and were the gem of choice in decorating items that produced healing magic. With the proper incantations, rubies could ward against natural and magical lightning and earthquakes. Extremely rarely, a ruby might contain a creature that could be released from the stone by delicately shattering it. Such creatures were usually unknown in Faerûn and grew rapidly upon release.

A crushed ruby worth at least 1,000 gp was required to cast the extremely powerful necromatic spell soul shift.

Rubies were one of the preferred gems for the psionic circuitry in the inventions of the Creative Creed.

Supposedly, powdered ruby was an ingredient in the vision-granting deep gnomish wine Gogondy.

Reputation
Rubies were considered prized gems among the drow. They especially liked the darker ones.

Notable Rubies
 Kossuth ruby was a magical gem that held a spark of elemental fire.
 The Wyvern Crown of Cormyr was studded with jewels, including rubies.

Notable Users of Rubies
 Members of the knightly order, the Keepers of the Mystic Flame, were awarded for defeating beings that posed a magical threat to all of the Realms.
 Sune, the goddess of love was known to wear rubies in her hair.

## Rulers' Blade

The Ruler's Blade, also known as the Great King's Sword or ar'cor'kerym in Elven, and sometimes referred to as the Crownblade,) was one of three elfblades created in ancient Cormanthyr to help the elves establish a ruling family. This particular blade was used to name the Coronal, the monarchical ruler of the kingdom of Cormanthyr and leader of the elves.

History
The Ruler's Blade was created by Elven High Magic alongside its mates, the Warblade and the Artblade in, at the behest of the ill Coronal of Jhyrennstar, Oacenth, who desired the magical swords to determine the rulers of the united tribes and new nation of Cormanthyr. Since it was drawn by the first Coronal Kahvoerm Irithyl, it never left the side of the Coronals.

In the, after the passing of Coronal Eltargrim Irithyl, the Ruler's Blade hovered atop the Rule Tower awaiting a claiming ceremony during the five years of mourning instituted by the Coronal's niece and lone heir, Aravae Irithyl.  Aravae was ultimately murdered three years after her uncle died, and in the, the first open Claiming Ceremony begun in Myth Drannor. Every noble house sent representatives who each in turn were destroyed after being proven unworthy. Days passed as more and more elves tested their might, but none proved worthy. Soon, some non-elves attempted to draw the blade, which caused civil strife and combat before the Rule Tower. In the end, the Srinshee drew the blade and was proven worthy. However, saddened at the moral failings of the elves, she took the blade and her High Mages and departed from the mortal realm to Arvandor until a time when she deemed the elves worthy of the gift of the Ruler's Blade and a Coronal who couldunite the elves as Oacenth wished returned to claim it.

In the, the Srinshee returned at last to Myth Drannor and offered the Ruler's Blade to Ilsevele Miritar in recognition of her wise rulership and involvement in the realm's refounding. Ilsevele accepted the blade and became the first Coronal in over seven centuries.

Blade-Rite
To become Coronal of the elves, an heir must pass the test of Blade-rite, an ancient ritual which culminated in the drawing of the Ruler's Blade. Only one such elf failed to draw the blade; this was Divisav Irithyl, a greedy son of the first Coronal, Kahvoerm Irithyl.

A successful candidate to wield the Ruler's Blade should be lawful good and have the interests of Cormanthyr at heart. They should be socially adept and able to handle delicate negotiations with diplomacy.

Failure of even one of the three tests, as part of Blade-rite, saw the wielder consumed in arcane fire within a minute, killing them instantly and leaving no trace.

Description
The blade appeared as if it was carved from stone from afar, though in reality it was simply raw iron that was pitted and craggy. An elven crown rune was stamped just above the quillons. Golden light crackled like lightning up and down the blade, casting its light. Two black selu'kiira were bound at each end of the quillons, while a red one was on the pommel.

Powers
The Ruler's Blade, like others of its type, shared some common characteristics with its kin elfblades, but also had its own special powers.

With a command word, the wielder of the blade was able to heal other as with the heal spell three times per day and sense deceptions as with detect lie or discern lies at will.

The blade's owner was also granted the knowledge of three high mages whose essences were imbued in the gems within. The knowledge of the three high mages manifested as greatly improved knowledge of the Art and spell-craft, and a general improvement in all other branches of knowledge.

## Rundeen

The Rundeen, originally meaning the Safe Coin and also known as the Tradesmen or the Trader's Friend, was an expansive and dominating mercantile, criminal, and slaving consortium and secret society that operated in Calimshan, Tashalar, the Chultan Peninsula, and the southern Sword Coast.

Organization
Structure
The Rundeen had a relatively loose and informal hierarchy, but had a clear, layered structure. These layers operated independently and even unaware of the others, with only the leadership grasping the full picture of the organization.

The leaders of the Rundeen were known as the Yrshelem, an Alzhedo word meaning 'coin collector(s).' They were the secret inner circle of the organization, never identified nor directly observed and directing affairs covertly. The Yrshelem were in turn divided into two tiers; there were the five Grand Yrshelem and under them were the fifteen First Yrshelem. Overall, the Yrshelem operated as an oligarchy. The members of the Grand Yrshelem each had an area of specialty and authority, controlling a particular aspect of Rundeen business—caravans, raw goods, money-changing and loans, shipping, and slavery—but evenly divided both the responsibilities and the profits, aiding one another when it came necessary. Although Calishite society had traditionally sexist attitudes against women outside the home, let alone in positions of power, the Grand Yrshelem had included many female members over the centuries. Known members of the Grand Yrshelem circa 1370 DR were:
 The Grand Yrshelem of Ships: Zuras el Ireen
 The Grand Yrshelem of Caravans: Rafi al Shuree
 The Grand Yrshelem of Slaves: Saibhon el Bhiras

Next, the members of the First Yrshelem mainly handled the organization and management of the Rundeen's assets, holdings, personnel, and finances. Each of the Grand Yrshelem was served by a triad of First Yrshelem: the first to handle money, the second personnel, and the third physical assets and costs, divided within their superior's area of authority. Although they usually operated as covertly as the Grand Yrshelem, their identities were known to some of the richer and more influential Tradesmen of lower levels. The First Yrshelem were all once lower-level Tradesmen themselves, whether paid members or employees, who'd been promoted to the inner circle either through hard work and/or by willingly abandoning their independence for the privilege and opportunity to make higher profits. Again, there were many female First Yrshelem over the organization's history; much fewer than in the Grand Yrshelem but still many more than was common in Calishite society. A known First Yrshelem circa 1372 DR was Fahd yn Sarsor al Nisr.

Serving the First Yrshelem was a group known as the Mitalibbar, meaning 'wealth watchers.' Each mitalib—a word meaning 'watcher'—was an agent tasked with collecting membership fees, making payments, and communicating with merchants, as well as looking out for rivals, creditors, and enemies like the Harpers or Shadow Thieves, in his or her city, for which tasks they would employ agents of their own. Broadly, they were the Rundeen's collection agency. While the Yrshelem directed the overall course of the Rundeen through the Mitalibbar, they permitted them autonomy in their day-to-day affairs. Within themselves, the Mitalibbar had ranks named for and in order of importance of the value of old coins from at first ancient Tashalar and later the Shoon Imperium—the highest ranking mitalib were called nirdeen (singular: nirdey; platinum coins), those of the next rank were tardeen (singular: tardey; electrum coins), and the those of the lowest rank were cardeen (singular: cardey; copper coins). Each nirdeen served one of the First Yrshelem triads according to their financial power in a given city, though some nirdeen were superior to others and all would obey any member of the Yrshelem. All merchants and lesser members paid their fees to their local nirdey, who was often the highest-level Tradesman they knew of; they rarely even saw anyone higher. The Mitalibbar had cells in every city in the Rundeen's domain and a nirdey or tardey agent in every port or caravan city south of Lake Weng in Amn as of 1370 DR. In the cities of Calimshan, with their power and influence in the underworld, a nirdey had the effective status of a pasha, albeit with superiors of their own, and some Calishite pashas either secretly served the Mitalibbar or even were members of the Mitalibbar, which erased many distinctions between them. Known members of the Mitalibbar circa 1370 DR included:
 Husuf yn Harun el Halid, nirdey of the Emerald Ward of Calimport
 Jafar el Uabar, nirdey of Volothamp
 Daudon yn Saibh el Yakan, nirdey of Memnon

Below the Mitalibbar, but serving the First Yrshelem directly, were a variety of subsidiary organizations and operatives that supported Rundeen goals or enacted its commands. These included noble houses with historical alliances with the Rundeen, like the Vyndahla clan of Calimshan; mercenary and adventuring companies that worked as enforcers, private armies, and treasure hunters such as the Clenched Fist; top assassins like the Fist of Rundeen; pirate fleets including the Bloody Buccaneers, the Scimitar of Fire, and the Sea Reavers; and thieves groups like the Many Long Arms, as well as a host of other semi-independent enforcers, smugglers, and thieves.

Lastly, at the bottom of the pile came numerous regular Tradesmen, both individuals and their families. These folk were ordinary merchants, caravaneers, moneylenders, shipbuilders, and other business owners. They had no titles nor hardly any influence in the Rundeen. It would take many years of labor or significant profits for them to be promoted.

Recruitment
Being an exclusive organization, the Rundeen did not accept any common merchant. When considering new members, the Rundeen took an interest in merchants and organizations with links to an industry in which they specialized, such as shipping, caravaning, or trade, and who had businesses big enough to warrant their attention. For example, a small-time moneylender of Schamedar who had clients in neighboring Manshaka was of no interest, but one who reached across Calimshan to Memnon or even into Tethyr at Myratma had both the coin and the ambition to catch their eye. Thus, a prospective merchant must also be competent, cunning, efficient, and prosperous. When a worthy merchant satisfying all three measures was identified by an agent, information on them and their business was reported up  through the Mitalibbar and Yrshelem to the Grand Yrshelem who oversaw their particular trade. That Grand Yrshelem would decide whether to decline or offer membership. In the latter case, a Rundeen representative would contact the merchant to offer a contract and incentives to join, not threats and fearmongering. They did not need to. Those who rejected the offer were either approached again later until they did, or else treated as a rival and driven out of business, or even killed or disappeared if particularly opposed, to make way for an existing Rundeen member. After seeing the fate of their fellows, many businesses were quite careful when consider declining the Rundeen. Those who accepted were counted as business partners of the Rundeen.

In contrast, bandits, pirates, and other criminals hired by the Rundeen were required to demonstrate discipline, loyalty, restraint, and the ability to follow orders exactly, doing no more and no less than required. For example, if a pirate vessel was ordered to only pursue a rival merchant ship, it should not instead board and raid that ship, while one ordered to board a ship must not sink it. Laxity, recklessness, and carelessness would harm the Rundeen's reputation. Meanwhile, adventurers should not be over concerned about doing what was right, legal, or proper.

Benefits
Partners of the Rundeen enjoyed an array of benefits. First, their caravans received greater protection against bandits and their ships against pirates—often the Rundeen's own—discount rates on various services and products purchased from Rundeen merchants or their allies, preferential treatment in any port or city under Rundeen mercantile control, and more general benefits from being a fellow Tradesman. The reduced prices on wagons and ships alone was sufficient to make membership worthwhile for any enterprising merchant.

Rundeen control of markets brought greater benefits. For example, in Almraiven circa 1370, half of all shipbuilders (including Yulthaari Shipyards and the Zalath family) were either sponsored or owned by the Rundeen, which granted them cheaper rates on lumber and labor from Lapaliiya, the Tashalar, and the Shaar, while Rundeen ships were prioritized for construction, sale, and outfitting—even Calimshan's syl-pasha came second, but was usually unaware of it. Meanwhile, in both Almraiven and Schamedar, the masters of the coopers', farriers', moneylenders', saddlers', and shipwrights' guilds were all full members of the Rundeen, which paid their fees, taxes, and personal bribes to local governments on their behalf. This in turn established closer links with vizars who in granted Rundeen members best rates, business opportunities, and patronage.

Terms & Conditions
The requirements of membership in the Rundeen were simple, yet deceptive. First, a prospective member must sign a contract agreeing to pay dues worth 5% of their monthly earnings (either their own or their business's) to the Rundeen every sixmonth period. Instead of all being due at the start/end and middle of the year, the sixmonth payments were staggered for different members, so a handful were due for collection each month. The partner delivered their payment to their local mitalib, either the nirdey or a selected tardey,Cloak & Dagger p. 132 writes "his nirdey or to a designated nardey", mistyping either 'tardey' or 'cardey'. The former, higher-ranked position is presumed to be correct. and this collector counted, tallied, and distributed the coin as required by superiors. But these memberships dues were a quick and rocky road to ruin.

First, as a matter of courtesy, a partner should come to their mitalib at the appointed time with the proper amount—making the Tradesmen ask for their money was seen as rude. If the partner did not visit the mitalib, then a cardey came to them with a friendly reminder of their responsibilities. The partner must then visit the nirdey with their payment by dusk the next day; once this was done, the matter was resolved without disgrace. But if the partner failed to show in time, then a gang of enforcers came around to their home to break a few things as a warning. If they still failed to pay after a tenday, then the enforcers returned and this time cut off the partner's finger. Otherwise, if the paid amount was less than required, the partner was given only a tenday's grace in which to make up the shortfall.

In either case, the Yrshelem were notified the partner had defaulted and the partner was informed of their updated payment plan. Violating the contract by failing to pay the sixmonth dues in full was grounds for the Rundeen to now take 20% of the next sixmonth's total earnings. If the member failed to pay the full amount for another two sixmonths, that is, a full year, then the Rundeen automatically became half-owner and full partner in the business, taking 50% of gross earnings before expenses. Moreover, if this amount was not paid, then the Rundeen had the right to buy out the entire business for the value of the member's current debt and consider it settled.It is unclear if the Rundeen pay the member the amount owed or if they do not pay the member but consider the debt settled at the cost of the business: Empires of the Shining Sea p. 181 says they "consider the member's debt to represent payment in full" while Cloak & Dagger p. 129 says they have "the option of buying out the business for the amount owed by the original owner." Given their behavior to this point, it is unlikely they pay any money. Alternatively, the Rundeen could choose to take up to 70% of earnings and retain the former owner as an employed manager instead with a salary according to the business's revenue and their name still on deeds of ownership. The Rundeen actually preferred the latter option, for the continuity of business, stable front, and fewer suspicions raised. Those businesses that were bought out received a new Rundeen owner, were controlled directly by the leadership rather than another regular member, and were converted entirely for use by Tradesmen. They were typically renamed after current or obsolete coinage, but were otherwise not identifiably Rundeen operations. Examples included the Seven Gulders Caravan Coster of Yallasch (named for a Tethyrian gold piece), the Yulthaari Shipyards of Almraiven (after then yulthaar, an exotic platinum currency used in ancient Myth Drannor,), and the Gold Lion Wheelwrights of Saelmur (named for the Cormyrean gold piece).

If at any point the debtor ran away rather than pay up, then the Rundeen sent its enforcers to track them down. Their extensive connections and transport businesses made it hard to escape them for long; most were apprehended in only a few tendays. They had a grim fate: their tongues were cut out to make them mute and they were sold as slaves. In some cases, they need not bother: the spendthrift Pasha Saamark II of House Jaleh, having debts to the Rundeen, cut his guards' pay and was cut down by them in turn.

Membership

The Rundeen was a vast organization, with approximately 3,000 standard members and countless more people employed by them or otherwise involved.

They were largely merchants, pirates, bandits, and others, each a specialist in their respective areas and operating mostly independently, especially at the Rundeen's lower levels. As such, they shared little in the way or training or talents. Nevertheless, some skills were useful, especially to those canny enough to hone them. Caravaneers did well to practice tracking and orienteering, while wise Rundeen pirates learned matters of ship design, livery, heraldry, and appraisal to target the correct ships and cargos. Those who'd been in the Rundeen for several months acquired a sense of etiquette useful in identifying fellow Tradesmen and their superiors and not inadvertently slighting them.

Meanwhile, those in the higher, leadership positions no longer needed to do much work, especially physical work. They needed only employ their sharp business acumen and knowledge of trade to advance.

Activities
Goals & Strategy
The Tradesmen purported to stand for the common benefit of merchants everywhere who had to travel and brave dangers and for a fair share of trade, but, more than anything else, the Rundeen desired profit and a total monopoly on trade all around the Shining Sea and up the Sword Coast. Their focus was entirely on money and mercantile matters. They did not care about achieving political power—that was merely a byproduct of controlling trade and was too much effort besides. The Rundeen operated as a, by and large, legitimate business network, despite their domineering practices. They preferred to induce potential rivals to join them than to threaten them, and applied brute force against rivals and potentials threats only as necessary.  Moreover, in contrast to mercantile social groups that created trade alliances like the Knights of the Shield, the Rundeen were an independent organization with its own resources and finances: they controlled fleets, shipyards, costers, caravans, and associated businesses, and operated unseen in every area of trade in the South, and few but the leaders of great realms like Calimshan could check their power.

In contrast to other mercantile organizations of their kind, the Rundeen were disinterested in production and trade goods and instead focused on the trade itself: the routes, services, maintenance, ships, warehouses, and so on. They had their hands in the businesses and guilds of carters, coachmen, and dockworkers; the farriers and trainers of the horses that led caravans; the makers of harnesses and saddles for animals; both shipwrights and wagonwrights; the sailors and officers of trade ships; moneychangers and moneylenders; coopers who made barrels and joiners who made crates for transporting goods; the butchers, fishermen, and fishmongers who turned livestock and fish into meat for sale; and the salters and packers who preserved and prepared food for trade; and even folk who carved wood and ivory. What trade goods they did deal in directly were raw materials like wood, grains, spices, sugar, wool in its raw form, furs, and leather that was tanned but not yet worked. They also traded in slaves.

Thanks to their ruthless dominance and support and protection of members reducing hazards, Rundeen businesses were historically able to embark on riskier and longer-range trade expeditions and business ventures that would earn greater profits, as compared to regular trading costers. Moreover, they could reserve the longest, riskiest, and most profitable trade routes for themselves, and leave the local scraps to lesser merchants. In addition, they had contacts throughout Faerûn, which aided in finding markets in far-off lands. Thus, for example, they could arrange trade from Calimshan to as far away as Luiren in the Shining South or the ports around the Inner Sea, thereby accessing goods and markets that most other costers of the Shining Sea could not hope to reach, and do so regularly and with high profits. Rundeen caravans were protected by well-armed guards.

To support their efforts, the Rundeen dedicated a great deal of time and attention to simply monitoring the trade landscape: keeping an eye on the roads and their caravans to keep them safe; watching the seas for pirates and rival vessels; checking out markets for popular goods; identifying merchants and others that might make worthy members and partners; determining what cargos or important persons rivals were transporting; and investigating lesser Rundeen members for double-dealing. For example, the Brothers Humuz stone carvers of Calimport spent time working in wealthy clients' homes and reported what they saw and heard to their Rundeen masters every sixmonth. Despite this, they moved slowly, preferring to consider all aspects before deciding on a course. They would only act swiftly when needed to protect a partner from banditry or piracy, or to strike at a rival with their own banditry or piracy.

Naturally, many members venerated Waukeen, the Merchant's Friend, but any faith might be found amongst them.

Piracy & Banditry

Piracy was a traditional preferred tactic of the Rundeen, not merely to take treasure but as a way to sabotage rivals' businesses and reinforce their own control of sea trade. They employed bandits in much the same way on land against caravans. Against rival merchants who were either trying to crack into a Rundeen-monopolized market or who elected not to use Rundeen services, the pirates or bandits disrupted their shipping or caravans or scared them away, pushing them toward ruin. They could also steal trade secrets for the Rundeen and interfere with others' trade deals. The Three Scourges pirates would even steal cargoes, pose as the actual crew of the captured vessels, and brazenly deliver the goods themselves to take the profits for the Rundeen.

Through messages (which might be ordinary or via magic), the Rundeen would inform their employed pirates or bandits of the routes, destinations, and current positions of specific ships or caravans that were worth their effort. They would also give them precise instructions for the raid: whether or not to only harry or actually sink a ship; whether to damage, destroy, or steal cargo; and whether or not to harm personnel and passengers. Pirates might be ordered to pursue a vessel but not board it, or board it but not scuttle it. The Rundeen expected such missions to be carried out to the letter and never exceeded; otherwise, they punish the pirates. Should a ship escape the pirates, which may or may not be intended, the owners would be force to consider going elsewhere or joining the Rundeen.

Known Rundeen pirates were the Bloody Buccaneers, the Scimitar of Fire, the Sea Reavers, and the Three Scourges, Groups that engaged in banditry included the Clenched Fist.

Such piracy was previously commonplace on the Lake of Steam, but in the late 1360s DR it was only an occasional problem around the Nelanther Isles. Rumors of a connection between these pirates and the Rundeen had circulated for decades. Meanwhile, the Rundeen had long wielded their influence to prevent Tashalar, Calimshan, and Lapaliiya from sending their navies against the pirate haven of Narubel in Thindol.

Slavery
In the face of opposition from antislavery organizations, the Rundeen continued to engage in slavery as a sideline, even keeping the despicable practice going long after it would've been disrupted and abandoned for being unprofitable if nothing else. They made themselves the key link in the slave trade in the South, and brooked no opposition or competition to their dominance.

Rundeen slavers targeted coastal villages and inland communities in Chult, Tashalar, Thindol, and the Shaar, raiding these places and carrying away native peoples. From these places, they had total control over the flow of slaves into Calimshan and further north up the Sword Coast, and to Innarlith in the Land of the Lions, where they supplied the likes of the Red Wizards of Thay and the Zhentarim. They also traded native Calishites into slavery within Calimshan, and even sold to Underdark slavers in the area, but in both cases their control was only approximately 30% though still far outcompeted even the drow for volume of slave raids on the surface. Prominent Rundeen slaving companies included the Ice Chain and the Haedi family of Almraiven. Their slave trade was thriving around 1367 DR, but no longer a total monopoly as of 1370 DR.

Unsurprisingly, Rundeen slavers prided themselves on their professionalism and dismissed smaller competitors as "amateurs". A starved, sickly, or injured slave meant lower profits at auction and a dead one none at all, so they made sure to provide sufficient water, food, cold baths, clean-enough clothing, space to sleep in the hold, and otherwise took good care of their living cargo. Standing-room-only holds, beatings, whippings, mutilation, and rape were not reported in the Rundeen slave trade.

Ironically, the Rundeen were also the primary opponents of slavery—by others. To maintain their grip, they gave their pirates standing orders to attack rival slave ships from their targeted regions, making it too dangerous for independent slavers and even Calimshan's navy, el Nallojal, to conduct their own raids. They would even give tip-offs about rival slavers and their plans to known Harpers or Janessar who would eliminate the competition for them.

Mercenaries, Adventurers, & Assassins
Finally, the Rundeen either employed or sponsored a number of mercenary and adventuring companies, and even a band of assassins. They wielded them much like their pirates and bandits, to destroy independent bandits that had preyed on Rundeen caravans, to expose Harpers, and to eliminate other threats, and to otherwise enforce their will or simply loot treasure. Noted groups were the Clenched Fist mercenaries and the Fist of Rundeen assassins, as well as the Many Long Arms.

Bases of Operations
While Tashalar was the old seat of Rundeen rule,Empires of the Shining Sea p. 180 states that the Rundeen's "major seat of power lies south of the Chultan Peninsula", which might put them outside mainland Faerûn entirely. As their Tashalan origins are commonly stated in other sources, this is presumed to mean they are southeast along the peninsula, in Tashalar. much of their power was centered in Calimshan as of the late 1300s. The Yrshelem were based out of a variety of private residences in the cities of these two realms, including Calimport, Manshaka, Memnon, and Tashluta. Whenever they met in person, they chose a different one of these bases each time, to better keep their activities hidden and to throw off spies.

From here, the Tradesmen monopolized trade across much of southwestern Faerûn, especially the realms around the Shining Sea, namely Tashalar, Calimshan, and Chult. In Tashalar, they had a stranglehold on trade and indeed on the ruling merchant oligarchy and their defensive navy. In Calimshan circa 1370 DR, their control was reduced to only certain areas of the realm or to greater or lesser degrees, owing to the rise of Syl-Pasha Ralan el Pesarkhal causing them to lose Calimport and Almraiven. Nevertheless, either directly or via their members, they still controlled almost half, over 40%, of the shipping and trade across Calimshan in a majority of relevant industries, as well as the majority of ports and caravans on the northern coast of the Chultan Peninsula. And while they were yet strong in Tashalar and Chult, the fewer cities and settlements here made them less appealing to the Tradesmen, so they looked north across the sea, especially after they'd become entrenched in Calimshan. They also had members to the east in the Shaar. They were also making inroads into Amn, Tethyr, and further north, thanks to their links with the Knights of the Shield. They sought to control trade up to the Dragon's Neck Peninsula.

To a lesser degree, the Rundeen had agents distributed all over the Shining South, in Athkatla in Amn, in Lantan and Mintarn, Westgate on the Dragon Coast, and in every primary trading port in Faerûn where humans were predominant. Outside their core territory, these agents could not of course exert the Rundeen's desired control, but could acquire local goods as needed without undue difficulty.

Symbol

For their symbol, when they used one at all, the Rundeen favored a coin penetrated by a nail. This might be an actual object or it might be an image drawn or inscribed on a surface. This alluded to the meaning of their earliest name, the Safe Coin.

In addition, members identified and greeted one another by means of a secret gesture: crossing their wrists over their chests and then clenching their fists. (The Knights of the Shield had a similar custom, but slightly different: the Knights closed their fists before crossing their wrists, the Rundeen, after.) An informal variant was a simple clenched fist.

Possessions
Members wore no uniform or livery and had no standard equipment, nor any other consistent elements. The only thing they had in common was the superiority of their gear: high-quality tools, wagons, and even ships, sold to them at a discount by Rundeen merchants or Rundeen-owned businesses. Moreover, many of the Rundeen enjoyed showing off their riches, often adorning themselves in jewelry, except when they might want to avoid attention.

Similarly, Rundeen ships and caravans bore no badges or colors that might indicate them.

Relationships
Allies
When it came to allies, the Rundeen could afford to stand alone—they were big enough, powerful enough, and entrenched enough to be able to maintain their power and satisfy their own needs. Nevertheless, they established a few as needed.

The first, and most fraught, relationship was with the Knights of the Shield. They had much in common: both were secret societies focused on mercantile matters and they shared a similar greeting, but whereas the Knights were an exclusive social group that connected merchants for individual trade alliances, the Tradesmen were expansive and numerous and focused on enrichment of the organization. They also shared history: while some presumed the Rundeen were an ancient offshoot of the Knights, they were in fact separate organizations that had established a close alliance in the time of the Shoon Imperium in the 3 to 5 centuries DR. But with the empire's fall, both tried to occupy the resulting power vacuum and the Knights attempted a takeover of the Rundeen, and were rebuffed, ending the alliance. But greed forgives all grudges and they maintained links in later centuries, links each would try to exploit. In the late 14 century, they got interested in another alliance, with each seeing more advantages and opportunities than risks and threats. The Rundeen hoped to use the Knights to expand into Amn and further north, while the Knights sought to use the Tradesmen to regain influence and trade in Calimshan and the south, especially after Ralan el Pesarkhal's rise to power. However, the Knights expected they could move up in the Rundeen again and reacquire the influential positions they'd held in the days of Shoon. While the Calishites saw no issue with divided loyalties and goals, the Rundeen were a little put-off by it. Nevertheless, by 1373 DR they had reestablished their partnership, helping the Rundeen retain their grip on Calishite shipping. Several figures were involved in this new closeness: Pasha Syan al Derak of Teshburl was a key member of both organizations who oversaw an operation of reworking stolen gems into untraceable new jewelry, with both Knights and Tradesmen like the nirdey Jafar el Uabar smuggling the gems to him. Among the Knights, the Baldur's Gate merchant Kestor was a leading non-Calishite looking into closer relations with the Rundeen. Connections could also run through families: Rundeen moneylender Nurih al Suhal had a Knight of the Shield brother-in-law Roffas Calindor.

Less of an alliance, during the el Djenispool Dynasty the Rundeen enjoyed complete control of six of the dozen or more major criminal families of Calimshan. After the Darkstalker Wars and Syl-Pasha Ralan, however, only four remained by 1370 DR and all were under el Pesarkhal control. The only remaining Rundeen family was the Vyndahla clan of smugglers and pirates operating in Almraiven and Volothamp, who worked with the Rundeen to oust el Pesarkhal, who in turn waited for a chance to destroy them too.

The Twisted Rune had had some unspecified influence on the Rundeen over the course of its history, by means of cat's-paw agents and utter secrecy. In truth, also by subtle and unseen means, they'd formerly controlled almost all the major crime families of Calimshan—including those the Rundeen thought they had control of. Many agents of the Twisted Rune were also members of the Rundeen, the Knights of the Shield, and other groups that had no awareness of their primary loyalty.

One of their more recent allies around 1370 DR was the Se'Sehen tribe of yuan-ti, who came to them to learn more of human organizations and trade. Previously, the yuan-ti's only market to the wider world was in Mhairhetel in the Mhair Jungles. While the yuan-ti supplied exotic herbs and poisons from the jungles, the Rundeen delivered slaves to the snake people and transported their spies north, both purebloods and tainted ones, who went as far north as Skullport and liaised with the Iron Ring slaver Zstulkk Ssarmn. The Rundeen even began secretly including pureblood agents amongst its ranks.  Privately, the Rundeen saw this as more of a mentorship program than an actual alliance, but would not admit this to the serpentfolk. Nevertheless, the Se'Sehen got their spies, thieves, and assassins operating across Faerûn, maintained their power against rival yuan-ti tribes in the Black Jungles, and advanced their plans to rule the surface world.

Similarly, the Sauringar, a weaker yuan-ti tribe who were close allies of the Se'Sehen, had worked with the Rundeen for several centuries, albeit in subtle and indirect ways. They supported the Rundeen's piracy and trade protection policies and in the 1300s DR discreetly discouraged them from choosing leaders who displayed the extreme ophiophobia that was customary among Tashalans. Thus it was that the Rundeen were ready to accept the alliance with the Se'Sehen, which in turn increased security for the Sauringar.

Enemies
In addition, the Rundeen had little in the way of enemies, for their only interests were mundane profits, markets, and trade. The majority of those who despised the organization were smaller-scale merchants forced to compete with better-supported Rundeen partners, and few were worthy of their notice, let alone their antagonism. Their greatest potential rival in mercantile matters was the Iron Throne, simply because both desired monopolies on trade in west Faerûn. The Zhentarim were also natural rivals for similar reasons.

Similarly, the pashas of Calimshan had the power to counter the Rundeen in their realm, and when Syl-Pasha Ralan el Pesarkhal united Calimshan under his reign through the 1360s DR, the Tradesmen lost their grip on Calimport and Almraiven, forcing them to work to regain their monopoly. Ralan had a ruthless desire to amass all political, mercantile, and criminal power in Calimshan under his own authority, and the Rundeen stood in his way so he sought to banish them from the land drive them out of business, or get them somehow under his control. Despite this, he admired the Tradesmen and had no personal animosity toward them and he'd done business with them previously. Understanding the power of the Rundeen and where and how they had influence, Ralan elected to avoid bestirring them until he'd consolidated his own power. For his son, the sultan and admiral Tahyr, the enmity was personal: Rundeen pirates had killed his wife and two brothers.This also means Rundeen pirates killed two of Ralan's sons, but this is not addressed in the sources. The attack may have occurred after the statement of Ralan's positive views, Ralan may somehow be unaware, or he simply may not care. As of 1370 DR, the Yrshelem waited and watched to decide their course of action and evaded his spies by meeting always in a different place.

Naturally, there was also friction with powerful organized crime groups, who would either prey on their trade or compete with their own criminal enterprises. Among these were the Night Masks of Westgate, whose few real enemies included the Rundeen, Knights of the Shield, Men of the Basilisk, and other mercantile secret societies that had the resources and will to counter them at every opportunity, though none could harm them significantly either. The Cult of the Dragon and the Rundeen had also occasionally, albeit briefly, clashed over control of criminal trade. The Xanathar's Thieves' Guild's entry into Calimport was noticed but had not yet brought them into conflict. The Shadow Thieves of Amn named the Rundeen their greatest foe, being the biggest quasi-criminal power in Calimshan and thus the biggest impediment to their expansion south. But the Rundeen did not share their acrimony: they had different spheres of interest and influence when it came to crime and territory. The Yrshelem would be more interested in making a deal, such as surrendering some kinds of criminal activity for trade agreements or joining forces against Syl-Pasha Ralan, if given the chance. In fact, the Rundeen's offer of minor trading rights south of Amn for information and access to Maztica was rebuffed.

But perhaps their greatest enemies were the Harpers, while the Rundeen also figured highly among the many foes of Those Who Harp. The Harpers opposed the Tradesmen for their slaving and monopolizing, while the Rundeen despised them for regularly disrupting their plans, and especially for the major defeat inflicted on them during what they dubbed the Treacherous Stab in 1349 DR. Previously, however, the Rundeen had rarely opposed the Harpers, except on such occasions when they tried to end their monopolies, piracy, and slavery. Those Rundeen leaders who held a grudge established their various anti-Harper mercenary companies. In The Code of the Harpers p. 119, where the Rundeen were first mentioned, it's said they "rarely oppose the Harpers—except when the Harpers move to break up Rundeen monopolies (such as they once held in the Tashalar ports)" referring to a present state of affairs. However, all later coverage details their enmity after the historical Treacherous Stab. Therefore, this less-aggressive relationship is presumed to be before the Treacherous Stab. Thus, when they were discovered, the Rundeen were quick to seize any opportunity to attack and eliminate Harpers, capture and interrogate them before killing them, sabotage their plans, and destroy their bases. They kept their mercenary and adventuring bands on retainer for this reason.

History
Origins
The origins and past deeds of the Rundeen were little known and few scholars worked to uncover it—many of those who tried were found dead, particularly members of the Harpers and Heralds of Faerûn. Moreover, due to their later connection, some incorrectly theorized it was an offshoot of the Knights of the Shield, owing to their entwined history and certain similarities, namely both being secret societies and having similar greeting gestures. What was known was that they, or their precursors, were originally a Tashalaran merchant company that emerged during the later period of the Shoon Imperium, sometime before the 6 century DR, at least as far as could be safely ascertained. In fact, they were even older, most likely having been around since the empire's peak.

Thus, the majority of studies began and ended at the etymology of their name, which was formerly written as Rulnadeen or Rulndiin in old records before simplifying to Rundeen over time: ruln was a Dwarvish word roughly translated as 'safety from fear of theft', and deen was a generic root word for 'coin' in use at the time of the Shoon Imperium. Hence it was the 'Safe Coin' consortium, focused on the protection of members and their assets and riches over long and hazardous trade expeditions. Extant shipping records of the time made mention of their heavily armed cargo ships escorted by swift military vessels, little better than corsairs and raiders, that launched preemptive attacks on rival shipping in order to secure their own. Although this increased costs, such ruthless and aggressive protection saw the Rulnadeen, or Rulndiin, dominate rival merchants. Their habits would change little over the next eight centuries, except when pressed to by outside forces.

In the, the Syl-Pasha of Calimshan, Akkabar Shoon the Elder, arranged for pirates to murder almost the entire Tethyrian royal family, paving the way for his coup and the establishment of the Shoon Imperium. He tried to frame the deaths on the Rundeen, then an up-and-coming merchant cabal, but without success.

One known early member of the Rundeen during the time of the Shoon Imperium was a notorious pirate and slaver named Malavar.The novel Blackstaff p. 255 mentions three historical figures named Malavar, with the Rundeen one as second oldest, implied to be related to the Shoon in some way. The eldest Malavar appeared around  and died around 92 DR, while the youngest Malavar is a trader of Athalantar, which lasted from  to . Given the chance of overlap between their lifespans and the Imperium being established in, the Rundeen Malavar would've lived sometime between 27 and 342 DR.

The Coin and the Shield
In time, the Shoon Imperium stretched across the Shining Sea to the northern coast of the Chultan Peninsula, arriving in Chult and the Confederation of Tashtan when they conquered the Tashtan cities in the . When the Knights of the Shield, then controlling a network of trade information-broking in Calimshan and Tethyr, tried to expand their influence to these new markets, they were rebuffed by the Rulnadeen. Upon investigating their new opponents, the Knights realized that it would be much simpler to agree to the merchants' demands and join them as members than to get rid of them by force. They allied and the Rulnadeen quickly began extending northward. Being more experienced in trading with foreign powers, several Knights quickly became high-ranking members of the Rulnadeen while the Tashalaran group used its new contacts to gain a foothold in Calimshan. At this time, the Mitalibbar switched the names of their ranks from Tashalan coins to Shoon Imperium coins.

Even when the Shoon Imperium was weak and divided under Qysari Shaani (–), the alliance of the Tradesmen and Knights meant that trade continued to flow from the Chultan Peninsula into the core of the Imperium and back.

In the final years of the Shoon Imperium, the leaders of the Rulnadeen began offering affiliate memberships in exchange for dues to various merchant houses, allowing the Rulnadeen to make bigger profits for less work.

When the Shoon Imperium finally fell in the, both the Rulnadeen and the Knights saw an opportunity to fill the power vacuum left behind. The Rulnadeen supported the Tashlutan merchants in establishing an oligarchy to govern the once-again independent realm and maintain order, following the death of the viceroy of Tashluta and his Shoon wife. The Knights tried to take over the top leadership positions of their southern partners and thereby seize mercantile control of the whole northern coast of the Chultan Peninsula. But the Rulnadeen resisted their efforts and, resenting it, gave an ultimatum to the Knights who had joined them: sever all ties to the Knights or leave forever. Most chose to leave;Cloak & Dagger p. 128 says most Knights chose to leave the Rulnadeen, while Serpent Kingdoms says most chose to stay. As the latter appears to be based on a misreading of the former, this article adopts the older version. with the empire's collapse, they'd lost or given up their southern possessions, lost control of trade over the Shining Sea, and so no longer held sway amongst the Rulnadeen. In contrast, it was the Rulnadeen who were gaining the upper hand in Calimshan and kept their businesses there. The Rulnadeen and the Knights of the Shield were entirely separate organizations once more.

In the aftermath, without their former allies as rivals, without political stability in the area, and without imperial soldiers to enforce peace between merchants and prevent piracy, the Rulnadeen were perfectly positioned to fill the power vacuum and unilaterally create and enforce their own mercantile law. They discontinued simply attacking all rival shipping and instead only attacked some rival shipping and shared their protection with their affiliate members and any who'd give them a cut of the profits. As they were the only group to offer any protection, more and more merchants, preferring to lose some profit than all of it, opted for such dubious sanctuary with the Rulnadeen and their protection racket. And those who declined struggled to compete with the swelling Rulnadeen fleets and to avoid their attacks. Soon, only ships protected by the Rulnadeen were safe to travel in Calishite waters. Moreover, the Rulnadeen could delegate routine voyages to other companies and reserve riskier but more profitable expeditions for themselves. This practice would continue even into the 1300s DR.

Thus, in the, the Rulnadeen established a monopoly on all trade into and out of every port in Calimshan, around the Shining Sea, and along the Chultan Peninsula, where they also dominated the slave trade. With the fall of the Shoon Imperium, the Rundeen succeeded them as the primary architects of the slave trade, and even kept the cruel practice going long after it might've been broken and abandoned as unprofitable. In this time, their name also distorted into 'Rundeen', partly due to simplicity, partly due to outsiders' mispronunciation.

In a major stratagem to disrupt and seize control of Calishite trade, in the, the Rundeen arranged for ships sailing under false colors to arrive in the harbors of Calimport and Almraiven, take berths deep amongst other vessels and near shore, and then, by magic, explode. The resulting Port Fires devastated both cities, razing all docks, shipyards, and port warehouses and many other structures, not to mention claiming many lives.

The Fist of Rundeen, an elite band of assassins used to eliminate threats and traitors when deemed absolutely necessary, were formed some time in the 11 century DR.

The Rage of Wizards conflict, fought between two dozen ambitious archmages of Tashluta and Lapaliiya, who fought the yuan-ti of the Coiled Cabal, in the, momentarily shook the Rundeen's control of Tashalar, but did not break it.

In the early 1200s DR, Rundeen assassins murdered the vizar and wizard of Almraiven, Halasahr the Red, and his favorite jhasina (concubine) in their sleep. During the attack, the jhasina's enchanted slippers were also stolen.

The Treacherous Stab of the Harpers

Of course, this dominance drew the attention of meddlesome (that is, good) outsiders and many such organizations endeavored to liberate the port cities from their iron grasp. Among these groups were the Harpers, who would try to thwart their monopolizing, piracy, and slavery. However, the Rundeen were always able to fend them off or redirect them at their rivals and the rest of the time were content to ignore them. Nevertheless, some Tradesmen held a grudge and established the various anti-Harper mercenary companies. They would go on to have several clashes, but were defeated several times by the Harpers, who saw them as no longer effective by 1367 DR.

But then, in the, came an attack they could not shrug off. With a plan developed by Cylyria Dragonbreast of Berdusk and led by Myrmeen Lhal, a band of Harpers dedicated to freeing the Chultan ports from the Rundeen exposed and killed three members of the Yrshelem as well as forty-seven agents and representatives of the Mitalibbar. The Rundeen's monopoly on the Chultan Peninsula was successfully broken and their activities in Calimshan were seriously disrupted, to the point that they were yet to recover two decades later.
The Rundeen leaders dubbed this attack "the Treacherous Stab of the Harpers" and sought revenge. In retaliation, the Rundeen would kill or capture, interrogate, and execute any and all Harpers they could find, vowing to never offer quarter to a Harper. As part of their efforts to retake control of their so-called "lost cities", the Rundeen altered their approach to dealing with politicians there by being more open, proactive, and prominent. Moreover, they established companies of mercenary adventurers to loot ruins and battle their enemies on land, and hired pirates to raid rivals on the sea, and generally snatching up businesses tied to trade, such as shipbuilders and suppliers of raw materials, so they might shut out competitors.

Around 1352 DR, the current nirdey of Manshaka, a caravaneer named Rahimat yi Manshaka, revealed 'himself' to actually be a woman named Rafi el Shuree. Impressing leadership with her skill and audacity, she was appointed Grand Yrshelem of Caravans only two years later in the .

In the, the Clenched Fist fought a band of Harpers including the bard Mintiper Moonsilver, who had nothing good to say about them.

Modern History
In the, the then Grand Yrshelem of Ships died of old age, so the others appointed Zuras el Ireen, a successful Rundeen shipping businessman and former pirate to the position.

The mass death of Bhaal-worshiping assassins during the Time of Troubles in the, gutted the Shadow Thieves who'd once dominated Calimshan's underworld. In only a few months, the Rundeen and eight other criminal organizations (including the Dark Dagger and some beholden to the Twisted Rune) moved in and carved it up between them, maintaining a new, uneasy balance of power for almost two years. But the following year, the Calishite authorities caught up and tried to muscle in, triggering the Darkstalker Wars for control of the criminal underworld. This served as cover for the rise to power of Ralan el Pesarkhal. Over the course of the 1360s DR, Syl-Pasha Ralan el Pesarkhal united the cities of Calimshan under his rule. With this, the Rundeen lost their grip on Calimport and Almraiven and their monopoly on shipping across the Shining Sea. Nevertheless, their continuing control of other cities let them retain their monopolies in several industries.

In the, the First Yrshelem of Slaving retired and passed the position onto Saibhon el Bhiras, an enterprising slaver and businessman.

Material for Volo's Guide to Calimport was written over 1364 to 1365 DR by the Master Traveler, Volothamp Geddarm, but all final drafts were destroyed by both Rundeen agents and Calishite pashas, so it went officially unreleased.

Fortunately, at least for them, the Rundeen uncovered a band of young Harpers passing through Myratma in the, and had them kidnapped, and presumed murdered. The group had been journeying northward to arrange aid for the Janessar in their rebellion and slave emancipation, and carried information on them. That year, the Grand Yrshelem of Slaves retired; Saibhon el Bhiras was promoted to the position.

As of the, to make up for their losses, the Rundeen had begun to be more proactive and visible in their deeds as they were making efforts to regain their monopoly around the Shining Sea and the Chultan Peninsula, move into Lapaliiya and the Lake of Steam, retake control of Calimport and Almraiven, keep hold of what they still had in Calimshan, and start making contacts in Amn and Tethyr. To this end, the Yrshelem were even contemplating allying with the Knights of the Shield again: it could get them renewed access to the northern shore of the Shining Sea, while the Knights would avoid piracy in the south. Moreover, a goodly number of Rundeen agents had joined the Knights and it promised to be a profitable arrangement, despite residual suspicion of another attempted takeover. This time, the Rundeen would ensure no Knights joined the Yrshelem. In any case, the Rundeen were patient and the plans were heavily discussed but unlikely to be enacted for a couple of years at least. The Rundeen and the Knights of the Shield would reestablished their alliance by 1373 DR. In addition, the Rundeen patiently but closely monitored Pesarkhal, understanding they were a barrier to his goal of amassing all political, mercantile, and criminal power in Calimshan. Their options ranged from an alliance, but deemed this unlikely given his ruthless habit of disposing of those no longer of value to him, to an assassination. The Yrshelem gambled that their anonymity would protect them and that threatening Pesarkhal might persuade him they were too big to try to bring down.

Meanwhile, the growing trade from Maztica attracted the attention of the Grand Yrshelem of Ships, Zuras el Ireen, but he found it still too hazardous for both Rundeen trade vessels and pirates and elected to wait until the route was safer. Nevertheless, the Rundeen contacted a few known members of the Shadow Thieves of Amn, seeking information and access to Maztica in exchange for some minor trading rights to Amn's south, but this deal was rejected.

Similarly, the Rundeen's interest in Amn was threatened by the emergence of the Sythillisian Empire, a monstrous humanoid kingdom seizing land in southern Amn. While the Rundeen avoided dealing with armies and non-humans, they did mull over making a deal with the empire's ruling ogre magi, Sythillis and Cyrvisnea: Rundeen pirates like the Sea Reavers would blockade Amnian ports like Murann to prevent treasure ships fleeing, in exchange for a cut of that treasure, as well as stop the Amnians retaking the city. They then might have a new ally and base in the north without needing the Knights of the Shield at all. And while the Shadow Thieves might wish to strike back, Amn was too far north and Tethyr was in their way. While they had some early successes in negotiating with the ogre magi, the Yrshelem were not sure if they could trust them. Yet they went ahead and made the deal in 1370 DR and by 1372 DR the Rundeen and their pet Nelanther pirates were using Murann as a safe haven and defending it against naval counter-attack.

At some point, Rundeen slavers on a raid in Tashalar ran into yuan-ti from the Black Jungles, and attacked. However, these yuan-ti, hailing from the Se'Sehen tribe, were on something of a fact-finding mission about human organizations and trade and held the slavers off until they heard them out. Thereafter, they had good relations and pureblood ambassadors accompanied the Rundeen slavers to meet with the mitalibbar of Calimport, and thence the Yrshelem. The yuan-ti proposed a limited alliance: for a supply of slaves and the chance to work alongside and study the Rundeen's human members, they would offer exotic trade goods and maybe even arcane lore. If the Se'Sehen could use what they learned to produce their own goods and invigorate their culture, this arrangement had the option of becoming a proper trade alliance. Intrigued by the possibilities, the Yrshelem agreed and, over the course of 1370 DR, a good number of yuan-ti appeared amongst the Rundeen membership, with pureblood agents being posted in Rundeen-dominated cities. Amongst these was Sarsor "the Rope" yi Tashluta, a pureblood working as a Rundeen enforcer. However, only the Grand Yrshelem knew the truth of these new members.

At some point, Rundeen-affiliated pirates caused the deaths of two sons of the syl-pasha of Calimshan, Ralan el Pesarkhal, as well as the young wife of a third son, Tahyr.This is undated, but Tahyr's age (36 in 1370 DR) would put this sometime after 1350 DR. Moreover, it can be no later than Flamerule of 1370 DR, the date of the Calimport sourcebook (published October 1998). As there is no known reference to this in other books and Ralan has positive views on the Rundeen in Empires of the Shining Sea p. 74 (published earlier in August 1998), it is likely to be more recent but not followed up on in the transition to 3 edition. Hence, it has been placed at the later date. This is supported by the other apparent attack on the Pesarkhals.

There came another outrage in 1370 DR when the husband of Fatima el Galsah—the eldest daughter of Ralan el Pesarkhal—vanished from Almraiven and Vizar Majel el Arpooristan accused Rundeen trader and suspected slaver Kyahj el Haedi of arranging the man's kidnap and sale into Underdark slavery in the Alimir Hive. While Kyahj firmly protested his innocence, Fatima also blamed the leaders of the Rundeen-controlled Vyndahla clan. This led to fears of an imminent war between factions in Almraiven. Meanwhile, Fatima sought adventurers to rescue her husband from the beholders.

In the spring of the, there was a surprise wedding between Farid al Nisr, the son of First Yrshelem Fahd al Nisr (and a Harper), and Syllia Adarbrent, daughter of House Adarbrent of Waterdeep (and also a Harper). Their families did not have long to be shocked before the pair sailed off to Maztica with no clue when they might return. House Adabrent were masters of the Adarbrent Shipping Company and the Horizon Armada league of shipowners, yet were struggling financially. Seizing an opportunity to expand and claim these northern shipping fortunes, Fahd contacted Lord Royus Adarbrent with offers of generous loans at very reasonable rates, if they but signed a contract with the Rundeen. Unfamiliar with the southern merchants, Royus considered this reluctantly and cautiously. Via a go-between, he would hire unconnected adventurers to look al Nisr business dealings first.

Meanwhile, by Shieldmeet of that year, a series of assassinations and blackmails of the princes of Tashalar's ruling families had rocked the realm. Many suspected the Rundeen or yuan-ti or lesser merchant families to be behind this, with the ultimate goal being to install a noble as overall ruler of the realm who would serve as their puppet. Whatever the truth, all factions involved began hiring mercenaries and adventurers for either their own defense or to make counter-attacks on suspected foes.

But whatever their plans, following the Spellplague of the, a vast stretch of Tashalar was submerged beneath the sea, while the genies Calim and Memnon and their genasi followers took over Calimshan, reshaping the realm. And so, after centuries of operation, the Rundeen faded from history.

Appendix
Notes

Rise and Fall of the Rundeen
Although the Rundeen were first introduced in The Code of the Harpers (1993), they were not detailed or integrated into other plotlines and lore until Lands of Intrigue, Calimport, and Empires of the Shining Sea (1997–1998) and Cloak & Dagger (2000). Coming at the very end of 2 edition and the transition to 3, several plotlines like the growing conflict between the Rundeen and the Pesarkhals seem to have been forgotten or fizzled out, so an outcome is unknown, although the Rundeen would be covered in other areas in 3 and 3.5 editions.

However, the last published mention of the Rundeen was in The Grand History of the Realms (2007), while the last chronological mention was in Serpent Kingdoms (2006; dated 1373 DR), both for 3.5 edition. No outcome was given to their ongoing plots, nor was there any hint of an end to the organization. But since then, 4 edition destroyed the Rundeen heartland of Tashalar and radically altered Calimshan, while 5 edition has been largely focused on the Sword Coast and the Savage Frontier, so there has been little opportunity to include the Rundeen either. It therefore appears that the Rundeen were severely weakened, if not ruined and driven out of business, by the Spellplague, loss of their bases and businesses in both realms, and the breaking of the long-distance trade networks they relied on. The post-Spellplague paragraph of the above history is included to bring closure and establish this possibility, but it remains speculative.

## Rust monster

A rust monster was a creature that corroded and ate metal. They were favored by the Mulhorandi god Geb, the dwarven god Abbathor, the gnomish god Flandal Steelskin, and the Duergar gods Laduguer and Deep Duerra.

Description
A rust monster was the size of a small pony, with four insectlike legs and a squat, humped body. Its hide was thick and lumpy and was yellowish tan on the belly and rust-red on the back. Its tail ended in what looked like a double ended paddle. Two long antennae come out of its head, one under each eye.

Behavior
Normally a docile creature, a rust monster could smell metal from ninety feet away. They always went for the biggest source of metal first, and preferred ferrous metals over precious ones.

Combat
A rust monster had a weak bite and did not normally attack with it. They attacked anyone who had metal, however, with their antennae. The touch of an antenna could turn even magical metal items into rust.

Ecology
Rust monsters ate the rust of freshly rusted metal objects.

Some sages speculated that rust monsters might be related to or the larval form of rust dragons The extremely few rust monsters who lived long enough to reach old age somehow transported themselves to the plane of Acheron, where they ravenously consumed the abundant metal from the plane's cubes for about one year. Afterwards, they encased themselves in cocoons made of spun metal, where they stayed for three years. Following this period, they emerged from their cocoons as rust dragon wyrmlings.

It was not known, however, whether rust monsters were native to Acheron and only traveled to the Prime Material plane as a part of their larval cycle to become rust dragons, or if rust monsters were in fact native to the Prime and were altered by the magic of Acheron once transported there.

Rust monster antennae were used in the creation of rust blades. They found these items extremely delicious and nourishing.

## Ruha

Ruha was an outcast spellcaster of the Mtair Dhafir tribe that famously helped unite Bedine people during the mid–14 century DR. She went on to have an illustrious career as a member of the Harpers, playing an important role in both the Trial of Cyric the Mad and the Return of the Archwizards sagas.

Description
In her mid–20s Ruha had elegant good looks that were highlighted by her striking eyes, that were often the only feature that was visible behind the veil and shawl she regularly wore. Her cheeks were marked by tattoos in the form of a small pattern of crossed lines.

Personality
Ruha was a confident and determined individual that fiercely valued her independence and freedom to make her own choices. While experienced great sadness and isolation in her life, she was quite selective about whom she allowed to be close to her. Ruha made sure that those around her treated her with respect, even when doing so would break from Bedine custom.

After leaving her homeland, Ruha looked to prove herself in the wider world, seemingly more so to herself than others. While her confidence faltered after some failures in life, Ruha was forced to rediscover her place as a force for good. In the end she found her role to play in the world that stood in stark contrast to the one in which she grew up.

Abilities
Since she was a young child, Ruha had received visions of events that had not yet come to pass. Her first vision was that of a flutter of hundreds of butterflies, which preceded swarms of moths that filled with holes nearly every piece of clothing and fabric that the Mtairi tribe owned. She would later have more intense prophetic visions, ones that guided her path as an adventurer and others that haunted her waking hours.

Due to the years of training she spent while in exile, Ruha was adept at casting elemental magic, specifically the the provinces of those practiced by the elemental mages that originated from Zakhara. She was particularly proficient in the sand and wind provinces.

For a brief time in 1371 DR, Ruha wielded terribly destructive magical powers bolstered by the god of destruction himself.

Possessions
She owned an enchanted +3 jambiya that was extremely important to her, as it was a gift from her foster mother and first tutor of the arcane arts when she turned 12 years old. After recovering during an arduous trek across the desert, Ruha kept with her the spellbook that also belonged to her foster mother and mentor.

One of her most prized possessions was her Harper pin, a symbol of the life she had made for herself, gifted to her by her the person that introduced her to the Harpers organization.

Relationships
Ruha was born to her mother, one of the wives of Sheikh Sabkhat, leader of the Mtairi tribe. She did not have much of a relationship with either of her parents; Ruha's mother died when she was only five years old, and Sheikh Sabkhat exiled his daughter to the a isolated oasis when when it became apparent she was having visions, an affliction that was shunned by the Bedine. The two would share a brief reunions twice more in Ruha's life, though in both instances Sabkhat wanted nothing to do with his daughter for fear of how it would affect the Mtairi people.

For years Ruha was raised by Qoha'dar, the witch that dwelled alone in the Sister of Rains oasis. A fellow exile of the Bedine, Qoha'dar taught Ruha how to wield arcane magic and use that power to survive in the harsh Anauroch desert.

Romances

Ruha enjoyed a very brief marriage with the proud Qahtani warrior Ajaman, before his untimely death mere days after their union. She traveled with his younger brother Kadumi for some time, becoming quite fond of the young man, despite his misguided overbearance and strict adherence to the more antiquated Bedine traditions.

Not long after Ajaman's death, Ruha became quite close to Lander, an outsider of the Bedine that was acting as an agent of the Harpers. Despite the custom of waiting two years after a husband's death to remarry, Ruha and Lander shared growing intimacy during troubling times, and a brief but passionate romantic tryst.

While serving on a mission for the Harpers in Elversult, Ruha garnered the infatuation of Prince Kao Chou Tang of the Ginger Palace. Although the pampered Shou prince believed he was in love with the "wu-jen" Ruha, she did not return his affection.

Nemesis
One of Ruha's most troubling relationships was that which formed between and the chosen of Cyric, Malik el Sami yn Nasser. The two repeatedly tried to kill each other a number of times over the course of a year. Ruha sought to bring Malik to justice for inhumane crimes he committed against innocent servants and scholars, while Malik continually acted in service of his Mad God.

History
Life in Anauroch
As a child, Ruha would often have visions of things to come. Because the Mtairi viewed them as a sign of witchcraft, Ruha was outcast by her father, Sheikh Sabkhat. She was then raised at the Sister of Rains oasis by the senior Qoha'dar, a fellow outcast spellcaster and kindred spirit. When Ruha was only 16 years old, Qoha'dar died and the young witch was left alone in the world. She remained at the oasis for some time, before trying to find her way back to the Mtair Dhafir camp.

In the, Ruha finally returned to her home tribe, though it was less than momentous occasion. After nearly half of the Qahtani threatened the sheikh they would leave if Ruha remained, Sabkhat arranged for her to marry a warrior from the Qahtan tribe, a some of the sheikh named Ajaman. After only three days of marriage, the Qahtani camp at El Ma'ra was attacked by the forces of the Zhentarim and no survivors remained at the camp, save for Ruha. She was not alone for long this time however, as she soon met up with Kadumi, Ajaman's brother. The pair set off to El Rahalat, in order to warn the members of Ruha's tribe of the Zhentarim, where came across a young Harper by the name of Lander, who sought to do the same. The three were unsuccessful and just barely escaped the onslaught of the Zhentilar soldiers.

Together, the three of them saw to it that many of the desert tribes formed an alliance against the Zhentarim. They traveled to the Colored Waters oasis and managed to convince Sheikhs Sa'ar of the Bait Mahwa, and Utaiba of the Raz'hadi of their cause. After defeating the Zhentarim in a decisive battle, Ruha and her two companions traveled twice across the Shoal of Thirst to equip her with the magic she needed to aid the battle against the Zhentarim. While Kadumi was slain during the journey by a Zhentarim assassin, they were ultimately successful in their mission.

Ruha, Lander, and their sheikh allies managed to gather together the leaders of 13 other Bedine camps and meet at the sacred oasis of Elah'zad. There she had a vision of the future and served as a conduit for the Mother of Waters, and shared the goddess' messages for the leaders of the Bedine: stop the invading Zhent forces.

Harper in the Heartlands
Even though she had played a great part in the unification of the Bedine tribes, and earned the trust and admiration of their respective sheikhs, Ruha felt the same isolation that she endured for so many years in her youth. She decided to leave the desert and join the Harpers herself, the group to which Lander once dedicated himself with all his efforts. For her first mission, Storm Silverhand dispatched Ruha to Voonlar to help disrupt a slave trade, posing as a serving girl at the Swords Meet. Ignorant to local customs, Ruha inadvertently accepted money from a slaver and angered him when she did not offer the services he expected. While she was fortunately saved by the Harper spy she was meant to assist, both of them were forced to leave town. The failed mission led to many slaves being sold or killed, and came to be known in Harper circles as the "Voonlar Debacle".

In the, the aspiring Harper was then sent to Elversult, to help restore the Lady Yanseldara from a magical coma, and free her from the clutches of the dracolich Hethcypressarvil, also known as "Cypress". During the voyage across the Dragonmere, aboard the Storm Sprite captained by the half-orc Fowler, Ruha took it upon herself to come to the aid of another ship that was being assailed by a black dragon. Ruha was able to destroy the dragon at sea, inadvertently causing the destruction of the Sea Sprite. The pair were rescued the Shou caravel named the Ginger Lady and given passage to Pros.

After Ruha made contact with Vaerana Hawklyn of Elversult she was appraised of her mission to infiltrate the Ginger Palace, the Shou embassy on the Dragon Coast, and locate Yanseldara's staff which they believed could lead to her whereabouts. After gaining admittance into the embassy, Ruha attracted the unwanted infatuation of Prince Kao Chou Tang and the ire of his wife Wei Dao, a dynamic that led to her abduction. Despite being taken prisoner for a short while, Ruha managed to convince the Prince and Minister Hsieh Han Liu, as well as Vaerana Hawklyn, they should not work against one another, but align themselves to rescue Yanseldara and Tang's mother, Lady Feng.

Ruha's insight and ingenuity gave her the chance to draw out Cypress and temporarily weaken it, while Prince Tang managed to rescue his mother, and destroyed the spirit gem that bound Cypress' soul along with that of Lady Yanseldara. The plan was successful and Ruha managed to destroy the dracolich once and for all in a great battle and stop its rampage in the streets of Elversult. Having achieved such great success as a new member of the Harpers, Ruha earned the trust of Vaerana Hawklyn, Lady Yanseldara and even Storm Silverhand herself. Perhaps more importantly, she had regained her self-confidence after her previous failed mission.

Chasing the Chosen of Cyric

As an experienced agent of the Harpers, Ruha completed several other missions with great success. She rescued the daughter of Duke Wycliff from the clutches of some hill giants and saved some children that had gone missing in the Trollclaw Forest. In the, Ruha received a vision about a beggar that lived in filth in front of Candlekeep. She flew south with the Griffon Cavalry of Waterdeep, arranged for residence within the Keeper's Tower, and soon met the harbinger of misfortune that would plague her for the next year, Malik el Sami yn Nasser. Masquerading as a pitiable retch named Moukhtar, Malik lied his way into Candlekeep and butchered several innocents in his hunt of the Cyrinishad, including a monk of the Avowed, along with Gwydion of Torm and Rinda a scribe from Zhentil Keep. With help from Cyric, Malik fled Candlekeep and Ruha began in pursuit.

Ruha and her ally Zale Protelyus took to the skies, riding atop the hippogriff Silvercloud, and tracked Malik west towards the Wood of Sharp Teeth. While in the forest, Ruha demonstrated greatly increased destructive powers with her magic. Under the guise of Mystra, the mischievous god Mask appeared before the pair, informed Ruha that her new powers were a boon bestowed upon her to aid in her quest to stop Malik. When Ruha next prayed to the true Mystra for guidance, the Lady of Magic directed her to use whatever means necessary to prevent him from retrieving the Cyrinishad, unaware her servant wielded the calamitous power of Talos, by means of the trickster Mask.

The pair tracked Malik down to a minor chapel of Cyric in a village within the Storm Horn mountains. As they approached, Ruha was besieged with a terrifying vision of Cyric. She directed Zale to evacuate the village and used her newfound powers to level Cyric's chapel, believing that was her goddess' will. This proved to be a terrible mistake however, as Zale was injured in the ensuing chaos and Malik still managed to escape. An avatar of the true Mystra appeared before Ruha and admonished her for her actions. Bewildered by Ruha's actions, Mystra cut off the Harper witch's access to the Weave, preventing her from casting magic any longer.

During her ongoing pursuit, Malik managed to gravely wound Silvercloud, leaving Ruha with no advantages left over the crafty Cyricist. Stripped of her powers and alone, Ruha traveled to the nearby town of Voonlar, the site of her first great failure as a Harper. Ruha met a third avatar of the gods that day, when Talos took mortal form and informed her of Mystra's imprisonment by Helm for interfering in Cyric's trial. The Storm Lord tempted her with new powers of spellcasting in exchange for her conversion to his faith, but Ruha refused. In a conciliatory gesture, Talos helped Ruha thwart the plans of the Mad God and informed her she could find the Cyrinishad in Zhentil Keep.

Ruha made her way into Zhentil Keep riding atop the healed Silvercloud, and confronted Malik once again at the city's temple of Iyachtu Xvim. While she was unsuccessful in apprehending Malik, Ruha came to understand his true intent: to swap the Cyrinishad with the True Life of Cyric at Cyric's trial. During their confrontation, was incapacitated by High Tyrannar Fzoul Chembryl, and left writhing in pain as a sacrifice for the Godson. Mystra appeared before her faithful servant once again, touched by Ruha's faithfulness to the tenets of the Mystran faith. Goddess and servant reconciled, Ruha lent her wisdom to Mystra ahead of Cyric's Trial, and Mystra freed Ruha from the clutches of the city, allowing her to leave with Silvercloud unharmed.

Return to the Desert

By Mirtul of the, Ruha began investigating the newly-returned Shadovar of Thultanthar on behalf of the Harpers. After Laeral Silverhand arranged for Ruha to serve as a guide for Princes Brennus and Clariburnus Tanthul, Ruha gained access to the flying city of Shade. At the same time, she remained determined to hunt down Malik and bring him to justice for the deaths of Rinda and Gwydion.

Ruha managed to ambush Malik in the flying city and the two nearly killed one another. The Shadovar were forced to intervene in their ongoing feud; as each were guests of Hadrhune and the Princes of Shade, Ruha and Malik were allowed to remain in the city so long as they did not harm one another.  After a short while in the city together, Malik orchestrated a plot to frame Ruha for murder, by staging the death of the celebrated giant sculptor Aris and an attempt upon his own life. Malik's attempt was a failure, only achieving the exile of Ruha to the sands of Anauroch and the placement of himself judgement of High Prince Telamont Tanthul.

While in the desert, Ruha was soon joined by Aris and his traveling companion Galaeron Nihmedu, an Evereskan practitioner of shadow magic. Ruha guided the pair of outlanders across the desert for three days, until they came upon a small oasis in the Saiyaddar and the water-deprived stone giant inadvertently rushed into the lair of a young blue dragon. While the trio were able to overcome the great beast, Aris suffered grave wounds, and he had could not travel until Ruha solicited aid from Storm Silverhand.

Once the giant recovered, the trio made their way to Arabel in Cormyr and Ruha used her status as a Harper to gain audience with city leaders. Ruha, Aris, and Galaeron were brought before the Regent of Cormyr, Princess Alusair Obarskyr, along with the attending Price Rivalen Tanthul of Thultanthar. Rivalen accused the three exiles of theft against Thultanthar and Princess Obarskyr "imprisoned" them within the dungeons of Arabel's Citadel overnight, in truth ensuring their safety from the Shadovar. When Rivalen and the Shadovar attempted to free Ruha and the others from Cormyrean imprisonment, Alusair sprang the trap she had laid. Princess Alusair used the offense to declare that all the shades of Thultanthar were to immediately leave Arabel and Cormyr, and remain banished until they ceased their terraforming magic focused upon High Ice, and no longer threatened environmental devastation across the Eastern Heartlands.

On Mirtul 26 Ruha traveled back to Anauroch alongside the War Wizard Caladnei and Cormyrean scouts to locate the flying city of Thultanthar. During their journey Ruha reunited with Sheikh Sa'ar, the leader of the Bait Mahwa and former ally at the Battle of the Chasm. She found the Mahwa tribe idling around Shadow Sea, the newly-formed body of water set upon the once-inhospitable Shoal of Thirst. Much to Ruha's displease, the Bedine people as she saw it were giving up their traditional nomadic lifestyle for one of ease and leisure.

The Cormyrean commander Hhormun generously "allowed" Sa'ar to carry out a raid on a herd of the Shadovar's flying veserab mounts, allowing the over-eager sheikh to believe he could rule over this stretch of Anauroch. This aggressive act drew the Thultanthar's warriors into battle, but granted the Cormyreans insight to the location of their flying city. For her part in all this, Ruha became a target for death by the Princes of Shade.

Joining the Chosen
In Flamerule of that year, Ruha accompanied Galaeron to a council of heads of state to Arabel, to discuss the ongoing threat posed by the Shadovar. The meeting fell apart when a lone phaerimm—whose species had besieged Evereska for nearly over half a year—took hold over Mourngrym Amcathra of Shadowdale and attempted to negotiate the cessation of Evereska from Princess Alusair and the other leaders of the Heartlands. After the meeting, Galaeron, Ruha and their recently-famous friend Aris met with Storm Silverhand and shared with her Galaeron's plan to have the chosen of Mystra take down the city of Thultanthar and free Evereska from the phaerimm threat.

During Aris' touching and bittersweet farewell to the Arabellan citizens, the four companions were joined by Mystra's other chosen, Khelben Arunsun, Laeral and Alustriel Silverhand, and Dove Falconhand, the famous Knight of Myth Drannor. As the companions ventured out, Galaeron received a troubling message from Malik el Sami, who still resided within Thultanthar. Knowing Ruha's determination for bringing Malik to justice, several among the group believed she should stay behind, lest her anger interfere with their plans. While on the High Road west to Anauroch, the companions' wagon was harried by blue dragon allies of the Shadovar. During the fighting Ruha became seperated from Galaeron, Aris, and the chosen, who continued on with their mission in Thultanthar.

On Eleasis 2, the chosen's mission in Thultanthar went awry, and the flying city briefly plummeted down to Toril. Ruha got in touch with Alustriel by means of magical communication and investigated the Shadow Sea below on their behalf. There Ruha witnessed the Karsestone falling from the sky, and spied Malik chained to the ancient artifact, praying for dear life. Ruha manipulated the Weave itself to stop Malik's descent, and shared a few select words with her longtime quarry. As the two spoke, the chain from Malik's manacle broke, and the Karsestone plunged into the Shadow Sea, where it was then claimed by Shar. Ruha reunited with Mystra's, apprehended Malik, and together they tricked him into revealing what he knew about Shar's greater plot regarding the Shadow Weave and her potential dominion over magic. With Malik finally in hand, Ruha set out for Twilight Hall to bring the little man to justice.

## Runestone

Runestones were stone slabs with messages carved into them. These messages could be warnings, instructions, stories, or accounts of events. They varied in shape and size, depending on the creator and time they were made. Some runestones even had magic enchantments placed on them.

History
The ancient civilization known as the Adamant Kingdom of Xothaer was home to numerous dwarven scholars. They recorded vast amounts of information on runestones. These records could still be found underneath the Cloud Peaks in various conditions, most shattered, in the 14 century DR.

Around 1370 DR, the dwarves of Iltkazar were famous makers of runestones.

Description
Runestones were large slabs of durable stone, usually about an inch thick. They had stories, instructions, and accounts of events carved into one or both of their faces. Runestones were sometimes carved with images or runes in relief, and thus could be used as seals or temporary path markers when pressed into wet mud. Although extremely rare, there were also runestones made of precious metals.

Variations
Dwarven Runestones
Dwarven runestones were written in the Dethek script. They usually came in diamond or circular shapes. The text started at the edge and spiraled into the center, where an illustration (such as a clan symbol) was usually carved. Due to this, they were read from the outside edge to the center.

Dwarven runestones were also used to mark graves, where an illustration of the dwarf in question would be carved into the center of the stone. Most dwarven runestones were used as maps and clues to hidden hordes of treasure. These stones were some of the most sought-after by greedy adventurers.

Known Runestones
 Delzoun Runestone: This runestone was said to explain the whereabouts of the legendary Hammer of Thunderbolts.
 Thunderholme Runestones: These runestones granted access to the ancient city of Thunderholme. Although some were recovered, many were still lost or hidden.
 Spawn Wars Runestone: A fragment of this runestone was discovered several hundred years after it was made. It detailed the events of the Spawn Wars.
 Rockfire Disaster Runestones: In 1374 DR, Cragwarden Samiel Tussin discovered two runestones southwest of Trythosford at Itzcala. The two stones described the events of the Rockfire Disaster of 987 DR.
 Duergar Runestones: This series of runestones explained the history of Clan Duergar. They were discovered in 1383 DR by the crusaders of the Great Rift.
 Axemarch Stone: This huge runestone stood  tall and  wide. It rested on the highest peak of the Marching Mountains, commemorating the dwarven king Aldir Velms leading the dwarves to the mountain range as well as the founding of High High Shanatar. The rune dated this event as "day 75 of the 492nd year of the founding of House Axemarch".

 Angelhead Runestone: A runestone inscribed with writing of an unknown script and language was found lying amongst the rocks outside the main entrance of Angelhead Mine, wherein ancient ruins were found in 1479 DR.

## Ruathym

Ruathym  was an island in the Trackless Sea and one of the larger islands north of the Moonshae Isles. It was the homeland of the Northlander peoples.

Geography
Ruathym was located more than  north of the Moonshae Isles, and was sometimes considered by the Northlanders to be nominally a part of that archipelago.

It was a rocky island with rugged coasts dotted with narrow coves, fjords, and inlets. The island itself was largely barren but with green hills and forested purple mountains. A few villages and steadings that clung to the coastline. It was a generally inhospitable place.

Culture
The people of Ruathym were known to be savage and violent, even compared to other Northlanders. As the first of the Northlanders, they were said to believe that it was their birthright to be regarded as the rulers of all other Northlanders. They respected the driving winds and waves of the sea, and historically venerated warriors and the battles they waged. By the late 15 century DR, with many of their longtime rivals diminished following the Spellplague, the island's long traditions of raiding had faded, and island was relatively peaceful. Even so, outsiders were cautioned that the island could rally for war at a moment's notice: the people of Ruathym remained savage and warlike, but mainly focused their aggression on each other, with wars between petty rulers invariably erupting every few years.

The dead were cremated in large funeral bonfires that escorted them on their way to the Halls of Tempus.

Language
The people here spoke Ruathen, which was a dialect of Illuskan, although most spoke Common. Dock-alfar meant dark elf. Hamfriggan (literally "shapestrong") referred to the shapeshifters of Ruathym.

History
Early History
Some time around, the Sailors of the Mountainous Waves, the navy of the dwarf realm of Haunghdannar, built a stone fortress they named Sonnmorndin on the island, which was originally called Arauwurbarak.

The first humans to settle the island arrived circa, having sailed there from somewhere to the west. By, they would found Illusk on the Sword Coast North, and by, they would begin colonizing the many islands of the Sea of Swords, including the Gull Rocks, Gundarlun, Mintarn, the Purple Rocks, the Teeth, Tuern, Umukek, the Wave Rocks, and the Whalebones. They avoided the Moonshae Isles at this time, believing them to be cursed by the fey that dwelt there.

In the, Uthgar Gardolfsson led his Ruathen reavers in a raid on Illusk. They sacked the city, but the future deity was ultimately driven back by the angry townsfolk.

In the, the Tome of the Unicorn was reported to be in the possession of the monarchy of Ruathym. It was stored in the Green Rooms.

Modern History
The Tome of the Unicorn was stolen by a Calishite mage called Shond Tharovin in the . Ignorant of the true culprit, the last foreign ship to leave the island—Telgontan Horn, a caravel from Luskan—was attacked and sunk. In response, Luskan declared war on Ruathym. The Luskanites destroyed Ruathym's fleet and landed troops on the island. With help from the Holgerstead tribe, First Axe Ulphron Lithyl's forces managed to drive the Luskanites back into the sea after a few months of warfare.

However, Luskan attacked again the following year. This time they were much more successful, managing to conquer the entire realm save for scattered pockets of resistance. In Uktar of the, Aumark Lithyl, Ulphron's son and a member of the famous Knights of Myth Drannor, returned home. After his father died in battle just before the winter storms set in, he managed to unite the four tribes of Ruathym under his banner.

In Kythorn of the, the Lords' Alliance threatened war against both sides unless they came to an accord. Realizing that Luskan and the Northlander island realms had more in common than any other coastal city, Ruathym, Luskan, the Whalebones, and Tuern declared an alliance called the Captain's Confederation (also known as the Captains' Alliance) the following summer and began raiding shipping and coastal settlements up and down the Sword Coast in concert.

During the Time of Troubles that year, avatars of Labelas Enoreth and Clangeddin Silverbeard battled on Ruathym. After the destructive battle, plantmen appeared in the streets, attacking residents and the crew of the Realms Master. Although the crew managed to escape thanks to the intervention of Labelas, many of the island's inhabitants died to the creatures' onslaught.

In the, the Captain's Confederation was dissolved when Luskan attempted to conquer Ruathym a second time. Luskan, with the help of the Kraken Society, began a conspiracy (mainly involving pickled sea elves placed in Ruathen casks) intended to provoke Waterdeep and the Lords' Alliance against Ruathym, but the conspiracy was discovered by Liriel Baenre. The attacking Luskan navy, led by High Captain Rethnor, and a merrow army led by Vestress of the Kraken Society, was defeated by the Ruathen with her help. The mind flayer Vestress, self-proclaimed Regent of Ascarle, sent a merrow army against Ruathym but the Northmen managed to repel them. Threatened with war by Waterdeep, Luskan withdrew its forces.

A third war was instigated between Luskan and Ruathym when Black Garius sent men to steal the Tome of Iltkazar from the Green Rooms in 1374 DR.

By the, Luskan had largely collapsed, and the loss of this old enemy led to a relaxing of Ruathym's more warlike posturing. At the same time, they policed their ports more stridently, going so far as to turn away ships from the Moonshaes in order to avoid the many troubles that were plaguing that archipelago at the time. It was at this time that the Storm Maiden began to rally the people of Ruathym, who believed that she could lead them to a golden age of conquest.

## Rusalka

A rusalka was a female fey water spirit that lived in cold lakes, streams, and rivers. They were notorious for their song, which could lure mortals into their arms or into watery graves.

Description
A rusalka appeared to be a beautiful woman, with pale white skin and hair that was an odd hue of green and gold, but red lips. Despite their cold environs, they often wore little more than a gown or shawl of diaphanous cloth, but a number would even go nude. However, Liriel Baenre encountered a warrior rusalka with muscular body and wearing leather armor, with white skin and empty white eyes.

Abilities
In a form of symbiosis, a rusalka was mystically bonded to a specific body of water or waterway, ranging in size from a pond to a lake or a stream to a river. She could move to a distance of up to 300 yards (275 meters) away from the water's edge, but beyond this point she could no longer breathe. She had to hold her breath or immediately begin to drown in air, and could not breathe normally again until she'd re-immersed herself in her home water. However, provided she was immersed in or in contact with her watery home, a rusalka had great skill in hiding and escaping capture. In any water, they were expert swimmers.

Rusalkas were most known for their beguiling song that could lure listeners toward them. A single chosen being within 300 feet (91 meters) of their water who could hear them sing would approach the rusalka by the most direct path, if they could not resist or were not immune to mind-affecting magic. A rusalka had to keep singing to keep up the effect, but could do so either above or beneath the waves. If a beguiled being's route took them through a hazardous, they might be discouraged and had another chance to shake off the beguilement. If they made it to the rusalka's side (5 feet, or 1.5 meters), then they fell under a charm (as with charm monster), and the rusalka could stop singing. If the being ever resisted the beguilement or charm, then they were immune to a rusalka's powers for another day.

Combat
Rusalkas did not seek for battle, only lovers or easy victims. When threatened, they invariably fled into the water, relying on their ability to hide and their familiarity with their waterways to avoid those who chased them there. They might carry only a simple dagger.

When luring a victim to their doom with their song, a villainous rusalka might force them to enter or cross a dangerous area, usually deep water or a swift current. They would keep moving, never letting a victim get in arm's reach.

Four times a day, a rusalka could cast the spell water breathing like a powerful sorceress, allowed the recipient to breathe easily underwater for a period of 24 hours.

Personality
Trapped at their watery homes, rusalkas were often terribly lonely. For company, they regularly used their beguiling songs to lure mortals, usually men, to their water to join them. Wicked rusalkas, and even some who were simply uncaring, would cause their charmed victims drown, while those of good heart would emerge to bestow water breathing on them, letting them join them underwater.

In this way, rusalkas and charmed or willing mortals developed relations, but these were fated to end in tears. Realizing her love could not be content in her watery world, a rusalka eventually broke off the relationship. She freed the mortal from her charm, or withheld the water breathing power, forcing them to leave.

Society
It was commonly believed, even in Rashemen, that rusalkas were the unquiet spirits of drowned maidens, like ghosts, forever bound to haunt the sites of their deaths. Their attacks on swimmers were to be believed to be deliberate, or else the result of the ghost remembering drowning, panicking, and grabbing hold of the living. In truth, however, they were fey beings and natural water spirits.

Living lonely lives, rusalka usually lived singly, and only one could be found dwelling in a given body of water, if at all. However, on occasion, multiple rusalkas would appear in an area and form a covey of three to six members.

In some stories, it was said vodyanoi took rusalkas as wives.

Homelands
Found in cold climes, rusalkas dwelled in the lakes, streams, and rivers they were bound to.

In particular, rusalkas were found in Rashemen, in and around Lake Ashane. They were known to dwell in White Rusalka Vale, near the lake's shore. It was something of a dead-magic zone to all but Rashemi Witches. One also appeared in a hot spring near Dernovia.

History
In Rashemen in the Year of Maidens, 1361 DR, in the spring near Dernovia, Liriel Baenre had an encounter with a rusalka. She estimated her to be the ghost of a warrior who'd died whilst encountering, perhaps robbing, the dragon-like guardian spirit of the spring. The rusalka attacked Liriel and tried to drag her into the pool, but the dragon spirit swallowed the rusalka.

In the Year of the Bent Blade, 1376 DR, a felucca carrying Bastun, Faena, Duras, and other warriors across Lake Ashane was attacked by rusalkas. They sang part of the Firedawn Cycle to lull and beguile the crew, luring them into the water. When the alarm was raised, they tried to drag their victims overboard. The Rashemi fought them off, and afterward feared the spirits had risen against the exiled vremyonni, Bastun.

Appendix
Background
The rusalka is based on the rusalka of Slavic mythology. They first appeared in Dungeons & Dragons and the Forgotten Realms in Elaine Cunningham's novel Windwalker in 2003, before being re-introduced in the sourcebook Frostburn in 2004.

There has been some contradiction in the sources over whether rusalkas are undead ghosts or living fey. The rusalka of Slavic mythology appears to have begun as a nature spirit before being described as the spirit of a dead woman. Windwalker calls them water spirits and only speculates they are ghosts, but strongly depicts one as an undead being. Frostburn however specifically contradicts this, making them fey. The Shield of Weeping Ghosts (2008) followed suit, basing its version on the sourcebook.

## Ruining

The Ruining, also known as the Cataclysm or simply the eruption of Mount Hotenow,Proper names for this event, such as "the Ruining" and "the Cataclysm", appear only in the 2013 video game Neverwinter and the 2024 cookbook Puncheons & Flagons. Other canon sources refer to this event as either a/the "cataclysm", "the eruption of Mount Hotenow", or "when Mount Hotenow erupted". was the destruction of the city of Neverwinter when Mount Hotenow erupted at the end of the summer of the . It was the defining event of the 15 century DR in the history of Neverwinter.

Description

The northern peak of the Mount Hotenow volcano exploded, sending a pyroclastic flow of superheated ash, fiery boulders, and lava directly into the city of Neverwinter. Buildings and people alike were consumed by flames, creating streams of ash and blood. Lava flowed into the city via the Neverwinter River, transforming the water into hissing steam as hot as a forge before becoming just lava. Falling boulders easily demolished homes, and the air was filled with hot ash and searing gas that could incinerate flesh. Much of the city was burned to the ground and half of the population died in an instant.

The eruption also triggered a massive shockwave and a tremendous earthquake that caused some of Neverwinter's districts to sink and others to rise, creating steep cliffs within the city. These tremors tore the city apart and caused buildings to fall in on themselves, leaving much of the architecture badly damaged if not reduced to rubble. Most surviving structures were those which were heavily or magically reinforced, such as military fortifications, public buildings, or the homes of powerful wizards or wealthy nobles. The many collapsed buildings broke and clogged the city's catacombs and sewers, turning them into a twisting underground maze that threatened to collapse further. When sewer tunnels did collapse, they created trenches running through the ruins which served as convenient highways for monsters.

Locations
Neverwinter

Some districts of Neverwinter were hit harder than others, with the western neighborhoods (located further from the quaking mountain) tending to fare better.

Alchemist Quarter
The southeastern quadrant of the city was perhaps the hardest hit: this was where the earth shifted so violently as to open the Chasm, which swallowed the Arcanist Quarter and left the whole area as a broken maze of ruins. The cathedral of Mystra fell into the Chasm, alongside the nearby Apprentice Row. Two other nearby structures—an army post and the fortified home of Roderick the Wary—remained perched perilously on the Chasm's edge.

Blacklake
The noble mansions of the northwestern Blacklake District were largely left standing thanks in part to their sturdy (and sometimes magically reinforced) construction, however the lives of the district's residents were not spared in the fires. The Blacklake itself became so choked with ash that it was little more than a pond of sludge.

The House of a Thousand Faces survived, but was abandoned in the immediate aftermath.

City Core
The city's southwestern quarter remained comparatively intact. The Hall of Justice was largely undamaged while the House of Knowledge was nearly ruined. Of the three bridges spanning the Neverwinter River, only the Winged Wyvern Bridge survived while the Dolphin and Sleeping Dragon bridges were destroyed.

Castle Never was hit hard by the quakes and fires, leaving parts of it toppled and most of its inhabitants dead. However, the Castle's catacombs—known as the Neverneath—were left strangely intact.

Upheavals in the Neverdeath cemetery altered its elevation, causing mausoleums to collapse and graves to spill open.

Docks District
The docks were destroyed and the whole harbor was swamped with tidal waves, leaving submerged buildings and drowned corpses blocking any access by ship. As such, the city docks were left flooded, rotting, and abandoned. The earthmote upon which the Moonstone Mask was located became untethered from the ground and floated out to sea for several days.

River District
The northeastern districts were destroyed, although the area boasted many hidden underground cellars and chambers that remained sealed and untouched. The Merchant Guild Hall and Merchant Square survived mostly intact, but were utterly abandoned afterward. The merchants' homes on nearby Waukeen Way were not so lucky, with very few left standing.

Other Settlements
The destruction wrought by the eruption was mostly confined to Neverwinter, although it had far reaching consequences for other parts of the region. The eruption devastated the town of Thundertree—which was quickly abandoned before coming to be haunted by possessed plant creatures—and it erased the county of Corlinn Hill. Meanwhile, Helm's Hold was battered, but it endured and strove to continue serving as a place of healing for survivors. Its sturdy Cathedral of Helm was unharmed.

Mount Hotenow

The northernmost of the volcano's two peaks was completely gone after the eruption, and the remaining mountain was a fuming, jagged crag filled with fire and lava. Both Mount Hotenow and the foothills north of Thundertree were sundered, and the shattering of the igneous rock around the volcano ended up revealing forgotten dwarven passages and ruins, such as Fireguard Fortress. Deeper underground, parts of the abandoned dwarven city of Gauntlgrym were flooded with lava and some areas, including its great forges, were damaged.

Neverwinter Wood
The shockwave from the eruption leveled parts of Neverwinter Wood while the lava burned a path toward the coast. The forest recovered with startling speed, however the scars of the eruption remained clear. The woods on the slopes of Mount Hotenow were completely incinerated—alongside nearly every dryad living there—leaving behind blackened husks that came to be called the Charred Wood. The Neverwinter River was overrun with lava and remained clogged with ash for months.

Shadowfell
Mount Hotenow's explosion also caused its counterpart in the Shadowfell to erupt. While this mirror eruption did not devastate the Shadowfell city of Evernight in the same way that Neverwinter had been, it also did not stop: decades later, a river of lava continued to stream through Evernight and the surrounding forest had become the ever-aflame Burning Woods. Some feared that the ongoing eruption in the Shadowfell might trigger another eruption on the Material Plane, which threatened to tear open the barrier between the two planes.

Outcome

What remained of Neverwinter initially descended into utter chaos and most survivors fled. The city would struggle for decades to rebuild and the ruins soon become bait for unscrupulous adventurers and necromancers. The volcano would continue to quake and belch smoke ominously, keeping the threat of an even more devastating cataclysm fresh in people's minds. Many viewed the events in Neverwinter as evil portents, and some came to believe that the cataclysm had been a punishment from the gods.

Ash zombies roamed the region in the eruption's aftermath, apparently animated by the fell magics released by the eruption. These zombies attacked the survivors in Neverwinter—contributing to the danger of clearing the ruins where the eruption had created corpses—and overran the remains of Thundertree. It didn’t take long for Thayan necromancers to begin creating more ash zombies.

The Sword Coast North as a whole suffered for the loss of Neverwinter, becoming a more dangerous and dark place. Overland trade in the region mostly dried up and the High Road north of Waterdeep fell into disuse and disrepair. The cataclysm also served to dispel many orcish superstitions about Neverwinter Wood, leading to an increase in orc activity in the region, notably an incursion into the Neverwinter ruins by troops from Many-Arrows led by Vansi Bloodscar.

Casualties
Thousands of people died in the cataclysm, commoner and noble alike. Their bodies came to be buried either wherever space could be found or in mass graves in Neverdeath Graveyard, or else they became fodder for carrion birds and necromancers.

Notable casualties included:
 The Alagondar royal family, who perished at Castle Never alongside all of their servants. The ancestral Crown of Neverwinter was lost along with them.
 Hugo Babris, the lord in charge of running Neverwinter.
 The Hallix family, who were eventually interred in their family mausoleum in Neverdeath Graveyard.

Survivors

Among those who survived, some went mad from the pain and grief. Nearly all survivors fled the ruined city, heading south as refugees, but a few proud Neverwinterians stayed and began trying to rebuild. It was from among these stubborn survivors that the Sons of Alagondar would eventually rise to contest the rule of Dagult Neverember.

Notable survivors included:
 Herzgo Alegni, who survived by escaping into the Shadowfell.
 Drizzt Do'Urden, who was not present in Neverwinter but witnessed the eruption from afar.
 Artemis Entreri, who survived by wedging himself beneath the Winged Wyvern Bridge before escaping the city via the sewers.
 Emmek Frewn, who fled the ruined city to Waterdeep with his parents and sisters.
 Jelvus Grinch, who would become the "First Citizen" among the survivors who resettled the ruins.
 Heartleaf, the only dryad to survive the obliteration of the woodlands on the slopes of Mount Hotenow.
The Vannath family, who fled to Baldur's Gate and married into that city's patriar class.

History
Causes
Unbeknownst to all but a few, the eruption had not been a natural occurrence: it was caused by the intentional awakening of Maegera, a massive fiery primordial sleeping beneath the forges of Gauntlgrym under Mount Hotenow. This creature had been imprisoned there centuries prior in a concerted effort by the wizard Maerin of Illusk with the dwarves of Delzoun and the elves of Iliyanbruen, but was forgotten after Gauntlgrym was abandoned in the . Maegera remained comatose thanks to a magical network stemming from the Host Tower of the Arcane in Illusk, which used sea water and water elementals to keep its power in check. However, after both the destruction of the Host Tower in the, and the disruptions of magic caused during the Spellplague in the, the stage was set for the primordial to be awakened.

In late summer of the, the Red Wizards of Thay sought to take advantage of minor tremors that had rattled the region for months and create a mass casualty event in order to empower the necromancy at their Dread Ring in Neverwinter Wood. Their local leader, Sylora Salm, plotted alongside Dahlia Sin'felle and the Ashmadai to do this by releasing Maegera from beneath Mount Hotenow, which required manipulating a dwarf of Delzoun heritage into awakening it. To that end, a small adventuring party consisting of Dahlia, Korvin Dor'crae, Valindra Shadowmantle, Athrogate, and Jarlaxle rediscovered ancient Gauntlgrym and made their way way to the legendary forges. There, the latter two were betrayed by their Thayan allies, with Athrogate hypnotically forced to activate the forge, which began the process of awakening the primordial Maegera.

After the party fled the dungeon, a plume of smoke began to rise above Mount Hotenow as the primordial freed itself from the last of the water elementals that had kept it in check. In a fit of bloodlust and rage, it triggered the eruption of the volcano.

Eruption
One of Mount Hotenow's two peaks appeared to lift into the air before being hurtled with purposeful, malicious accuracy directly at nearby Neverwinter. From the ground, the initial explosion of the mountain was eerily silent as it was clearly visible from across the region well before the sound—and shockwave—arrived. Screams and cries for loved ones began to ring out in Neverwinter as people spotted the plume before all was drowned out by the roar of the explosion and the raining fire. The actual destruction of Neverwinter was short and sudden, and left the city as a silent, gray wasteland. Both the air and the ground were choked with hot ash, making it hard for survivors to escape.

Luckily for the region beyond Neverwinter, this proved to be only a partial awakening of the primordial, which soon returned to its slumber beneath the forges of Gauntlgrym. In its fitful sleep, earthquakes continued to wrack the region, and began getting particularly severe in the . That year, Maegera was finally put back to sleep by the likes of Jarlaxle, Dahlia, Drizzt Do'Urden, and Bruenor Battlehammer, ending a second plot by the Red Wizards to trigger a much larger catastrophe. However, the ancient mechanism and magics that had long held the primordial in stasis were permanently damaged, and would not be restored until the, when Bruenor Battlehammer and his allies reclaimed Gauntlgrym and the Host Tower was raised anew in Luskan. Even so, the mighty primordial had become much more active, and would once again nearly escape its bonds in the summer of the, causing a great quake around Mount Hotenow.

Aftermath
The eruption disrupted the operations of several factions in the region. The Netherese had successfully infiltrated Neverwinter's government as they searched for the ruins of Xinlenal, but were forced to withdraw for nearly a decade. Most shadovar agents in the city survived the eruption by shadow walking to safety. Similarly, the destruction of Neverwinter prompted Bregan D'aerthe to withdraw its own growing interests in the city. The opening of the Chasm threatened to expose the presence of the Abolethic Sovereignty in the Cauldron of Blue Fire beneath Neverwinter, leading the aboleths to send waves of horrific plaguechanged monsters up through the Chasm in order to ward off any interference.

The potent magic released by Maegera's awakening continued to permeate the region for decades. Within the bowels of Mount Hotenow itself, fire creatures and volcano-worshiping cultists had experienced spiritual ecstasy when the primordial awoke, and worked for decades to bring about a second eruption under the leadership of the fire giant "king" Gommoth and a shadovar witch named Qalthus.

After decades of absence following the Spellplague, the Tower of Twilight began sporadically reappearing following the eruption.

Recovery

While most survivors fled the city immediately following its destruction, a few attempted to reclaim the ruins. These hardy and determined settlers who continued to eek out a life in Neverwinter did so amidst increasing attacks by undead as well as by the horrors from the Chasm. This soon led to the initial construction of a fortification known as The Wall out of the rubble and detritus to protect their Settlers' Enclave, as well as the formation of a militia known as the Neverwinter Guard. The first leader to emerge amidst these survivors was a man named Jelvus Grinch.

Neverwinter was a largely desperate and lawless place for several years. It was not until the, that Lord Dagult Neverember arrived in the city, declared himself its "Lord Protector", and brought in the mercenaries and resources needed to begin reclaiming and restoring Neverwinter in earnest.

As the city was slowly rebuilt, it became a patchwork of slums, new construction, and the broken remnants of the old city. The first district to be rebuilt was a southwestern portion that had been relatively lightly damaged by the eruption, and which came to be called the Protector's Enclave when it was completed in the . From there, Dagult Neverember's "New Neverwinter" movement was spearheaded. New buildings were erected atop the foundations of what had stood before, and by the, people had begun returning to the city to help rebuild and to open new businesses. The docks were also largely restored (at the cost of cutting down every tree on the nearby Upland Rise for lumber), allowing trade to resume. While progress was made, the city was still largely considered a ruin as of the, despite massive ongoing reconstruction efforts that resulted in a forest of scaffolding throughout the city.

It was not until the, that the Chasm was closed, trade along the High Road had resumed, and Neverwinter began to thrive once more. Even so, parts of Neverwinter remained abandoned, monster-infested, or in ruins through the 1490s DR, even in the western parts of the city. Castle Never was still undergoing repairs as late as the .

## Rust dragon

Rust dragons were planar dragons native to Acheron that had an insatiable hunger for metal.

Description
Physically, rust dragons resembled different metallic dragons from the Prime Material plane, such as silver, copper, and brass, except that their scales seemed to be covered by different forms of oxidation and their wings were thin with an iridescent character. Those differences were only in their appearance, as all rust dragons usually possessed a similar set of strengths and abilities. Moreover, rust dragons had somewhat insectoid traits, such as antennae and wings shaped in the resemblance of a butterfly's. Rust dragons' teeth were also unusual in the sense that they protruded from the creature's exoskeleton, instead of its skull.

The main sources of sustenance for rust dragons were ores and metals. They had a particular liking for adamantine, gold, mithral, iron, silver, and steel, but did not turn down other metals. They also enjoyed eating meat, particularly vermin, as palate cleansers between servings of different metals, in order to better appreciate their subtle differences in taste.

Personality
Unlike pyroclastic dragons or howling dragons, rust dragons were not exceedingly aggressive or violent. They mainly attacked out of hunger, and even so only to the point where they could secure sustenance. Despite being very focused on the task at hand and not tolerant of much meddling in their affairs, they were not above withdrawing from a hostile situation in which they had the disadvantage.

Another trait that set rust dragons aside from other dragons was that they did not have the habit of hoarding treasure, save for a handful of gems that some of the oldest rust dragons might want to carry.

Combat
Any metal object that came in touch with a rust dragon's skin or its bite immediately rusted away into uselessness. They were also incredibly resilient against damage from weapons made of metal. Only extremely powerful magic weapons had a better chance of withstanding this rusting effect, but no weapon was completely immune.

Rust dragons possessed two breath weapons: a line of acid and a cone of extremely corrosive reddish-brown liquid that immediately destroyed any metal items that it touched. Only objects with some sort of magical enhancement could stand a chance of surviving it. They could also create effects similar to the acid fog, repel metal or stone, and wall of iron spells.

Ecology

A minority of sages speculated that rust dragons might be related to rust monsters, but those claims were generally discredited. The extremely few rust monsters who survived to reach old age somehow transported themselves to Acheron, where they ravenously consumed the abundant metal from the cubes of the plane for about one year. Afterwards, they encased themselves in cocoons made of spun metal, where they stayed for three years. Following this period, they emerged from their cocoons as rust dragon wyrmlings.

It was not known, however, whether rust monsters were native to Acheron and only traveled to the Prime Material plane as a part of their larval cycle to become rust dragons, or if rust monsters were in fact native to the Prime and were altered by the magic of Acheron once transported there. In any case, it was speculated that rust dragons were largely responsible for the ongoing degradation of the oldest cubes of Acheron. The Doomguard in particular maintained that, if left unchecked, rust dragons were capable of destroying the entire plane within one millennium.

Society
Besides their home plane of Acheron, rust dragons could also be found in Banehold, Clangor, Dragon Eyrie, Hammergrim, and Nishrek. If found in the Material plane, they tended to build their lair in underground caverns near veins of metal, to the usual dismay of miners. They were usually found alone, but could also form families as they raised their young. Clutches of young rust dragons were also commonly found.

Sometimes the achaierai collected rust dragon cocoons and raised them as pets. Domesticated rust dragons were considerably more docile and tolerant of others than their wild counterparts.

## Rutterkin

Rutterkins (or rutterkin singular and plural) were a warped breed of deformed tanar'ri and among the weakest of demons. In a sense, they were true representatives of chaos, for the pathetic mutants were held in utter contempt by all others and completely unbound by masters, societies or causes.

Description
Rutterkins were  tall, disfigured demons that resembled hideous humans, and were considered ugly even by demonic standards. Their skin was mottled green and blue-violet, and if not completely hairless had only the occasional patch or two of uncomfortable-looking wiry bristles. They had backward-pointing ears on either side of their pointed, elongated skulls, and tiny, red eyes swollen with vicious hatred.

Every rutterkin was deformed in its own unique way, their misshapen limbs and bodies making them universally asymmetrical. Most were hunched and frequently howled in pain as their bodies continued to warp and contort at random. Sometimes (about 10% of the time), they'd be so badly mangled that an arm would no longer function or their already staggered or limping gait would get even worse.

Personality
Rutterkins were wild, undisciplined savages, crude and rough members of demonkind that respected only brute force and destroyed anything weaker than themselves with savage delight. The vicious creatures were known for being cruel and bullying whenever the opportunity presented itself. Whether they actually believed in the superiority they acclaimed over certain other demons or if they only did so due to an inferiority complex was unclear but irrelevant; they felt trapped in a life on ceaseless pain and weren't happy unless they were making someone else suffer.

Though they were feral and monstrous creatures, they weren't exactly stupid, most having average human intellect and relatively high cunning, and at least some of them being intelligent enough to understand concepts like treasure, value, and trade.

Abilities
As a whole, rutterkins were utterly incompetent, unwilling to attack with their natural armaments (claws or fists) since doing so caused them physical pain akin to punching with a sprained wrist. As far as demons went, they had few magical abilities; they were limited to the creation of small areas of darkness, flight, telekinesis and, once per day, teleportation.

They could also generate fear, either by touch or by emitting a wave of crippling terror when several of them grouped together, although their fear didn't cause others to run away, but stand rooted in place. Like other tanar'ri, they could summon more of their kind, but were limited to summoning other rutterkin, least demons like manes and dretches or, on rare occasions, chasmes.

Combat

Rutterkins lacked creativity in combat, rarely using the few abilities at their disposal to interesting effect. When they spotted enemies, they gathered together in an overwhelming mob and descended upon them. Though they had some sense of self-preservation, many felt the only recourse to dealing with their pain was destruction and would kill anything in their path or end their miserable lives trying.

Though only a few donned armor, they had a preference for wielding weapons, and they were well-known for the exotic, often masterwork, arms they employed. When forced, rutterkins wielded longswords or battleaxes, but even these weapons were strange for their type and rarely seen anywhere outside of grotesque, rutterkin hands.

Their serrated broadswords were known as flatchets and were especially effective against the unarmored and usually wielded alongside shields or other weapons. Their most frequently wielded weapons were the axes, similar to those of orc double axes but with crescent-shaped blades. Rather than a battleaxe however, many rutterkins wielded a snap-tong, a polearm ending in a sharp spike with one or more crescent-shaped pincers on the end that allowed the wielder to ensnare and continue to injure their enemies. For ranged fighting, they wielded about five tri-blades,  wide, three-bladed wedges flung from sling-like deviced called atlatls.

Society
Even by the standards of the chaotic evil "society" of the Abyss, the rutterkin were total outcasts, exiles among the deranged ranks of the tanar'ri. They were utterly despised by all demons, regarded as pitiful weaklings by and granted lower status than even the actually weaker dretches. So deeply did the rutterkins hate their position that they wandered the Abyss as loners, rampaging in areas of the Abyss unclaimed by more powerful demons.

There were only a few exceptions to this general rule. Chasmes that tried to carve out territories as minor lords took on rutterkin servants, but even then the fly demons only treated them as allies because they found rutterkins easy to dominate and rule over, and they otherwise ignored them. Armanites, despite their general hatred of least tanar'ri, infrequently took them on as servants, smithies, groomers, and auxiliary riders.

Even in matters of warfare, the rutterkins were treated as lesser beings. While many Abyssal armies used the rutterkin as basic foot soldiers, putting them under the bullying control of bulezau or armanite captains, they weren't even granted the "privilege" of being drafted into the Blood War to die in droves as cannon fodder, although this was debatable.

However, despite hating their kin, the rutterkin unwittingly ended up serving them as guards for their home plane against non-demons. They were constantly searching the Abyss looking for those that they could prey upon and viciously savaged any unwelcome intruders, and though usually far too weak to stop these beings, they typically tried to summon reinforcements, thus alerting other demons of the presence of trespassers.

When not gathered into a standing army, the rutterkins most often grouped together in gangs of at least three and at most twelve. By no means did they do this searching for compassion or to offer sympathy; they heckled, belittled and beat up each other as well. The rutterkins recognized they were near the bottom of the pecking order, and ganging up allowed them to return the favor for their abuse on beings stronger than themselves, such as lone vrocks or hezrous. They only restricted themselves to manes and dretches normally out of self-preservation, but any feeble or greatly wounded tanar'ri they believed couldn't hurt them back too badly was fair game to them.

Ecology
Rutterkins came into being when the foul, chaotic energies of the Abyss mutated other creatures. The process was similar to the effects of a disease known as warp touch, where raw chaos and dissolution caused the afflicted being to mutate with results ranging from useful or benign to dangerous or deadly. They were also known to spontaneously emerge from the corpses of dead manes.

Like those infected with a horrible disease, rutterkins could spread the plague of their cancerous existence by bite. Those that succumbed to this Warping Plague underwent an excruciating process as their bodies contorted, until they were mere abyssal wretches, bereft of their former identities and left to join the rest of their kind following the wake or rutterkin destruction.

Like manes and dretches, chaotic evil mortals occasionally reincarnated into rutterkins after dying in Thanatos, one of the layers of the Abyss.

Outside of some form of magical intervention, there was a way of rutterkins to claw their way out of their wretched state. By expressing their anguish through distinguishing brutality and a thirst for destruction that rose above the norm, rutterkins could tap into the chaotic essence of the Abyss and transform into a new, more powerful kind of demon.

Creation
Demon lords had rutterkins formed from raw manes in a process so unholy and disgusting that blindness could be considered a positive alternative, doing so with intent to create a slightly more powerful breed of soldier that could be easily kept under control. They also used it against powerful demons as a method of demotion in situations where killing the subjects could be considered merciful.

Sibriexes, obyriths that could channel Abyssal power to create demons from other creatures, could create entire armies of rutterkins within a few days, and nalfeshnees made them from the souls of the evil but incompetent and arrogant to teach them humility through humiliation. Another way of becoming a rutterkin was to start as a dretch and then rise above the others in the pack.

Regardless of how they got that way, becoming a rutterkin didn't stop being painful just because the transformation was complete, for indeed it never truly ended. Their corrupted forms continued to mutate without warning leaving them in constant, shrieking pain. Even if they managed to become a different kind of demon afterwards, one that became a rutterkin never truly recovered, and would resort to anything to keep their grip on power.

History
In 1370 DR, rutterkins along with other tanar'ri were found in the Dungeon of Death on its Habitat Level, where they were subservient to the adolescent nabassu Visceris.

In the 14 century DR, Vheod Runechild read of an ancient tower and wished to find out more. He gifted a rutterkin a hefty amount of gold and a magical cloak to determine the tower's location.

Rumors and Legends

Ancient stories told of a race of humans that, from the corners of the Prime Material Plane, experimented with planar travel and probability travel. On their journey throughout planes, they discovered the tanar'ri in the Abyss, who enslaved and abused the, to them, alien creatures, and turned them into the first rutterkin.

## Runestick

A runestick was a magic wand made by a druid to store a certain number of spells for later use.

Description
Runesticks were made of oak and measured around 12 inches (30 centimeters) in length. They had runes carved into their surfaces and were bound in mistletoe or holly.

Powers
A runestick served as a repository for a limited number of druidic spells that could then be cast directly from the wand itself, similar in general principle to a ring of spell storing. It could store a maximum of five spell levels, and only druids of sufficient power could fashion such an item. After carving the runestick, the druid would then cast the desired spells directly into it to be stored. The wand could then be used by any druid when the proper word of command was spoken. The power of the magic contained in the runestick was equal to that of the druid who originally created it.

Restrictions
The lifespan of a runestick was limited. A month after it was created, the wand deteriorated to inert dust whether the spells stored in it were used or not, and it could not be recharged before that time. The only non-druids capable of using the wand were maidens of "pure heart" (provided they knew the command word), but in such cases the magic was only half as effective.

## Ruby golem

Ruby golems were beautiful gemstone golems made from rubies.

Description
A typical ruby golem stood  tall and could easily weigh over . They resembled dark red glossy human statues, with some appearing such a deep red that they were nearly black. They were often crafted with intimidating appearances such as armor or the head of a dragon, gargoyle, lion, or other monster. Their surface was smooth and slick.

Combat
Ruby golems were formidable opponents in battle. The easiest way to combat them was to disrupt the circle of wizards that controlled them. Any disruption to the circle caused the ruby golem to wander aimlessly.

They struck with each of their fists during combat which, when combined with their considerable strength, caused significant damage.

Magical weapons were needed to strike ruby golems. They were resistant to many divine spell spheres and attacks from witches of Rashemen. They were completely immune to any heat or electric-based attacks but were more susceptible to sound-based attacks than other gemstone golems. They could also regenerate damaged parts of themselves surprisingly rapidly.

A rock to mud spell would immobilize a ruby golem for several minutes. The crystalbrittle spell allowed normal weapons to harm them and eliminated their immunity to heat and electricity. The shatter spell caused significant harm to a ruby golem. A ruby golem would be completely healed if a wizard used a mending spell on them.

A ruby golem was liable to interpret all orders to attack as orders to wrestle a foe into the ground. They were very likely to continue to attack downed foes long after those had fallen.

Society
Ruby golems were used by the Red Wizards of Thay. A circle of at least a dozen wizards, led by a superior, was needed to control them. As of, ruby golems were more common without such direct control; those golems were likely to ignore orders and start acting on their own initiative.

Creating a ruby golem did not require enough ruby to craft the entire construct, but rather a ruby of no less than ten thousand gold pieces which served as the golem's "heart." The rest of the body could be made of other materials, predominantly semi-precious stones, totaling at least fifty-five thousand gold pieces. The magic of the golem's creation would transform these stones into ruby.

Ecology
Ruby golems had the ability to slowly burrow underground, and favored this mode of movement even where walking was more efficient.

A ruby golem would collapse into a pile of rough cut rubies and powder when slain. The gems were quite valuable, and the powder could be used by wizards for spell components although it was otherwise worthless, with most of the golem's creation material having been destroyed. The golem's "heart" would also be left behind.

History
Like all gemstone golems, the secret to creating ruby golems was devised by practitioners of gem magic in ancient Mulhorand. The secret was rediscovered by the Red Wizard Aznar Thrul in 1368 DR, who sought to use the golems to conquer Rashemen. However, by 1372 DR he had lost control of his golems, who went rogue and spread out across the continent to pursue misremembered or gargled directives.

## Ruin elemental

Ruin elementals were fearsome elementals summoned by ruin chanters to protect their homes.

Description
Ruin elementals were massive earth elementals approximately  tall and  in weight. They were composed of rubble from the ruins they guarded. Over time, this rubble slowly eroded into smaller pieces, so that the oldest ruin elementals appeared to be composed of gravel.

## Ruin chanter

Ruin chanters were melancholy fey that lived in the ruins of cities.

Description
Ruin chanters resembled gaunt humanoids about  tall, with skin that could be tan to grey in color and silvery hair. Their eyes were all black, without any white or iris. Ruin chanters typically wore ragged clothing of earthen tones. Common articles of clothing included cloaks, hats, scarves, and goggles.

Personality
Ruin chanters were sorrowful creatures that cared little about anything outside of their home. They were hostile towards those who came to plunder the ruins they lived in.

Abilities
Ruin chanters had a number of innate magical abilities. They were immune to both poison and disease, but, like other fey, were vulnerable to cold iron. They could also fly despite not having wings.

Ruin chanters could curse people with infirmity of either the body or the mind. In both cases, the victim appeared aged: skin became wrinkled, hair turned white, and their posture became stooped. When the body was effected, the victim grew physically weak and in poor health. When the mind was effected, the victim grew senile and could not reliably tell friends from foes. These curses could not be placed on the same creature, and lasted an hour. A remove curse spell could dispel either curse.

Additionally, once per week a ruin chanter could summon a ruin elemental. The ritual took one hour, and for the duration of the ritual a storm manifested over the ruin chanter's home. The ruin elemental would serve the ruin chanter until it was destroyed. Most ruin chanters had multiple ruin elemental servants.

Combat
When forced into combat, ruin chanters preferred to send their ruin elemental servants into melee while using their own spell-like abilities. They would conceal the fact that they could fly by leaping, and would lure opponents onto unstable structures in this way.

Society
Ruin chanters were solitary fey that were personifications of worldly decay. They lived in ancient ruins and sang songs of past glories, or dirges of what was lost. They ate ash and dust, and drank whatever water was found in their home. Air full of dust or grit did not bother them, and they slept on beds of rubble.

Ruin chanters sometimes kept each other's company. They respected each other's territory, and a visiting ruin chanter would sing a song as they approached the home of another, who would then either sing a song of welcome or warning. When a male and female met, they would mate and produce children, who would in turn seek their own homes far from their parents.

Ruin chanters were known to tolerate other creature living in their ruined domains besides themselves, mainly constructs, undead, or creatures native to the area. Any beings devoted to restoration were also accepted. Any creature that entered one's domain for any reason other than restoration or mere subsistence was the fey's enemy.

History
In the late 1300s DR, factions of both the Red Wizards and the Zhentarim actively searched ruins in the deserts and tundra where ruin elementals were suspected to be found, as such creatures typically guarded powerful magical artifacts. Whether they knew that such entities were created by ruin chanters is unclear, but they were willing to pay handsome sums of money for more information about such ruins.

## Ruling rings of Yrix Alquinnar

The ruling rings of Yrix Alquinnar were a set of four double rings created by the preeminent elementalist of ancient Netheril, Yrix Alquinnar. Each ring was attuned to a different element, Air, Earth, Fire, or Water, and allowed the wearer unprecedented command and control over the various elementals from those planes.

Description
Each of the rings was actually two rings joined by two tiny, jewel-inlaid silver chains, intended to be worn on adjacent fingers. The rings themselves were also set with tiny gemstones around their circumference.
Ring of Air
Two platinum rings set with diamonds. The silver chains contained sapphire chips mounted in the center of the links.
Ring of Earth
Two copper rings set with onyx. Amber pieces adorned the links.
Ring of Fire
Two gold rings set with rubies. The chain links contained peridots.
Ring of Water
Two silver rings set with emeralds. Aquamarines decorated the chains.

Powers
All "common" rings of elemental command were based on the work done by Yrix Alquinnar, and thus each of the ruling rings had all the powers and properties of the corresponding lesser rings plus a much greater chance to charm elementals from the attuned plane, and more. In particular, the ruling rings did not make the wielder more vulnerable to attacks of a particular type. For example, a ring of fire elemental command bestowed many benefits, but made the wearer more susceptible to cold- and water-based attacks.

In order to gain full access to the power of any of the ruling rings, the wielder had to slay an elemental from the corresponding plane. Until this condition was met, the rings manifested only one aspect of their suite of powers:
Ring of Air
as a ring of invisibility
Ring of Earth
as a ring of feather falling
Ring of Fire
as a ring of fire resistance
Ring of Water
as a ring of water walking

Individually, each of the ruling rings was formidable versus elementals from the attuned plane, but two- and three-ring combinations gave the wearer extraordinary influence over the associated para-elemental creatures as well. Wearing rings of opposing elements (Air/Earth and Fire/Water) did not grant any power over para-elementals, but still increased the number of regular elementals that could be controlled.

Rings of Air and Fire
effective versus smoke para-elementals
Rings of Fire and Earth
effective versus magma para-elementals
Rings of Earth and Water
effective versus ooze para-elementals
Rings of Water and Air
effective versus ice para-elementals

Wearing all four rings, two on each hand, spanning the two pairs of fingers on each hand, gave the wielder absolute control over all elementals and para-elementals that could be charmed by the wielder.

History
Yrix Alquinnar died of natural causes sometime during Netheril's Golden Age (roughly  to ) and he bequeathed one of the ruling rings to each of his four apprentices—the sisters Leia, Natasa, Sarim, and Tasia Maarekh, nobles in their own right. These women eventually married and started families, with three of them passing their family treasure down the ages until the line faded or tragedy struck. In total, the four rings had over two hundred owners spanning well over two millennia.

Ring of Air: The only ruling ring to remain in familial custody throughout its history, this ring was in the possession of Lady Aldara Maerklos (née Marrek), matriarch of the noble House Maerklos in Waterdeep, as of 1372 DR. It was kept in the family vault and Lady Aldara did not deign to use it, but was preparing to reveal its existence and pass stewardship to her daughter Tehss. Aldara could only trace her family back to the 8 century DR, but Khelben "Blackstaff" Arunsun knew that the Marrek clan was formerly spelled Maarekh and she was a direct descendant of the four Maarekh sisters.

Ring of Earth: This ring was lost very early in its history. It was bequeathed to sister Sarim who later died at the hand of her greedy brother-in-law who coveted the ring. Her husband slew his brother in revenge and tossed the ring into the Sea of Swords where it passed into legend and tavern tales. The only hint that it may have been recovered was the unusual command over earth and stone by a wizard pasha from Calimport wearing a double ring that fit the description of one of the ruling rings in the . Unfortunately, he was slain by rivals in the, and the ring was not on his corpse when it was found.These dates were computed using 1370 DR as the base for the "current year" in the Powers & Pantheons publication.

Ring of Fire: Khelben himself won this ring off of a tiefling wizard in a spell duel while planeswalking in the . He kept it in Blackstaff Tower's magically protected treasure vault on a scarlet crushed-velvet pillow. Laeral Silverhand, the Simbul, and Elminster were supposedly the only other people who knew of its resting place. The last known use of the ring by one of these august personages was in the .

Ring of Water: The Simbul, also known as the Which-Queen of Aglarond, was the bearer of this ruling ring since she received it as a gift from the last descendant of Natasa Maarekh, a former lover, sometime in the 8 century DR. As of 1372 DR, it had remained exclusively in her possession except for a brief loan to her sister Laeral and Khelben for study.

Rumors & Legends
Yrix Alquinnar was a legendary figure among elementalists and he was described using the four rings simultaneously by cupping his hands, bringing the outer rings on each hand in contact and forming visible bands of radiating power. For those not familiar with the history of the rings or elementalism, it was a common misinterpretation that "ruling rings" meant some form of mental control over other people, enabling the wearer to bend others to their will.

Sometime around 1372 DR, Khelben began feeling surges of power emanating from the ring of fire. He discreetly inquired about Lady Aldara's ring and was  told that it was also behaving oddly. Since he knew the location of the ring of water, he speculated that the ring of earth was somewhere in the city of Waterdeep. He consulted with Lord Maskar Wands and the two archmages began a quiet search of the vicinity, hoping to acquire the item or at least prevent its misuse.

## Ruby dragon

Ruby dragons were a rare species of gem dragons who often were confused with red dragons, however, unlike their chromatic cousins, ruby dragons were creatures that followed laws and stayed away from moral extremes.

Description
Ruby dragons had fine ruby-red shimmering and glistening scales, indistinguishable from those of red dragons from a distance of  or further. Upon closer examination, their gem dragon nature became immediately apparent.

Abilities
Like other dragons, rubies' main weapon was their breath. Unlike their red kin, ruby dragons' breath was a wave of intense metal-melting magic heat that melted and ignited anything and anyone unlucky enough to be caught in the breath's way. The intensity of the heat increased as a ruby dragon aged. Items made of light materials, such as paper, were incinerated instantly, leather was destroyed within 6 seconds, non-metallic items were destroyed within 12 seconds, and metals melted after 18 seconds. The heat of a ruby dragon's breath was intense enough to melt even enchanted metallic items, however, it took them 24 seconds or more to be destroyed. Magic items with resistance to fire were just as vulnerable to this type of breath as other magic items, however it took twice as much time to become unusable. Even if affected magic items were quickly cooled, there was a chance parts of the enchantments were nullified.

With age, ruby dragons became completely immune to mundane and magical flames and heat. Their jeweled hides granted ruby dragons good protection and resistance to cold.

Ruby dragons were able to take on humanoid shapes, seemingly for an indefinite amount of time. Some, if not all ruby dragons, could use the Art innately. Some of the spells they could cast included shocking grasp, summon swarm, and produce flame.Chenowyn's abilities, as far as they are known, differ significantly from those described in the Monstrous Compendium Mystara Appendix. It is unclear if she is unusual for a ruby dragon, or if the ruby dragons of Toril differ from those of Mystara.

Personality
Older ruby dragons enjoyed bathing in molten rock of lava and strolling along scorching magma.

Combat
When forced into confrontation, ruby dragons used their deadly super-heated breath, as well as their raking claws and powerful crushing jaws.

Society
Ruby dragons could be found in solitary existence as well as in tightly-knit clans and inhabited the same terrains as their red dragon cousins. Ruby dragon were known to build joined conclaves with red dragon on the tallest secluded mountaintops. However, ruby dragons were more likely venture into higher altitudes than red dragons. Most ruby dragons spoke their own language, the shared tongue of all gem dragons, and the language of red dragons.

These voracious gem dragons consumed meat as well as gemstones. Because of that almost compulsive love for consuming the most dense and precious of gems, ruby dragons often attacked dwarves with glee, hoping to find delicious gems among their possessions. Ruby dragons valued gems above all else and happily accepted such gifts, bribes, and offerings.

Ruby dragons could cross-breed with amethyst dragons.

History
Sardior the Ruby Dragon, was believed to be the first of the ruby dragons. Some sages believed that Sardior hailed from the First World and upon his death, he shattered, giving birth to other gem dragons.

Notable
Chenowyn, a young female companion of queen Zaranda Star during the Reclamation Wars of Tethyr.

## Russet owlmonster

A russet owlmonster was an horrid hybrid of owlbear and rust monster created by Nestor Podgin of Monsters Made to Order in Skullport in the late 14 century DR.

Description
Roughly half rust monster, quarter bear, and quarter giant owl, the russet owlmonster was largely similar to the owlbear in build, size, and stance, yet had a rust monster's armored carapace about its shoulders, back, and head. Similarly, it combined the worst traits of owlbear and rust monster. With its claws and beak, it could slash, grab, hug, and bite its victims, and with its antennae it could rust away all metal items—such as weapons and armor—they might have defended themselves with it.

The owlmonster was highly unstable and aggressive, perhaps more so than even the owlbear. They resisted all attempts to train them and had to be charmed into compliance.

History
Nestor Podgin was a transmuter hailing from Waterdeep with a too-keen interest in researching all aspects of the creation of life, but particularly how hybrid monsters such as owlbears, chimerae, and perytons came to be. He theorized magic had been used to make them and identified common traits of aggression and unpredictability in them and in his own experiments with the spell Duhlark's animerge (which he knew as join animal). He refined the technique and became skilled enough to make a profitable business of it, by making monstrous creatures to order.

The russet owlmonster was his most successful creation, with three specimens produced by 1370 DR. What's more, Nestor expected one to bear young of its own before long. If so, and if the offspring also bred true, then he would have created a whole new species.

Rukanyrs were highly mobile, the many-legged monsters being faster than normal humans, able to effortlessly scale walls, and incredibly difficult to push or trip. They could rake at their enemies with their claws, deliver a potent poison through their bite which reduced the mobility of their foes, slam into their opponents so hard it left them temporarily stunned, and possessed a healing factor that allowed them to recover from simple injuries.

Most unique of rukanyr traits however was their relation to sound and sundering (and they were notably immune to sound-based effects themselves). Every few dozen seconds or so, rukanyrs could unleash devastating roars from one of their mouths that deafened most creatures within a  diameter, and brought sonic ruin to whatever they made the focus of the attack, breaking their armor and weapons in the process. Their armored plates shifted and rubbed against each other in combat, creating a jarring noise like trees rubbing together in strong winds, and non-blunt weapons were at risk of being caught between the plates, torn from the wielder's grasp and damaged if not totally crushed.

Combat
Rukanyrs universally began combat by unleashing their sundering roars on the best equipped enemy targets before wading into melee and lashing out with a barrage strikes.

Society
Despite the express purpose of their creation being destruction, the kaorti were a bit too successful with the rukanyr, as they were destructive to the point the kaorti forbid them from their own enclaves. Instead, they were used to lay waste to areas the kaorti were afraid to enter themselves. A concentration of rukanyrs (they were known to be organized into pairs or bands of 3-8) was almost always a sign that a kaorti enclave was nearby, as their ability to exist in Material Plane settings uninhibited made them ideal guardians for the regions around such a colony.

One underground sarrukh temple was known to have a trap which imprisoned whoever triggered it with a rukanyr, keeping both there with six walls of force until either the invaders or the rukanyr were dead.

Ecology
It was believed that kaorti wizards created the first rukanyrs by exposing monstrous scorpions to Far Realm energies.

## Rundorl Moonsklan

Rundorl Moonsklan, the would-be Harper King, was an agent of the Harpers whose profound ambitions and mediocre abilities somehow allowed him to nearly bring down the secret society of do-gooders, some years before the Era of Upheaval.

Personality
Rundorl dreamed of leading some shadowy cabal that would influence and shape the destinies of the cities of the North, making him the power behind every throne. He was hardly up to that task, however, repeatedly allowing himself to be manipulated and dominated by powers far beyond his ken.

History
Following the initial conflicts of the Harpstar Wars of the late 12 century DR, Rundorl came to believe that the Harpers' leaders—namely Elminster and Khelben Arunsun, along with other senior agents―were slain or otherwise retired from the Harper business. After all, he'd never met them, nor heard tell of someone who had communicated directly with them, in his brief career with his Harpers. He thus decided that a new leader for the organization was needed and, fittingly enough, he could fill that role.

In order to gain some influence and wield power within the organization, Rundorl needed an edge. By chance, he happened one day to meet the Thayan necromancer Szass Tam, who himself had similar goals. The two struck a deal to serve each of their interests— Szass Tam would funnel Rundorl information on his rivals in Thay and Rundorl would gain prestige by sending Harpers to win victories over them. To justify this focus on Thay, a land previously neglected by the Harpers, Rundorl spread the story of "the spell of Undeath",There is no known connection between this (apparently fake) spell and the beckoning death introduced in the film Honor Among Thieves, but it has a close similarity and connection to Szass Tam. It is possible Szass Tam gave Rundorl his real idea in order to discredit it later, or Rundorl inspired Szass to develop it for real. horrifying magic developed by Thay's Red Wizards that could transform the populations of entire cities into the undead. Armed with this knowledge, and having fabricated a story of how Thay planned to "conquer the world" with their magic, Rundorl rose in influence among the Harpers' leaders. They began a campaign against the spellcasting order, striking into the lands of Thay itself.

The Harpers' meddling into Red Wizards' affairs drew the group into a prolonged conflict with the Thayan spellcasters, leading to many deaths on either side. Although they did not affect any great change in Thay itself, the Harpers managed to slay many Red Wizards and even zulkirs. But despite their perceived victories, more Harpers were dying than Thayans and Rundorl led a reluctant retreat out of Thay. Worse, Red Wizards pursued Rundorl and his exhausted Harpers into the Dragonreach region and meticulously killed them one-by-one, before raising them as undead servitors. Only then did Rundorl realize the treachery of Szass Tam and the trap he had fallen into.

To make matters worse, these undead Harpers knew all the secrets and strongholds of the Harpers and targeted their still-living fellows. Even more desperate now and fearing for his own safety, Rundorl turned to the lich Thavverdasz, an ally of the Cult of the Dragon who dwelled in the Vast Swamp, so that he might deal with the undead for him. Thavverdasz agreed—then rapidly dominated Rundorl Moonsklan's mind, took control of the undead Harpers, and assumed the title of 'Harper King' for itself. Rundorl, meanwhile, joined the fate of his fellow Harpers, ending up as just another undead creature in Thavverdasz's legion.

## Rurik

Rurik of Winterglen was the Northlander captain of the Winterglen Claw, a band of human rebels and escaped slaves fighting against their fey oppressors within Sarifal in the late 15 century DR.

Description
Rurik was a short man with big hands. He had a black beard (that was graying by the late 15 century DR) with a gruesome scar that bisected his lips, making him distinctly ugly. He had steel teeth.

Personality

Rurik was brave, blunt, crass, and could be bitingly sarcastic. He was eager to jump into action, grew impatient with delays, and had difficulty controlling his temper. He despised the leShay and Llewyrr rulers of Sarifal, having known them to be arrogant, debaucherous, and treacherous slavers. He also despised the long-lived fey of Sarifal more generally, accusing them of having stolen the future and culture of his people on Gwynneth. For this reason, he reveled in killing fey and dreamt of the day that he would throw off the yoke of their oppression.

Possessions
In battle, he wore battered chainmail, a steel cap, and seaboots. He wielded an axe but was also skilled with swords, and he kept a blade concealed on his person. He rode a powerful and swift horse.

Relationships
He counted Lord Mindarion of Synnoria as an ally, believing that the elf was amenable to his desires to be free of the fey in ways that others of his kind were not.

He and Suka, a gnomish traitor to Sarifal, found that they had many similar feelings towards the rulers of their realm. Suka respected his conviction but not his intelligence.

He did not particularly trust overtures from King Derid Kendrick, believing the Ffolk king to be a tyrant and only preferable to the fey because he would die much sooner.

History
He was said to be from the Winterglen. As of the late 15 century DR, Captain Rurik had gained a level of infamy that made both High Lady Ordalf of Sarifal and the humans of Alaron aware of him. In the late 1470s DR, he attracted the notice of House Kendrick, who nominally were interested in acquiring his loyalty in exchange for arms and support in his rebellion. Lord Aldon Kendrick travelled to Gwynneth aboard the Sphinx to meet with him and ask for an oath of fealty to High King Derid Kendrick, although this mission was undermined by Aldon's rivals within the Kendrick family, who tipped off spies of High Lady Ordalf. Some of Derid's message managed to reach Rurik only after he and his men rescued one of the crew of the Sphinx, the gnome named Suka.

Soon after, Rurik travelled with his troops, their ally Lord Mindarion, Suka, and a fomorian princess named Marabaldia to a meeting at Harrowfast, a citadel above Synnoria, where an alliance had been proposed among Rurik's Winterglen Claw, Mindarion's Knights of Synnoria, and Marabaldia's fomorian tribes to march against the ruling leShay in Karador. Rurik and the fomorians were eager to march, but were unable to reconcile disagreements with the Llewyrr elves of Synnoria, notably Lord Askepel, who took over negotiations from Mindarion after the latter had to rest to recover from a battle with the leShay's drow servants. Whereas Rurik insisted on liberation from the leShay, the Llewyrr wished instead to install a different leShay, Lady Amaranth, to the throne.

During a pause in the faltering negotiations, as it had become clear to Rurik that his hope of marching on Karador was slipping away, he and Suka retired to a lookout point above Synnoria where they were attacked by a wild wyvern. Descending the mountain to escape, they accidentally trespassed into the forbidden valley of the elves, and were accosted by three guards led by the Marchlord Oemeril Talos–claere. The elves insisted that no deed or words could absolve them of the crime they had committed simply by being non-fey inside their woods, and immediately assumed them to be escaped slaves. Rurik pretended to capitulate to Talos–claere before killing him and another guard while Suka slew the remaining one. Returning to Harrowfast, Rurik now had cemented the end of any possible alliance, and immediately departed with his troops. He invited Suka to join him, but she refused. He wished her luck and told her to lay the blame for the killings all on him.

## Ruldarr's Pipes, Locks, Tobacco, and Fine Furniture

Ruldarr's Pipes, Locks, Tobacco, and Fine Furniture was a store in the settlement of Bargewright Inn in the Savage Frontier in the mid-to-late 14 century DR. It was run by Ruldarr.

Location
It stood in the upper part of Bargewright Inn, on the northern side of the road known as the Rise, between Shondrin's Packsack of Plenty and The Bargewright Inn.

Interior
Inside, the store was dimly illuminated but fragrant with the scents of different woods and types of tobacco. The roof was supported by a dozen internal pillars, while furniture crowded the floorspace. The cellar was stacked with coffins, chests, and tobacco bins.

As Ruldarr was a keen gardener, his plants took up much of the space in his shop, many of them hanging plants in large, levitating pots. They rose and moved at Ruldarr's direction, or simply drifted about on their own, with leaves and roots dangling in the air. These plants seemed to enjoy Ruldarr's wines as well, and if a guest poured their unwanted wine into one, they would find several thirsty potplants hovering around wanting some too.

Defenses
On one pillar was hung an old and well-worn scythe. According to local legend, Ruldarr could command it to animate and come to his defense, should anyone assault him whilst in the store.

Moreover, the pillars were rumored to include secret compartments and even traps. One was confirmed to included a spring-loaded dagger blade after a thief was found skewered by it one morning before 1366 DR. Similarly, a good number of the furniture pieces for sale had hidden drawers, though Ruldarr of course did not hide his valuables or traps in these.

Services
As the name suggested, the store had an eclectic selection of goods for sale. Firstly, there was tobacco, which sold at a steady rate for 2 cp to 1 sp for a pouch depending on the variety. Secondly, there were also pipes for smoking them.

Thirdly, there was also available for sale fine furniture, with beautiful and ornate pieces, some with secret drawers, strongchests, and even coffins (presumably new, not used).

Finally, the store sold padlocks, which were the primary trade. In addition, there was a case of so-called "found" keys that would supposedly unlock doors and chests in ruins around the North. Adventurers brought these mystery keys to Ruldarr and other adventurers purchased them, at 6 gp for a key, in hopes of someday, somehow finding a lock they fitted, and claiming whatever riches lay behind.

## Running of the Saurs

## Rubber tree

Rubber trees, sometimes called rubber plants, were a rare kind of tree found on Toril. They were particularly valuable among Toril's many types of trees.

Ecology
Rubber trees could be found on the Malatran Plateau, in Maztica, and in particular regions in the far south of Faerûn, notably in the jungles of Chult. They tended to share habitats with cork trees.

Rubber trees were also noted to exist in other crystal spheres, such as on Krynn and Oerth.

Uses
The trees were a source of rubber, a congealed product made from their sap. Rubber was harvested and traded by the likes of the Itzas of Far Payit in Maztica and rubber trees were cultivated for rubber by the tribes of Malatra.

Rubber tree sap (sometimes called "uncured rubber") could also serve as a material component in the spells Bowgentle's fleeting journey and erase  and was proposed to be useful for creating potions of acid resistance.

History
By the mid-to-late 13 century DR, heavy harvests of rubber trees had led their populations to dwindle. This led to expeditions delving into the Chultan jungles simply with the goal of finding more of them. Rubber trees were seen as one of Chult's many great treasures, and were prized as far and wide as Amn, Calimshan, Sembia, Tethyr, Thay, and Waterdeep.

Throughout the Amnian occupation of Port Nyanzaru in the 15 century DR, Chultan rubber trees and timbers were harvested and exported by Amnian trading companies.

## Rubber

Rubber was a substance derived from rubber trees.

Properties
Rubber was notable for its ability to bounce and to float. Depending on the treatment and production, it could be flexible or stiff. Other valuable properties associated with rubber included its toughness and its ability to stretch and deform before returning to its original shape. It could also provide effective insulation (and thus protection) against electricity and could accept magical enchantments.

By weight,  of soft rubber was  while hard rubber was .

The smell of burnt rubber was generally perceived as foul.

Applications
Games and Toys
In Faerûn, commonfolk in cities like Waterdeep had access to cheap rubber balls which were used to play jacks. Similar rubber balls were used to play boule, a casino game in which the ball was spun around a special numbered bowl and gamblers would bet on where it would land.

Also in Faerûn, rubber bath toys (including those shaped like ducks) were favored by the likes of Elminster and Mirt.

In Malatra, rubber was used exclusively to make balls for use in games and as children's toys.

In Maztica, rubber was most notably used to make the hard,  balls for use in the Maztican ball game, as well as to make the soles of sandals used in playing that game.

Apparel
Rubber was used to fashion masks and curvy corsets. At the Baths in Arabel, hedonistic revelries sometimes included pink rubber dragon costumes.

In Waterdeep, rubber boots were not unheard of as part of a miner's outfit.

Tools and Equipment
Bouncy rubber balls were considered a useful tool for finding and neutralizing common traps, and trap-wary adventurers might be advised to carry several of them. Such a ball was simply thrown down a dungeon corridor and allowed to bounce and roll around, triggering traps or at least allowing the thrower to listen to the ball for information about the path ahead.

Rubber was useful for enhancing training environments and obstacle courses. Mock pit traps might include nets of rubber cords to catch and bounce fallen trainees, while rubber paint-bomb darts could be fired by bow to non-lethally hit and mark a target. Such features were used in the Assassin's Run of Waterdeep.

In Ankhapur, the Vorbel wagon wheel was a major trade product, which sometimes featured a layer of rubber wrapped around the rim.

In Maztica, rubber strips were sometimes used to weave hammocks and the like.

In Ravens Bluff, rubber was used in the construction of slakers, specialized flasks designed to extinguish fires. These were used by the Red Ravens fire brigade as of the mid-to-late 14 century DR.

On spelljammers, rubber was sometimes used as a material for floor mats.

Plugs and Seals
Some vials made use of rubber stoppers. An assassin could store poison in such a vial and simply poke or stab a weapon through the rubber in order to apply the poison without having to open the whole vial.

Natural rubber might be mixed with gorgon blood to seal the interior of items like dead boxes in order to disrupt detection magic.

In Durpar, rubber was sometimes used to coat realistic prosthetics, such as those sold by Orviir Kalgalast.

In Zakhara, rubber plugs were sometimes used to stop drains in baths and pools.

Magic
Rubber served as a material component in several spells, including afterclap, Bowgentle's fleeting journey, deadly dance, erase, false face, insulation lightning shield, sacrosanct, tentacle of withering, and translocation shift.

Some versions of the wand of wonder could conjure hundreds of permanent rubber balls that rained down around the wielder.

Production
Rubber was produced using the congealed snd cured sap of the rubber tree. This sap was melted and molded into usable shapes or sculpted into objects.

Rubber was known and used in Faerûn since at least the time of ancient Netheril. As of the mid-to-late 14 century DR, rubber was harvested and traded by the likes of the Itzas of Far Payit in Maztica and was cultivated as an agricultural product by the tribes of Malatra. In Faerûn, rubber trees were highly sought after, with expeditions searching for them in places like Chult. Rubber was also imported to Faerûn from Maztica.

## Rune Hammer

The Rune Hammer was a variety of enchanted warhammers created on the Sword Coast North in the late 14 century DR, with the original and the most powerful variety bing an artifact of the Broodhull Clan of the Vilhon Reach.

Description
This hammer appeared to be etched with glittering silver runes.

Powers
There existed three variants of the Rune Hammers, each possessed a +1, +2, and +8 enchantments what made the bludgeons deadlier and guided the wielder's hand towards the target. All three versions were ensorcelled to offer the wielder spell resistance of various strengths.

A Rune Hammer could be created from an enchanted warhammer and a slab of adamantite.

History
The artifact known as the Rune Hammer was a product of the dwarvish Broodhull Clan that ruled over the mountains of the Vilhon Reach region in Central Faerûn for centuries until its mysterious disappearance. Within a single year, all two hundred members of Clan Broodhull were gone, leaving behind only empty halls. One artifact that the Broodhull Clan left behind was a glittering, rune-carved, mighty war hammer.

More common versions of the Rune Hammer with a simpler +1 enchantment were forged by Master Blacksmith Marrok of Shining Knight Arms and Armor shop of Neverwinter to help the heroes who sought the cure from the dreaded Wailing Death of the . Later the same year, more powerful versions were created by Master Blacksmith Barun of Beorunna's Well to help the forces of Neverwinter stand against Lady Aribeth the Betrayer, who led a marching army of Luskan in the deadly but short-lived Neverwinter-Luskan war.

## Ruined keep (King's Forest)

The ruined keep was a former Purple Dragons outpost in the King's Forest of Cormyr, destroyed during the Sschindylryn invasion of Eveningstar.

Description
Post-destruction the keep was little more than a foundation, a few partial stone walls, and ballistae in disrepair.

History

The keep was originally built by Cormyr to control the magic that ran through that location. The drow fell upon the keep early in their surface invasion, destroying it and then moving on without realizing its worth. It was then taken over by a group of bandits that included many werewolves, who used it as a base while performing their raids. In response to this, the War Wizard Arganon Pine took a group of Purple Dragons and seized the ruins once more, along with adventurers sourced by Yenward Chorster. They protected Arganon while he performed a ritual to create a powerful ward over the area to keep away enemies. The counterattack was fierce, with bandits joined by werewolves after the sun set, including Claive the Carver and Bladeback. The defenders were able to hold them off and even repaired the fort's old ballistae for the defense, letting Arganon finish the ritual. Arganon then left to further investigate these werewolf bandits on the High Road.

Zenyff Bravehand took over from Arganon, but then had to deal with malevolent druids emerging out of the forest, who sought to take the magic for themselves and use it to control the surrounding area. Zenyff attempted another ritual that aimed to protect the area from their nature-based magics, just in time for another battle to take place. Facing off against the forces of nature—wolves, winter wolves, bears, mudmen, water elementals, and vine horrors led by a druid shapeshifted as a dire bear and the wood woad Gordy—the forces of Cormyr was successful, safeguarding the ruined keep and the magic within.These druids may have been the same as those corrupted by Halsaime from the Druid's Deep, but this is not stated.

## Ruardh Lightshiver

Ruardh Lightshiver was an elven Laranlor of Ardeep, and one of the founders of the Fallen Kingdom of Phalorm.

Relationships
Together with two other kings, Torghathar and Javilarhh Snowsword, Ruardh was one of the three kings of Phalorm. They had two dukes under their command.

History
Laranlor Lightshiver was one of the founders at the famous Council of Axe and Arrow that occurred in The Laughing Hollow, together with Torghatar blood of Bharaun and Javilarhh Snowsword. On the, this alliance with the shield dwarves of Dardath was forged; the combined region became Phalorm, the Realm of Three Crowns, in the .

Around the, his forces defeated the armies of Najara at the Battle of Blunted Fangs by assassinating the yuan-ti infiltrators sent south to the Reaching Woods by King Nejizar, dealing a defeat that forced these serpentfolk to retreat to the Serpent Hills.

He perished in battle in that same year, as Phalorm fought back an army of hobgoblins that devastated the dwarven duchy of Hunnabar near Dragonspear Castle.

Legacy
The words of Ruardh Lightshiver during the founding were preserved in an enspelled king's tear gemstone kept in the Vault of Sages in Silverymoon.

## Ruined Outpost (High Road)

The Ruined Outpost was a former fortification of the Purple Dragons of Cormyr.

Geography
The outpost was located on the north side of the High Road as it entered the foothills of the Storm Horns west of Tyrluk, placed to overlook the road as the land grew rougher and harder to travel.

History
By the mid-1480s DR, the outpost was long abandoned and fallen to ruin, being little more than crumbled stone and rotted wood. During the Sschindylryn invasion of Eveningstar, the withdrawal of the Purple Dragons from the High Road allowed the enemies of Cormyr to use the outpost as a base of operations in plain sight, including the bandits of Theodas Riveron and the Shadovar of Thultanthar who sought the Abyssal Planus Mechanus Nether Scroll.

## Runt

Runt was a commoner child who lived in the Lower City of Baldur's Gate in the late 15 century DR.

Description
Runt was a young human boy with a light skin tone. He had brown eyes and dark hair, hidden underneath a tight green cloth skullcap. His fanciful garb suggested Runt was from a family that had coin.

History
In the, Runt could be found in the Lower City with his two friends, Stinker and Spool, often playing "chase the gelatinous cube" in the alleys or loitering in front of Devil's Fee shop, daring eachother to go into the "devil's house" and accusing eachother of being scaredy-cats.
