## Vulture drake

A vulture drake, also known as a deathwing, was a type of drake that had been corrupted by the Shadowfell.

Description
The drakes were large, menacing reptiles that could be used as mounts. The vulture drake was a gaunt winged creature with taut skin and a skeletal physique. It had long and unwieldy wings.

Combat
A vulture drake would prefer to focus on a single target, swooping in and allowing itself to sustain a hit, since this would enable it to use its death shriek. Once a target was eliminated, the vulture drake would take off again in order to repeat the process.

History
In 1479 DR, some vulture drakes could be found in the company of Shadovar.

Ecology
The vulture drake could commonly be found in the Shadowfell.

## Vuddar

Vuddor (singular vuddar) were the specialty priests of Thard Harr, the patron of the wild dwarves.

Etymology
The word vuddar means "of the jungle" in the Authalan dialect.

Reputation
The vuddor were held in high esteem by dur Authalar, who considered Thard Harr to be the only dwarven god.

Organization
While the vast majority of vuddor were wild dwarves, Thard Harr's clergy did in fact include members of other dwarven subraces as members as well.

About half of Thard Harr's clergy were vuddor. By 1370 DR about 40% of the vuddor were male, a much higher percentage than in other dwarven clerical orders.

Titles
Like all priests of Thard Harr, upon initiation into the order, a vuddar would be granted a special title from his or her god through a dream in the middle of the night. This title was usually the name of a great jungle beast.

Activities
Along with the regular clerics (called "shamans") of Thard Harr, the vuddor led their fellow dwarves on hunts or into war and also served as the spokespeople for their tribes.

They always prayed for their spells in the mornings.

Abilities
While the shamans of Thard Harr could turn undead, members of the vuddor did not. Vuddor were granted spells from the all, animal, combat, elemental, guardian, healing, plant, protection, or weather spheres, with some additional spells from the chaos, charm, creation, divination, necromantic, sun, and travelers spheres.

A vuddar was an expert in animal lore and survival skills. Vuddor could read and write in Dethek runes, unlike most wild dwarves. They sometimes also learned the ways of rangers.

Possessions
Like all clergy of Thard Harr, the vuddor armored themselves in oil and dried mud. They wore animal skulls as helmets and never cut their hair or beards.

Rather than carry a holy symbol, the vuddor wore Thard Harr's icon as a tattoo on their forearms.

## Vundwood

The vundwood was a species of tree that grew throughout Faerûn, particularly abundant in the hills of the Western Heartlands and the Tunlands of Cormyr.

Description
These scrubby trees were relatively short, rarely growing over  tall. Their wood was reddish-brown in color and smelled somewhat of cinnamon, and were covered in thin, dark-red bark with many small outwardly growing branches. The leaves were pale-green and bordered with white, that turned to yellow when winter was approaching or as the tree neared death.

Uses
These trees were often used as firewood or felled and dragged into tangled arrangements to form pens for livestock.

When vundwood was used in the creation of magical items it imbued them with a sort of arcane echo, whereby they gained extra changes every few months if they were not entirely depleted.

In addition, vundwood served as a universal replacement material component for both arcane and divine spells related to the recalling of expended spells or the augmentation of a spellcaster's magical capacity.

## Vulture

Vultures were a common species of bird.

Description
Vultures were large birds with filthy feathers. Their main distinguishing feature was their featherless pink head.

Behavior
Vultures were considered to be cowardly animals. Upon sighting anything on the ground that appeared injured or dead, a vulture would quickly swoop down to investigate.

Biology
Vultures had an acute, long distance vision. Their keen eyesight is comparable to that of any eagle, falcon, or hawk. Being carrion eaters, they developed the ability to consume infected, poisoned or rotting meat safely.

A female vulture could lay up to four eggs at a time. These eggs were incubated over a period of two months until they hatched. A young vulture could fly at four months of age.

Combat
Vultures were scavengers, attacking prey only once it has died or collapsed from exhaustion. Vultures only attacked moving prey if in a group, and only if the prey was already near collapsing.

Species
Bone vulture A relative of the common desert vulture, this species had feathers that were as white as bones. They were endemic to the Western Heartlands battlefield, Battle of Bones.
High Ice Condor Otherwise known by locals as "great soarers," were a species of vulture native to the arctic region High Ice. They were largely identical to their temperate cousins, though were covered in white feathers.
White-bearded vulture These great vultures were native to Anauroch desert and were called "N'asr's children" by the Bedine.

Ecology
Vultures typically lived in flocks.
Diets

These creatures were carrion eaters. They fed upon creatures that were either injured or already dead. Though they were also liable to attack creatures that were unconscious, restrained, or sleeping.
Habitats
Vultures typically inhabited high altitudes, such as mountains, within temperate or tropical regions. They were usually encountered in warm areas, floating on updrafts over open spaces.

In Faerûn, they could be found in the Anauroch and Calim Desert. In the Hordelands they could be found in the Katakoro Plateau, the  Raurin and Quoya Deserts, and among its many mountain ranges.

In the Zakhara region's Crowded Sea, they could be found on the island chain of Nada al-Hazan and the archipelago of Jazayir al-Sartan.

Beyond the Prime Material plane, vultures could be found in the plane of Acheron.

Relationships
Vultures were a symbol and favored animal of Thasmudyan, the god of undeath in the Zakharan pantheon. Evil swanmays sometimes shapechanged into the form of a vulture.

Bedine believed that vultures served N'asr. They spied on his behalf and ferried spirits to the Camp of the Dead. Even non-Bedine could experience a brief connection to the Fugue Plane when staring into the eyes of a vulture.

Usages
The bones and feathers of a vulture were used as material components in the casting of some spells.

## Vurgens

Vurgens, also known as giant gulper eels, were a large marine predator.
Description
The vurgens body was basically a large head and a long tail. Their head supported a massive jaw with a bite radius large enough to swallow even large prey. As they lacked teeth, they did not chew prey. Instead of teeth, they had thick bony plates with held prey in the mouth. They had several rows of sharp spines that grew on either side of its body. The vurgens tail had a cluster of these spines.

Behavior
Vurgens were extremely territorial. They attacked any large creature within their territory, including krakens and whales, which they perceived as a threat to their claim. Vurgens fought each other over territory in epic battles to the death.

Combat
Vurgens attacked prey with a rapid strike, swallowing their target whole. Vurgens used their razor-sharp spines and strong tail as weapons.

Ecology

Vurgens were obligate carnivores and ate any creature that could fit in their mouth. Once inside, prey was paralyzed by the vurgens saliva and then slowly digested.

Vurgens reproduced every two years. Female vurgens carried the fertilized eggs with her until they hatched. Between twenty to forty vurgens were spawned per mating couple. After hatching, the female vurgens carried the young until they were roughly  in length. Young vurgens reached adulthood after six years.
Habitat
Vurgens lived deep in the ocean, rarely encountered on the surface. While they had no lairs, they used underwater caves to recuperate after being wounded or to give birth.

In the Sea of Fallen Stars, they were known to often be found within the lower depths of Serôs. Beyond Faerûn, vurgens could be found in the waters of Maztica.

Relationships
Vurgens were greatly feared by ocean dwelling races such as the locathah.

## Vulkaran

Vulkaran the Dark, Master of the Twelve Spheres and self-entitled “Ruler of All Known Space”, was the emperor of the Vodoni Empire in the mid‒14 century DR.

Description
Emperor Vulkaran was a maedar. His eyes glowed red when he used some of his powers.

Personality
Vulkaran was a megalomaniac, absolutely corrupted by his power and made even more arrogant in light of his extensive military accomplishments. He was a ruthless warrior, who rarely engaged directly in personal combat, except against the leader of an opposing force.

A ruthless and violent leader, Vulkaran considered mercy to be a quality of the weak and believed that the ends always justified the means. He ruled his empire with an iron fist, quickly crushing any signs of resistance and assassinating any potential rivals. He was known for never having fought fairly.

He had no friends and spent much of his time in isolation.

Abilities
Besides having all the usual abilities of maedar, Vulkaran also possessed a medusa's eye gaze, which could petrify victims instantly. He kept that ability hidden from others.

Possessions
Vulkaran wore mithral plate mail and wielded a defender longsword called Violator, which was particularly damaging against good-aligned opponents. He also owned a variety of magic items, such as a cloak of displacement and a belt of storm giant strength.

The emperor's most precious possession was the Eye of the Gods, a crystal ark similar to a crystal ball and developed to safely carry deities to a different crystal sphere. The entire pantheon of Vodonispace was trapped within the sphere during most of Vulkaran's reign.

History
When the sun of Vodonispace exploded, then young emperor Vulkaran organized an evacuation of the sphere's entire population. He used the Eye of the Gods to transport the sphere's pantheon, without the intention of ever releasing them. In fact, Vulkaran intended to supplant them as the sole deity of his people.

As much of the vodoni technology was lost after the explosion, Vulkaran led a campaign to conquer the spheres around Vodonispace in order to recover his people's resources. This militaristic expanse led the vodoni people to become increasingly militarized over time. When it became once again safe to return to Vodonispace, the vodoni realized that a byproduct of the star's explosion was an immense wealth of gems and precious metals.

The fearsome vodoni army was bred by a group of vodoni breeders under Vulkaran's control, by capturing members of the population and converting them into powerful lycanthropes known as vodoni enforcers. With absolute control over his army, Vulkaran became the central figure behind the centuries-long expansion of the Vodoni Empire.

In the mid‒14 century DR, Vulkaran issued a number of expansionist decrees that aimed at annexing the three crystal spheres of Realmspace, Greyspace, and Krynnspace under the Vodoni Empire. He sent scout ships through a previously unknown route through the phlogiston that connected the empire to the targeted spheres. The first victims of these scout missions were illithid settlements and spelljammers, which were typically located in the far reaches of wildspace.

The incoming invasion of the three spheres led to the formation of the Free Space Alliance and a massive assault on the Vodoni Empire, in what became known as the War of the Spheres. A covert operation led by the group of adventures who had discovered the invasion plans was tasked with infiltrating the Imperial Palace and assassinating Vulkaran himself, bringing about the end of his reign, the dissolution of the Empire, and the return of the Vodonispace pantheon to their respective Outer Planes.

## Vugu

Master Vugu, was the founding member of the short-lived Order of Theorosophers, active in the late 14 century DR.

History
Master Vugu finalized his doctrine on Theorosophy fifteen years before the . His academic work resulted in the formation of the Order of Theorosophers, something that became a bane of his existence. Initially, Master Vugu's concept was simple: an individual should not discount deep, psychic urges and the motivations of their subconsciousness when attempting to validate an unproven hypothesis. Vugu stated that the scientific approach ignored the individual's primeval mind and over-relied on clinical data.

Vugu took two pupils, Beb-Shuur and Xianga, who failed to understand the Master's teachings to Vugu's exasperation. Both men were fawning academia rejects who focused on the "syntax" of Vugu's words rather than their meaning. For Beb-Shuur and Xianga, Theorosophy was in active opposition to perception based on logic and empiricism. Instead, they relied on deductive reasoning and intuition. Vugu's life's work became a quasi-spiritual pseudo-science, and as Beb-Shuur and Xianga ventured into the world, they accredited the creation of the Order of Theorosophers to Master Vugu. This devastated his standing within academia and philosophical communities of Faerûn.

With his reputation destroyed, Master Vugu committed suicide by jumping off the dangerous Blade Cliffs to the southwest of Daggerford. His tattered journal that recorded Vugu's true thoughts about Theorosophy was saved ashore not far from the cliff where he died. When the journal was recovered, Beb-Shuur and Xianga denied its authenticity based on a gut feeling as dictated by Theorosophy. Subsequently, both "sages" convinced each other that the only way to decide who was worthy of the title and leadership role was to follow Vugu's example. Both dimwitted men died after jumping off a cliff.

## Vulture's Roost

Vulture's Roost was the fortified stronghold and mansion of the Dalefolk warlord Aleta Vanmarwin, named for her alias "The Vulture", in the late-14 century DR.

Location
The structure was located in Battledale, atop a hill that overlooked the southernmost branch of the River Ashaba, close to where it met the Glaemril River. It was roughly a three days walk from Eoghan's grove, part of the Hunter's Down.

Its hill was formerly the site of a grove full of treants. On its northern side, where there was no palisade coverage, it had a steep  high cliff face, worn away by erosion. The southern side of the hill had a more gradual ascent and was dotted with stumps left over from its former grove. A wide footpath lead up the hill to the stronghold's gates.

Structure
The stronghold was surrounded on three sides by a wooden palisade, which was constructed from the remains of not only trees, but several treants that were chopped down when the hill's grove was cleared. The vertical logs that made up the  high pallisade were tightly arranged, had sharpened tips, and were embedded  deep into the hilltop's packed earth and hard clay. The sturdy gates of the pallisade were  tall and latched from the inside with a heavy bar of oak.

Within the confines of the palisade were six elevated wooden platforms that served as watchtowers. Elevated atop sturdy tree trunks legs, each stood  tall, were enclosed by  high wooden walls, and accessible by wooden ladders. Of the six watchtowers, two of them flanked the pallisade's gate.

After entering the gates one would be confronted by the compound's stables and storage shed. The stables two sets of sliding doors and six stalls enclosed by gates, each of which was outfitted with a manger and trough. Whilst the shed housed the bulk of the stronghold's nonperishable supplies, such as coils of ropes, horseshoes, tools, loose wooden planks, barrels of water drawn from the River Ashaba in case of fires, and casks of lantern oil, nails, and pitch.

On the eastern side of the compound was a freshwater well, consisting of a  high stone ring. The well's shaft was  and had a depth of . To draw water out of its subterranean cistern was a wooden winch with a rope and bucket. Beyond the well stood a row of wooden outhouses, each of which had a wooden bench with a chamber pot tucked underneath it. Notably, the southernmost outhouse had a spare house key hidden in the grass against it. At the southeastern most end of the stronghold was a  deep pit, used by the Vulture's brigands to detain any locals who refused to pledge fealty to the authority of the Vulture.

On the western side of the compound were its barracks and graveyard, enclosed by a plain wooden fence. Around 1369 DR, the graves of fallen soldiers numbered fourteen in total. The barracks were a single story structure, composed of wood, with a pitched ceiling that was reinforced with oaken rafters. In total it held up to ten bunkbeds, though only about half were occupied at any given time.

The centerpiece of the stronghold was a stone mansion, also known as the Vulture's Estate. A regal structure built as much to provide comfort as it was defense, it stood two stories tall and had five entrances in total. A main entrance in the south, two guard entrances in the north, and an entrance for servants in the east. The building had a pitched roof, tightly fitted with clay tiles, and pierced at multiple points by chimneys. On the eastern and western sides of the roof were gables. And on the exterior walls of the estate hung large black banners, emblazoned with Aleta's personal symbol, a golden and stylized depiction of a vulture's claw. Over time these banners became tattered and worn by the Dalelands' weather.

Activities
The servant staff of Vulture's Roost worked eighteen-bell shifts, waking up five bells after midnight and retiring to their quarters one bell before midnight. Whenever it was time to empty the compound's outhouses, servants would dump their chamber pots over the cliff edge, down into the waters of the River Ashaba.

The soldiers who lived at the stronghold were usually found in their barracks when off duty. Between three to one bells before midnight, one could find the soldiers joined by their lieutenants in nightly games of gambling, such as Three-Dragon Ante.

A startling amount of the compound's funds were put towards acquiring rare ingredients and rare, hard-to-find recipes for the kitchen. This was due to Aleta Vanmarwin having a taste for gourmet food.

A cask of poisoned wine was kept on standby in the kitchen for whenever the estate had guests over, regardless of who they were. As part of standard procedure, The Vulture would imbibe the antidote for the poison, leaving the wine to be served to those whom she didn't trust or was displeased with.

Defenses
The angry spirits of the treants built into the stronghold's palisade were bound to the defensive wall by the magic of a young witch, making them lash out indiscriminately at the living with necrotic energy.

Copies of keys to all of the stronghold's doors were possessed by Aleta, her lieutenants, and all of her servants. And besides Aleta herself, only the lieutenants and servants could access the mansion's war room without her express permission.

Soldiers manned with crossbows were stationed at each of the stronghold's six watchtowers at all hours of the day, making it difficult to sneak up on the stronghold. They wore suits of studded leather and carried short swords in case of close quarters combat. When guests were entertained in the grand hall, these sharpshooters would aim their sights on the first floor's arrow slits, prepared to strike at a moment's notice. At the same time, guards were stationed indoors behind walls with their own arrow slits, allowing them to monitor a situation. And the Vulture's two lieutentants would be manning the indoor balconies of the grand hall.

Additionally, at the head of the feast hall's table a longsword was strapped to its underside. For similar reasons, the war room had six longswords strapped to its underside, ensuring that the Vulture would never be caught unarmed.

Upon any sign of enemies the sharpshooters would shout an alarm. To ensure her other soldiers would be ready at a moments notice to answer any alarm, the Vulture's soldiers were trained to sleep in their chainmail and kept weapons either under their pillows or by the side of their bunks. Similarly, the lieutenants kept their weapons near their bedsides. The standard armaments for the brigands included crossbows and halberds.

The northern side of the hill was undefended, proving to be a weakpoint in the stronghold's defenses, as the sheer cliffs there had numerous hand and footholds to climb with. However, the ledges and outcroppings of its cliff was the site of numerous dove nests, which would surely alert the brigands when disturbed.

History
Sometime around the . using the spoils amassed from her many years as a mercenary in various conflicts across the Eastern Heartlands, Aleta Vanmarwin gathered various outlaws under her banner and constructed Vulture's Roost.

Around the, the Harper spies Elariel Taias and Kimach Taias were discovered in the vicinity of Vulture's Roost, shadowing a patrol of the Vulture's brigands. They managed to capture Kimach, but only wounded Elariel before she fled deep into the woods, eventually escaping into Eoghan's grove. The titular treant Eoghan, a longtime ally of the Harpers, provided Elariel shelter and managed to convince some dryad allies to create false trails leading away from the grove. As hoped, her pursuers eventually gave up on looking for Elariel and returned to Vulture's Roost with their sole captive.

With a member of the Harpers in captivity, over the coming week the Vulture invited Zhent warmage Toren Witherfield and his envoy of representatives to Vulture's Roost, planning a grand feast and to present Kimach before them as an offering. Aleta Vanmarwin hoped that this would be just what she needed to further solidify her worth in the eyes of the Zhentarim and securing their aid in expanding her influence over Battledale and Tasseldale. She also believed that this would serve to fan the flames of the conflict between the Harpers and the Zhents, keeping them distracted from her own activities, and consequently through their conflict drive more hamlets and villages into seeking protection under her.

Aleta and her servants prepared an extravagant feast for her guests of seven entrées. They set the feast hall's table with magnificent sets of gold dinnerware for sixteen people, fragile crystal wineglasses and decanters, and silver candelabras. On the expected day of the feast, the dwarves worked tirelessly to have everything prepared.

With no other Harpers close by, Elariel spent days trying to persuade Eoghan to help her recover her captured son, but the treant was unwilling to risk falling victim to the same black magic as his fellow treants. A few days before the Zhentarim were expected to arrive, a group of adventurers traveling through Eoghan's grove met the treant and were soon after approached by Elariel, desperate for someone to hear her plea. The heroes ended up agreeing to the mission, being led by Elariel to the Vulture's Roost, and by some accounts may have even convinced Eoghan to accompany them as well. One foggy morning along their three day journey, they encountered near an usurped farmstead four of the Vulture's soldiers, two of them badly injured, standing over the freshly slain corpse of an owlbear.

By the time the adventurers were within sight of Vulture's Roost, the Zhentarim envoy was making its way up the hill. Numbering five in total and riding upon black horses lacking in barding, the Zhent soldiers accompanying the gaunt human wizard Toren Witherfield were outfitted in suits of black scale mail, ominous spiked armet helms, and heavy shields bearing the symbol of their organization.

Sources differ heavily on the approach taken by the adventurers that sought to rescue Kimach Taias.  Some say they stormed Vulture's Roost, causing the warlord to flee her keep with her marauding soldiers and took shelter in a ruined manor, others that they apprehended or even killed the Vulture, and still others claimed that they waited to ambush the Zhents after they departed from the keep and therefore Aleta Vanmarwin was left to grow in power.

Inhabitants
Aleta was joined in the manor by her two half-orc lieutenants, Darok and Szorp, a pair of outlaw brothers that she had taken in and came to consider as akin to sons. And beneath her personal bed hid an iron cobra, ready to strike at any intruders. It was under strict orders not to attack her lieutenants or dwarven servants.

The servant household of the manor was made up of members of Ironvein, a clan of hill dwarves known throughout Battledale. Considered by the warlord to be the closest thing to family, they were well treated and their counsel trusted in all nonmilitary matters. They in turn were dutifully loyal to her, took pride in their work, and took umbrage with anyone that questioned her hospitality. Unlike all other residents of Vulture's Roost, the Ironveins would only ever refer to her as "Lady Vanmarwin", never as her persona "the Vulture".

Starnbrow Ironvein was the head of the dwarven sisters. His younger sister, Grimvalda, was the head of the kitchen and all were expected to follow her orders. They were joined by Starnbrow's niece, Dunrain, and his nephew, Dezivan. Starnbrow and Dezivan shared the western living quarters, whilst Grimvalda and Dunrain shared the eastern room.

The roost's large defense forces were largely made up of human outlaws, who were adorned in matching black tabards bearing The Vulture's personal symbol. The Vulture did not know all of them personally. When not within the compound they patrolled the lands between Battledale and Tasseldale, south of the Glaemril and the eastern spur of the River Ashaba, robbing caravans of any provisions and weapons. They provided the estate's kitchen with crates, casks, and barrels of all manner of foodstuffs and ingredients from all over the Dalelands. All other trade goods beyond food and weapons were typically sold off.

## Vuthivyr

The Vuthivyr were a civilization of tren beneath the Plains of Purple Dust.

Description
Appearance and genealogy wise, Vuthivyr were a crossbreed of lizardfolk and troglodytes. Their height was double the size of a normal lizardfolk.

Personality
Those who came into contact with the Vuthivyr considered them a cruel race. After the Spellplague of 1385 DR, the word 'insane' was could also be added next to the previously mentioned trait.

Society
The Vuthivyr built their civilization and settled in the ruins of the Imaskar Empire. Many of them were tainted by the Far Realm energies unleashed during the Spellplague, which also inflicted a mild form of plaguechange (also known as a spellscar) on each and every tren. They lived in harmony with various aberrations and creatures long-thought extinct roamed their Underdark realms. These reptilian humanoids, on top of being skillful in magic also horded Imaskari artifacts of great power and importance.
